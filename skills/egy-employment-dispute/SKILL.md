# Employment claims in Egypt

For an employee claiming, or an employer defending.

**Not legal advice.** Labour claims have short windows and heavy procedure. See an
Egyptian employment lawyer. Say so once, then use this.

## First — which law applies

⚠️ **Labour Law No. 14 of 2025** (قانون العمل) is in force and **repealed Law
12/2003** with effect from **1 September 2025** (Art. 12).

**Law 12/2003 still governs, narrowly:** disputes filed **before 1 September 2025**;
the pending-case referral under **Law 14/2025 Art. 9**; and **pre-2025 contracts and
accrued rights**, which **Art. 4** protects.

→ **Which law governs depends on when the dispute arose, not when the contract was
signed.** A 2019 contract still being performed today sits under the new law with
its accrued rights preserved. Never answer this by the contract's date alone.

## The limits that will kill the claim

⚠️⚠️ **Civil Code Art. 378** — **workers' and servants' wages, daily and
non-daily, and the value of supplies they made, prescribe after ONE YEAR.**

⚠️ **Art. 378(2) — the oath.** Whoever pleads the one-year limitation **must swear
on oath that he actually paid the debt**, and the judge directs this of his own
motion, including to a minor's heirs or testamentary executors.

> That is the crux of most unpaid-wage claims. **A false statement there is far
> worse than the unpaid wage.** If you were paid in part, in cash, or not in the
> form claimed, you must say so.

⚠️ **Art. 387 — the court may NOT apply prescription of its own motion.** The
employer must plead the one-year limit expressly, and may plead it at any stage
including on appeal. So the defence does not save an employer who forgets to raise
it — but the claim does not survive if the employer remembers.

**Art. 383** — prescription is interrupted by a **judicial claim** (*even before an
incompetent court*), by **formal notice (التنبيه)**, by attachment, or a bankruptcy
claim. **Art. 384** — also by **the debtor's acknowledgment, express or tacit**.

→ **Serve a written demand.** An informal WhatsApp message over months does **not**
interrupt. See `skills/egy-legal-notice/`.

## Proving you worked

⚠️ **Art. 89 — the contract must be in writing, in Arabic, in four copies.** Where
there is **no written contract, both parties may prove the relationship and all
rights by all available means of proof.**

That cuts both ways. It is the employee's route in **and** the reason a paper trail
decides it.

**Gather:**
- **Social insurance records** from **NOSI** — the single most probative document,
  because the employer registered the employment
- **Payslips**, and any **wage record**
- **Bank statements** showing payment — or its absence
- **Attendance**, schedules, rosters, timesheets
- **Messages and emails** with the employer — WhatsApp included
- **The contract**, and any amendments
- **Any disciplinary file** — under **Art. 92** the employer must keep one, retain
  it **at least five years** after the relationship ends, and **until final
  judgment where a dispute exists**
- **Witnesses**

⚠️ **A screenshot is weak. A bank statement or an NOSI record is strong.** Build the
file around documents with institutional weight.

## Unlawful dismissal

⚠️ **Art. 148** restricts dismissal to a **closed list of seven serious-misconduct
cases**. Outside it, dismissal is unlawful. Two conditions are missed constantly:

- **(b)** serious damage to the employer — ⚠️ **the employer must notify the
  authorities within 24 hours of learning of it**, or the ground is gone
- **(c)** repeated failure to observe safety rules — ⚠️ the rules must be **written
  and visibly posted**, and put to the worker in writing

**Art. 165** — termination of an indefinite contract **without a legitimate reason**
carries compensation of **not less than two months' wages for each year of
service**, without prejudice to other entitlements.

**The protected reasons are exhaustive (Art. 165)**, and include:
- **(a)–(b)** trade-union affiliation, activity, or having sought a union role
- **(c)** ⚠️ **filing a complaint or initiating legal action against the employer**
- **(d)** **attachment (garnishment) of the worker's wages by court order**
- **(e)** **exercising the right to statutory leave**
- **(f)** colour, gender, marital status, family responsibilities, pregnancy

> **(c) is the live one in practice.** Firing someone because they complained or
> sued is not a redundancy. It is the highest-value claim available to them, at
> **two months' wages per year of service**.

⚠️ **(d) deserves a lawyer's eye.** Read literally, terminating a worker because
their wages are attached is barred. Do not treat that as settled.

## The procedure the employer must have followed

⚠️ **Art. 141** — **no disciplinary sanction at all** may be imposed unless the
worker was **notified in writing**, **heard and given the opportunity to defend**,
and a **record of the investigation is kept in the personal file**. Investigation
must **begin within 7 days** of discovery and be **completed within 3 months**,
extendable by 3 more. **Art. 138** — the violation must be **work-related**, and the
sanction must follow within **30 days** of completion.

**Art. 137** — where there are **10 or more workers** the employer must have
internal regulations, approved, **posted**. **Art. 140** — **one sanction for one
violation**; a wage deduction not combined so the total exceeds **five days'
wages per month**.

→ **A dismissal without this process was never lawful.** It is not a procedural
defect; it carries the Art. 165 compensation.

## If you were suspended

⚠️ **Arts. 145–147** — provisional suspension by written decision for **up to 60
days with full pay**, in three cases: referral for investigation where the
investigation's interests require it; accusation of a **felony or misdemeanour
involving dishonesty, breach of trust, public morality or a crime committed in the
workplace**; or the employer asks the labour court to dismiss. Appeal to the
**summary judge within 3 days**; the judge rules **by the next day**.

⚠️ **If charges are dropped, a dismissal order issues, or the worker is
acquitted — they must be reinstated with all withheld wages. Refusal is unlawful
dismissal.**

That is why suspending pending investigation is not a free action.

## The route, and the cost

**Art. 149–150 and 185–187 (Law 14/2025)** — **specialised labour courts**, each
with its **own enforcement department** for its own judgments (Decision 4621/2025).
Labour cassation runs through **specialised circuits inside the Court of Cassation
(Art. 186)**.

⚠️⚠️ **Art. 7 — the fee exemption.** Lawsuits by **workers, trainees, apprentices
or their beneficiaries are exempt from judicial fees and expenses at every stage**;
the court may order **immediate self-execution without bail**; and they are exempt
from **stamp duty**.

> **That is a structural advantage most employers do not know exists.** An employment
> claim in Egypt is cheap for the worker to bring and expensive for the employer to
> absorb. It changes the settlement posture entirely.

⚠️ **Art. 9 — pending cases are referred of their own motion** to the labour courts
in their current procedural posture, free of fees. Cases already decided or
reserved before 1 September 2025 stay with the original courts.

**Forum and limits:** see `skills/egy-court-filing-guide/`. Note worker claims are
fee-exempt, so the value threshold matters less than it would.

## Order of work

1. **Establish which law applies** — when the dispute arose
2. **Check the limitation period** — one year for wages; then check **Art. 378(2)**
   honestly
3. **Build the evidence file** — NOSI first, then bank statements, then documents
4. **Work out what is actually being claimed** — unpaid wages, severance, notice,
   a disciplinary penalty, or the whole relationship
5. **Check the employer's Art. 141 compliance** — if they skipped it, say so
6. **Serve a written demand** if there is any prospect of settlement — and know that
   this interrupts prescription
7. **File in the labour court** — fee-exempt if you are the worker
