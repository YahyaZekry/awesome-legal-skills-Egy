# Dispute Resolution under Egyptian Law

Governing law, forum, and the Egyptian traps. Read before touching the clause.

## Verified sources

- **Arbitration Law** — Law No. **27 of 1994** (قانون التحكيم في المواد المدنية والتجارية),
  signed 18 April 1994, Official Gazette **No. 16 bis, 21 April 1994**, in force
  22 May 1994. Amended by **Law 9/1997** and **Law 8/2000**.
- ❌ Not Law 34/1988. Nothing supports that citation.
- **Civil Code** — Law No. 131/1948, for governing-law principles and jurisdiction
  over civil matters.
- **Code of Civil and Commercial Procedures** — Law No. 13/1968. Arbitration Law
  Art. 3 **repealed Arts. 501–513** of it.

## Three questions, in order

### 1. Does arbitration even apply?

**Art. 1** — the Law applies to arbitrations between public-law and private-law
persons, **whatever the nature of the legal relationship**, where the arbitration
is **conducted in Egypt**, or where it is an international commercial arbitration
conducted abroad and the parties submit it to Egyptian law.

**Art. 1(2) — added by Law 9/1997, in force 15 May 1997.** For disputes
concerning **administrative contracts**, agreement to arbitrate **requires the
approval of the competent minister or the official assuming his powers in respect
of public legal persons, and delegation of that power is not permitted.**

> If the counterparty is a government body, a state-owned enterprise, or a public
> legal person, an arbitration clause **without the required approval is not
> enforceable**. This is checked early or not at all.

### 2. Court or arbitration?

**Arbitration is usually right when** the counterparty is foreign, the deal is
cross-border, the amount is large relative to court time, or enforceability
elsewhere matters.

**Court is usually right when** the counterparty is Egyptian and reachable, the
claim is small enough that fees dominate, you need interim measures urgently (see
below), or you want precedent/publicity.

**Watch these.**

| Trap | Provision | What it means for drafting |
|---|---|---|
| **Cairo Court of Appeal is exclusive** for international commercial arbitrations | Art. 9(1) | Supervision over **international commercial** arbitrations (seated in Egypt *or* abroad) lies with the Cairo Court of Appeal, **unless the parties agree another Egyptian Court of Appeal**. Do not assume you can pick your own supervisory court — and note you cannot contract out of Cairo entirely. |
| **Even number of arbitrators voids the arbitration** | Art. 15(1)–(2) | One arbitrator by agreement; failing agreement, **three**. If there is more than one, the number **must be odd — otherwise the arbitration is void (باطل)**. A template saying "two arbitrators" is fatal. |
| **Arabic is the default language** | Art. 29(1) | The language of the arbitration is **Arabic** unless the parties agree or the tribunal determines otherwise. A clause silent on language gives you an Arabic proceeding. |
| **90 days to annul** | Art. 54 | The action to annul an award runs **90 days from notification** to the party against whom it was rendered. Enforcing party must not assume it can wait. |
| **Interim measures: two different routes** | Arts. 14 and 24 | See below. |
| **State-entity consent** | Art. 1(2) | See above. |

### 3. Interim measures — do not conflate

**Route A — the court.** Arbitration Law **Art. 14**: on the request of either
party, the Art. 9 court may order an interim or conservatory measure **before the
arbitration commences or during it**. Available types are the court's ordinary
precautionary measures under the CPC (Law 13/1968) — not limited by the
Arbitration Law. ⚠️ The CPC measure article numbers are **not verified** in the
corpus; do not cite them.

**Route B — the tribunal, only by agreement.** Arbitration Law **Art. 24**: the
parties **may agree** to confer the power on the tribunal. Then:

1. The power must be **expressly conferred** — in the clause, later, or
   **inferred from the arbitration rules adopted** (agreeing to CRCICA Rules is
   itself such an agreement).
2. **The arbitration must have commenced.** Route A is available before
   commencement; Route B is not. This is the practical reason to know both.
3. **A party must request it.** The tribunal cannot act *sua sponte*, even with
   the power conferred.
4. General conditions: **probability** the applicant is entitled to its claim, and
   **imminent danger** to the subject matter absent the measure.
5. Security for costs may be ordered.

**Concurrency is preserved** — applying to the court does **not** waive the
arbitration agreement. You can do both.

**Practical difference:** a court order has imperium effect and is unrestricted as
to type, but obtaining one requires a suit with the other party heard. Enforcement
of a tribunal order may proceed by petition.

## Drafting text

### Court clause
> Any dispute arising out of or in connection with this Agreement shall be
> submitted to the exclusive jurisdiction of **[court name]** in [city], Arab
> Republic of Egypt. Either party may seek interim or conservatory measures from
> any competent Egyptian court at any time, including before proceedings are
> commenced.
>
> تختص **محكمة [الاسم]** في **[المدينة]**، جمهورية مصر العربية، حصرياً بأي نزاع
> ينشأ عن هذا العقد أو يتصل به. ولأحد الطرفين أن يطلب تدابير مؤقتة أو تحفظية من
> أي محكمة مصرية مختصة في أي وقت، حتى قبل بدء الإجراءات.

> ⚠️ Fill in the court deliberately. "The competent courts" is the default you
> are usually trying to avoid. For an international commercial dispute, remember
> the Cairo Court of Appeal's supervisory role is a separate question from
> first-instance jurisdiction — specifying a first-instance court does not
> displace it.

### Arbitration clause — Egypt-seated
> Any dispute arising out of or in connection with this Agreement shall be
> finally settled by arbitration under the Arbitration Law No. 27 of 1994
> (as amended). The seat shall be [Cairo / Alexandria], Arab Republic of Egypt.
> The tribunal shall consist of **one** arbitrator, appointed in accordance with the
> [institution] Arbitration Rules. **The language of the arbitration shall be
> [English / Arabic].** The award shall be final and binding.
>
> يُحال أي نزاع ينشأ عن هذا العقد أو يتصل به إلى التحكيم النهائي وفقاً لقانون
> التحكيم رقم **٢٧ لسنة ١٩٩٤** وتعديلاته. ويكون مقر التحكيم **[القاهرة]**،
> جمهورية مصر العربية. وتتكون هيئة التحكيم من **محكم واحد** يُعيَّن وفقاً لقواعد
> **[الجهة]**. **ولغة التحكيم [الإنجليزية / العربية].** ويكون الحكم النهائي
> وملزماً للطرفين.

> The **bolded items are the ones that void or damage the clause if wrong.** Odd
> number. Express language. Named seat.

### Arbitration clause — cross-border
> … shall be finally settled by arbitration **seated in [free zone / Cairo]**, in
> accordance with the **New York Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of
> Foreign Arbitral Awards (1958)** and the Arbitration Law No. 27 of 1994. The
> number of arbitrators shall be **three**. The language shall be **English**.
> Each party waives any objection to the jurisdiction of the arbitral tribunal.

Egypt acceded to the New York Convention on **9 March 1959** by **Presidential
Decree No. 171 of 1959**, in force **7 June 1959**, with **no reservations** — no
reciprocity reservation and no commercial reservation. That is unusually clean and
worth relying on.

## Things not to write

- **"Two arbitrators"** — voids the arbitration (Art. 15(2)).
- **"The language shall be as determined by the tribunal"** — technically possible
  under Art. 29(1) but you have given away a predictable decision. Name the language.
- **An arbitration clause in an administrative contract with no ministerial
  approval** — unenforceable (Art. 1(2)).
- **No clause at all** — you get the default forum and the default law, which is
  rarely what either party wanted.
