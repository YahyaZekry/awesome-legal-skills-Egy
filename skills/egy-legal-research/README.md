Find and verify Egyptian law — the Official Gazette vs Al-Waqa' al-Misriyya distinction, the ELP legislation portal, the Ministry of Justice legislation encyclopedia, Court of Cassation principles and annual volumes, and how to verify a citation before relying on it.

Exists because Egyptian law is cited constantly and wrongly. Building the corpus behind these skills overturned **more than twenty** widely-repeated facts, four of them things models assert confidently — the Commercial Code is 17/1999 not 12/1943, arbitration is 27/1994 not 34/1988, e-signature is 15/2004 not 15/2020, and Labour Law 12/2003 is repealed.

Carries the traps that cost time: **`alqalam.idsc.gov.eg` and `ela.gov.eg` have no DNS record at all** despite being cited everywhere as Egypt's legislation database (use `elpai.idsc.gov.eg`); **`gcc.gov.eg` returns HTTP 200 and is the Cultural Development Fund**, not the gazette; GAFI's legacy paths **200 into a styled 404** because the site is a SPA; and the Ministry of Justice's encyclopedia at `serviceapi.egyptianlaws.com` is the single best source — it carries **Supreme Constitutional Court rulings annotated inline**, which is how two tenancy statutes were obtained without a paywalled database.

Also states the point that trips agents hardest: **Egyptian Cassation "principles" are not binding precedent**. The Technical Office deliberately **exercises discretion not to publish** judgments it does not consider fit to become a principle — precisely so that publishing does not convert case-specific justice into a binding rule. Cite the annual volume, never write "Egypt follows the rule that". And records a methodology finding: Firecrawl's query mode **silently returned the wrong articles twice** on the official Arabic Civil Code PDF, so Arabic legal PDFs must be downloaded and extracted locally.
