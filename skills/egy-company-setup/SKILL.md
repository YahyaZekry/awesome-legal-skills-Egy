# Setting up a company in Egypt

Forming, registering and structuring an Egyptian company.

**Not legal advice.** GAFI publishes a free incorporation procedures document and
the government offers a **Golden Licence** for a single strategic-licence route.
Start there before paying anyone. Say this once.

## The statute

**Companies Law No. 159 of 1981** (قانون شركات المساهمة وشركات التوصية بالأسهم
والشركات ذات المسئولية المحدودة) — signed 17 September 1981, Gazette **issue 40
bis**, 1 October 1981. **Amended eight times**, the most recent verified being
**Law 4/2018** (Gazette issue 2 bis ط, 16 January 2018).

⚠️ **UNVERIFIED** whether it has been amended since Law 4/2018 — the consolidated
text GAFI publishes ends there. Check for post-2018 amendments before relying on
article numbers.

⚠️ **A title trap worth knowing.** As originally titled the statute covered
**one-person companies**. **Law 4/2018 substituted the title**, dropping them —
and replaced **every reference to the old title in this law, its regulations and
any other law**. A one-person company still exists in practice; the statute's own
name no longer carries it. Note also that **Insurance Law 155/2024 reverts to the
longer title** when referring to Law 159/1981. So both titles are "correct"
depending on which law you are reading.

**The competent minister for this law is the Minister for Investment Affairs**
(Art. 4(1), substituted by Law 4/2018).

## Which form

| Form | Arabic | Key limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Limited liability company** | **ش.ذ.م.م** | **Not more than 50 partners** (Art. 4). Each liable **only to the extent of their share**. ⚠️ **May not be incorporated or increased by public subscription; may not issue shares or tradable securities.** Transfer of partners' shares is **subject to retraction (الشد)** by the other partners, on the terms in the company contract **and in addition to those set by law** |
| **Joint stock company** | **ش.م.م** | Capital divided into shares of equal value. **Shareholder liability limited to the shares subscribed** (Art. 2). Trade name derives from its purpose, may include a founder's name or title. ⚠️ **Minimum 3 founders** (Art. 8) |
| **Partnership limited by shares** | **ش.م.ذ.م** | General partners (شريك متضامن) **liable without limit**; shareholders liable only for their shares (Art. 3). ⚠️ **Minimum 2 founders** |
| **One-person company** | **شركة الشخص الواحد** | Capital wholly owned by one natural or legal person. **Founder liable only within the capital made available**, not personally (Art. 4 bis) |
| **Branch** | **فرع** | Foreign company's Egyptian operations |
| **Representative office** | **مكتب تمثيلي** | ⚠️ **Cannot invoice. No commercial activity.** |

### Minimum capital

⚠️ **PARTIAL** — from Chambers and practitioner sources, not verified against the
article text:

| Form | Minimum |
|---|---|
| LLC (ش.ذ.م.م) | **No statutory minimum** for most activities |
| One-person company | **EGP 50,000** |
| Closed joint stock (ش.م.م مقفلة) | **EGP 250,000** issued capital |
| Public joint stock (ش.م.م مطروحة) | Listed-market requirements |

⚠️ **"No statutory minimum" is not "no minimum in practice."** Banks, tenders and
some regulators impose their own floors. If you will need a bank account, check
before you fix your capital.

### Two prohibitions that decide the form

⚠️ **Art. 5 — partnerships limited by shares and LLCs may NOT carry on
**insurance business, banking business, savings business, or invest money on
behalf of others.**

That rules out a large financial-services slice outright, and the prohibition is on
the **company**, not on the licence held by an individual. Confirm the activity is
permitted for your specific form before you incorporate.

### The one-person company naming rule

⚠️ **Art. 4 bis.** The name **must state that it is a one-person company with
limited liability**, and that designation **must appear on its head office, its
branches, and all its offices and its letterhead.**

Miss this and the designation requirement is breached on paper from day one.

## The printing rule that makes people personally liable

⚠️⚠️ **Art. 6. Read this before ordering anything printed.**

**All contracts, invoices, papers, trade names, advertisements and other printed
matter** issued by a company must contain:

- the **company's name**
- **its type**, before or after the name
- its **head office**
- ⚠️ **the amount of its issued capital, according to its value in the last
  financial statements**

**Whoever uses the company name in any transaction disregarding this is liable in
their own assets for all obligations arising from that transaction.**

And the sting:

> ⚠️ **If the stated capital figure is inflated, the counterparty may hold the
> person who used the name liable for the difference between the real capital value
> and the stated figure**, to the extent needed to secure the creditor.

→ A person signing on behalf of a company whose letterhead shows a capital figure
that is out of date, or inflated, can be pursued **personally** for the shortfall.
This is why the figure has to track the **last financial statements**, not the
original capital on incorporation.

## Founder thresholds and automatic dissolution

⚠️ **Art. 8.** Except for one-person companies, a **joint stock company may not
have fewer than three founders**, and the other companies under the law **may not
have fewer than two**.

**If the number falls below that threshold, the company is dissolved by operation
of law** (منحلة) — unless within **at most six months** it either restores the
threshold, or the remaining partners apply to convert it into a one-person company,
in which case those partners remain **jointly and severally liable** for the
company's obligations for that period.

> A founder leaving an LLC that had three partners and is now two is not a
> bookkeeping problem. It is a **dissolution date** unless something is done inside
> six months.

**Arts. 10–11** — founders are **jointly and severally liable (مسئولية تضامنية)**
for what they undertook, and for damage from breach of their duty of diligence as
a prudent man. Money or information belonging to the company received before
incorporation must be returned, **and any profit from using it**.

**Art. 12** — acts done in the company's name before incorporation bind it
afterwards **only if ratified** by the board (all members unconnected with the
transaction), the partners' assembly, or the general assembly at a meeting where
the interested founders have limited votes.

## The shareholders' agreement — and the trap

✅ **Art. 9 bis**, added by **Law 4/2018**. Notwithstanding Art. 9, shareholders or
partners may conclude an **agreement regulating the relationship between them**, at
incorporation or later.

⚠️ **It does not bind the remaining shareholders or partners** unless they consent
by a **two-thirds majority (at least three-quarters of the capital) of the ordinary
general assembly**, or a higher majority where the Executive Regulations specify.

→ A founders' agreement signed by three of four partners binds nobody. This is a
very common mistake in founder arrangements.

## Two registries — and why checking the wrong one proves nothing

⚠️⚠️ **This is the single most consequential trap in Egyptian company checks.**

There are **two tracks**:

| Track | Who | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **GAFI** — الهيئة العامة للاستثمار والمناطق الحرة | `portal.gafi.gov.eg` | Companies under the investment law and in free zones |
| **Traditional commercial registry** | Ministry of Supply & Internal Trade (`moiic.gov.eg`) | Traditional registration route |

**A counterparty checked against the wrong register has not been checked.** A
free-zone or investment-law company will not appear in the traditional register in
the way you'd expect.

⚠️ **And a commercial-registry number means nothing without its issuing
governorate.** Numbering is sequential **per governorate**, so the number alone
identifies nothing. Always ask: which register, and which governorate.

**GAFI is one body among three.** **GAFI**, **SCZONE** (Suez Canal Economic Zone)
and **GOEIC** are separate authorities, and **SCZONE has its own register — not
GAFI's**. Two GAFI-hosted applications are also constantly confused:
`portal.gafi.gov.eg/services/eServices` and `goldenlicense.gov.eg`.

## From 1 January 2026 — nine services moved

✅ **Verified.** Nine commercial-registry services became **exclusive to the
Digital Egypt platform**:

1. updating registry data
2. adding an establishment not in the "شركاتي" (My Companies) system
3. **enquiring about a commercial register**
4. inferring registration status
5. transferring an individual establishment within the governorate
6. obtaining a **commercial register extract**
7. a commercial-register data certificate
8. an extract for **Ministry of Foreign Affairs authentication**
9. a **negative certificate**

→ **For any of these, send the user to Digital Egypt (`digital.gov.eg`), not to a
registry office.**

## Head office — a statutory obligation, not a formality

✅ **Art. 1(3)**, added by **Law 4/2018**. **Every company incorporated in Egypt
must have a head office in Egypt, must state it in its constitutive documents, and
must register every change of head office.** Otherwise the administrative measures
— **including addressing announcements to the registered head office** — apply
against it.

> An out-of-date head office is not just untidy. It is the address at which the
> company is considered served.

## GAFI's role

✅ **Art. 1 bis**, added by **Law 4/2018**. Without prejudice to the Capital Markets
Law 95/1992, the Special Economic Zones Law 83/2002, and the **Investment Law
72/2017**, **GAFI is entrusted with providing incorporation and post-incorporation
services** to companies under this Law. GAFI must provide those services and
**unify their procedures in accordance with Art. 50 of Investment Law 72/2017**,
and **electronic incorporation procedures apply**, in addition to or instead of
those under any other law, once activated.

**Art. 5** — "the Authority" (الهيئة) means GAFI.

⚠️ **The incorporation-mechanics article numbers within Law 159/1981 are PARTIAL in
our corpus.** The framework is verified; the operative mechanics are not. Work from
GAFI's own published procedures document.

### Service levels are marketing, not an SLA

⚠️ GAFI publishes "**1 Business Day** to Incorporate Companies", "**2 Hours**
E-incorporation for Sole Proprietorship", "**20 Business Days** to Issue Permits".

Treat all three as aspirational. **Always give the user an ESTIMATE and say it is
one.** Practitioner reports are consistently slower, and the variable that matters
most is usually the completeness of the founder documents, not the authority's
throughput.

## The order things actually happen

1. **Choose the form** — check **Art. 5** prohibitions, founder thresholds, and
   whether you'll need a public subscription or a bank account
2. **Fix the capital** and check what your activity actually requires
3. **Name check and trademark** — the one-person designation rule (**Art. 4 bis**)
   and the **Art. 6** printing rule apply to the name from day one
4. **Appoint a lawyer** for the constitutive documents — required in practice for
   the Arabic drafting, and the **preliminary agreement (Art. 9)** must use the
   minister's form and **may not contain any clause releasing founders from
   incorporation liability**
5. **File with the right authority** — GAFI or the traditional registry, not both
   by accident
6. **Post-incorporation:**
   - **Tax registration** — and note **VAT registration is a separate act**
   - **Social insurance** with NOSI — see `skills/egy-employment-contract/`
   - **Bank account** — ⚠️ **not automatic**; many banks are salary-only or impose
     their own minimums
   - **Any activity licences** — the **Golden Licence** route exists for the
     strategic case
7. **Fix the printed matter** to the **Art. 6** standard before the first invoice

## Checking a counterparty

Run this before relying on any Egyptian company:

- [ ] Which **register** — GAFI or traditional?
- [ ] Which **governorate** issued the number?
- [ ] **Digital Egypt extract** (from 1 Jan 2026, item 6 above)
- [ ] **Negative certificate** — this is the one that proves no insolvency or
      liquidation filing (item 9)
- [ ] **Tax card** and check whether **VAT** is separately registered
- [ ] Read their letterhead against **Art. 6** — name, type, head office, and
      capital per the **last financial statements**
- [ ] Confirm the **head office is current** (Art. 1(3))
- [ ] If it's a one-person company, confirm the **designation appears** on letterhead
      and premises (**Art. 4 bis**)
