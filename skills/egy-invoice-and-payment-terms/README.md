Draft invoice and payment terms, chase overdue payments and enforce a debt in Egypt — EGP invoicing, due dates, default interest and the statutory ceiling, cheque and promissory note wording, which debt claims prescribe at one year and which at five or fifteen, interrupting prescription, and what payment evidence to keep.

Leads with the two ceilings that quietly change what a clause is worth: **Art. 227(1)** cuts any agreed default interest to **7%** with restitution of the excess — a 15% term is not invalid, it is silently reduced, and a counterparty who knows the article will use it to argue bad faith. And **Art. 224** means agreed compensation is **not due if the debtor proves no loss**, while **Art. 225** makes a penalty clause a **ceiling** on ordinary claims rather than a floor.

The limitation table is the part that loses people money: rent and professional claims at **5 years**, general contractual claims at **15**, but **1 year** for a merchant's sale to a non-merchant, for hotel and restaurant bills, and for **workers' wages** — where **Art. 378(2) requires the party pleading the one-year limit to swear on oath that he actually paid the debt**.

Also carries the asymmetry that decides whether to bother chasing: **Art. 387** — the court **may not** apply prescription of its own motion, so an un-enforced debt can sit for years and die silently unless someone pleads it. Serving an إنذار interrupts it under **Art. 383**. And on cheques: the paper is the instrument, not the electronic record; T+1 applies nationwide since Decree 13/2019, so the old Cairo/Delta/remote clearing table is abolished; and foreign-currency cheques clear **Monday–Thursday only**, which breaks any plan built on the Egyptian working week.
