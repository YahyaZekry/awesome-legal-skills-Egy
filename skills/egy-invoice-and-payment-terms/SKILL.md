# Invoicing, payment terms and recovering a debt in Egypt

**Not legal advice.** For a contested debt, or one large enough that fees matter,
get an Egyptian lawyer. Say so once.

## Write invoices that survive

⚠️ **The single most useful provision for you is Art. 11 of the Consumer Protection
Law**, which applies to consumer sales and is worth mirroring in every B2B term:
**if the invoice is not issued as required, the other side may prove the product's
specifications and all other contract elements by any means of proof.**

A defective invoice does not lose you the sale. **It loses you the evidence**, and
hands the customer the argument.

**Art. 10 — what an invoice must contain:**

- the **tax registration number**
- transaction date
- price
- specifications
- nature, type and quantity

Plus, under **Art. 5**, an invoice must be **in Arabic**, bearing the supplier's
address, contact methods, commercial-register registration data and trade licence.

**Art. 7** — prices must be displayed **inclusive of taxes and other financial
charges**. So: **VAT-inclusive**, not "from EGP X + VAT".

⚠️ **From 1 July 2023, expenses in the tax return are accepted only via the
electronic invoice.** If your business deducts costs, the invoice is not an
accounting nicety — it is the thing that lets you claim the expense at all.

## Interest — the ceiling you cannot draft around

⚠️ **Civil Code Art. 227(1)** — whatever rate the parties agree, **any rate above
the statutory price is reduced to 7%**, with **restitution of the excess**.

> A term promising 15% is **not invalid**. It is **silently cut to 7%**, and the
> excess is clawed back. Drafters over-promise here constantly without knowing, and
> a counterparty who knows the article will use it to argue bad faith.

**Statutory defaults — Art. 226:**

| Type of matter | Default interest |
|---|---|
| **Civil** | **4% p.a.** |
| **Commercial** | **5% p.a.** |

Both run **from the date of the judicial claim**, unless the agreement or
**commercial custom** sets another date.

⚠️ **Art. 232** — **no interest on capitalised interest**, and total interest may
**never exceed the principal**, save where commercial custom provides otherwise.

## Agreed compensation is not automatic money

⚠️ **Civil Code Art. 224** — agreed compensation is **not due if the debtor proves
the creditor suffered no loss**, and the court may reduce it if grossly overstated
or partly performed. Agreements to the contrary are **void**.

⚠️ **Art. 225** — loss **beyond** the agreed figure cannot be claimed **unless the
debtor committed fraud or gross fault**.

→ A penalty clause is an **evidentiary starting point the other side can defeat**,
and a **ceiling on ordinary claims rather than a floor**. Do not present a
late-payment penalty to the client as guaranteed income.

## Limitation — the periods are not uniform, and one of them requires an oath

⚠️ **This is where debt claims actually get lost.**

| Claim | Period | Art. |
|---|---|---|
| **General contractual claim** | **15 years** | 374 |
| **Rent** — buildings and agricultural land | **5 years** | 375 |
| Doctors', lawyers', engineers', brokers', teachers' professional claims | **5 years** | 376 |
| Taxes and duties owed to the State | **3 years** | 377 |
| **Goods sold by a merchant to a non-merchant** | **1 year** | 378 |
| **Hotel and restaurant bills** | **1 year** | 378 |
| **Workers' and servants' wages — daily and non-daily, and the value of supplies they made** | **1 year** | 378 |

⚠️⚠️ **Art. 378(2) — the oath.** Whoever pleads the **one-year** limitation
**must swear on oath that he actually paid the debt**, and the judge directs this
of his own motion, including to a minor's heirs or testamentary executors.

> That is a real evidential trap. If you are owed unpaid wages, the limitation
> defence carries an oath requirement — and a false statement carries consequences
> far worse than the unpaid wage.

**Art. 379** — for Arts. 376–378 claims the time runs from the creditor's
presentation of the claim; **but where a bond (سند) is executed, the claim is
prescribed only after 15 years.**

## Stopping the clock

The defensive side, and the reason to not sit on a debt.

⚠️ **Art. 383** — prescription is interrupted by:
- a **judicial claim** — *even if brought before an incompetent court*
- **formal notice (التنبيه)**
- **attachment (الحجز)**
- a claim filed in bankruptcy or distribution proceedings
- any act the creditor takes to enforce his right during a suit

⚠️ **Art. 384** — also by **the debtor's acknowledgment, express or tacit**. Leaving
property with the creditor as a possessory pledge (رهن حيازي) securing the debt
counts as tacit acknowledgment.

**Art. 385** — after interruption a **new** prescription starts from the end of the
interrupting cause, of the same length — **except** where a judgment has become res
judicata, or a one-year debt was interrupted by acknowledgment, where the new
period is **15 years**.

⚠️ **Art. 387 — the asymmetry.** **The court may NOT apply prescription of its own
motion.** The debtor must plead it, on his own application or that of any person
interested, and he may plead it **at any stage including on appeal**.

> **This is the practical reason to serve an إنذار rather than wait.** A debt can
> sit un-enforced for years and the court will let the claim die silently unless
> somebody pleads prescription. See `skills/egy-legal-notice/`.

**Art. 386** — prescription extinguishes the obligation but leaves a **natural
obligation (التزام طبيعي)**: no longer judicially enforceable, but not a release.

## Cheques — still real, and structurally different

⚠️ **Do not repeat the old geography table.** ✅ The CBE Cheque Clearing House
regulations, as amended by **Governor's Decree 13/2019**, settle **on the day
following presentation (T+1), regardless of where the drawee bank's branch is.** The
old Cairo T+1 / Delta T+2 / remote T+3 scheme is **abolished**. Practitioner
material showing that table predates 2019.

⚠️ **The paper is the instrument.** ✅ **CCH Regulations Art. 5** — the **paper
form serves as the legal force**, while the electronic data form is for
**accounting** only. **Disputes go to paper.** "The system shows it cleared" is not
the same as holding the instrument.

**CCH requirements:**
- EGP cheques: all governorates, T+1
- ⚠️ **Instruments for the EGP CCH must be valued in Egyptian pounds**
- ✅ **Commercial Code (Law 17 of 1999), transitional provisions:** in commercial
  matters, **the creditor may demand payment by cheque where the debt exceeds
  EGP 100,000**

**Foreign-currency cheques — where payment plans break:**
- Covered currencies: **US dollars and euros only**
- **Two sessions, on consecutive days, Monday to Thursday**, excluding official
  holidays **in Egypt and in the issuing country**
- ⚠️ **There is no Friday or Saturday session.** A plan built on the Egyptian
  working week fails here.

⚠️ **Cheque wording.** Amounts appear twice — **الأرقام** (figures) and **بالحروف**
(in words). **The words-line is the controlling one.** Close it with **لا غير**
("and nothing more") — **omit it and the line is open to tampering.** Writing the
amount out is **تفقيط**. Revenue stamp (**دمغة / ضريبة الدمغة**) is a tax paid at the
bank, not a postmark.

Cheques are in decline but alive: in **July 2025** the CBE processed **6.040 million
cheques worth EGP 2.56 trillion** — volume −41% YoY.

## Promissory note (كمبيالة) vs cheque (شيك)

**Same family, different function.** A **شيك** is drawn on a bank and travels
through clearing. A **كمبيالة** is a written undertaking by the maker, drawn by the
payer on the payee — it is an instrument in its own right and is endorsed like a
bill.

⚠️ In practice the two are used interchangeably and confused in informal
arrangements. That confusion is exactly why **Art. 378(2)'s oath requirement**
bites later — the debtor swears he paid, and the instrument is ambiguous.

**If you are taking a كمبيالة as security, get the wording right and take the
security on the debt, not on a promise about the debt.**

## What evidence to keep

| Method | Keep |
|---|---|
| **Cash** | A **signed, dated written receipt (إيصال)** naming payer, payee, amount in **figures and words**, the purpose, and the property or period concerned. ⚠️ For a large amount, **a bank-routed transfer beats any receipt** |
| **Bank transfer / IBAN** | The **bank confirmation** plus a **statement extract** covering the period. Must show date, payer, payee, amount. Arabic practice commentary treats these as **official documents with high evidentiary weight** |
| **Cheque** | The **original cheque** + payer's statement showing the **debit** + payee's statement showing the **credit** + the **rejection slip** if returned |
| **Promissory note** | The **original instrument**, all **endorsements**, and the **note's date** — it fixes when the debt is due, and therefore when prescription starts |
| **Mobile wallet / InstaPay** | The **app confirmation or SMS + transaction reference**, plus the payee's **account statement**. ⚠️ **Screenshots alone are weak** |

> **A screenshot is not a payment record. A bank statement is.** If the transaction
> is material, route it through a bank so the statement exists.

## Foreign currency in an invoice

**Never hard-code a rate.** The pound has moved violently and recently — a float on
**6 March 2024** took it from roughly **30.85** to roughly **49.4** against the USD
within weeks.

Any invoice or contract mixing currencies must state four things:

1. **the currency**
2. **the rate source**
3. **the rate date**
4. **who bears movement** between the rate date and payment

If nobody is told, the default is contested. And check **Art. 227(1)** — the 7%
ceiling interacts with any late-payment term you write.

## Chasing a debt — the order

1. **Check the limitation period first.** One year, five, or fifteen? A year-old
   wage claim and a year-old merchant claim are both **1 year** and both carry the
   **Art. 378(2) oath** on the defence side
2. **Confirm the debt is documented** — invoice, contract, cheque or note
3. **Get a demand** — `skills/egy-legal-notice/`. Serving an إنذار **interrupts
   prescription (Art. 383)** and fixes the evidential record
4. **Do not rely on the silence.** ⚠️ **Art. 387** — the court will not raise
   prescription for you
5. **Weigh the fees.** ⚠️ Judicial fees are governed by **Law 90 of 1944** as
   amended by **126/2009**. ⚠️ Court of Appeal tariff changes of 2024–25 are
   **administrative, not legislative**, and are under challenge — **do not quote a
   tariff as statutory.** For a small sum, compare the fee against the claim
6. **Consider the security** — if there is a cheque or a promissory note, say so
   plainly; a secured claim is worth more than an unsecured one
