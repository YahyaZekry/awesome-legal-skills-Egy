# Employment contracts and dismissal — Egypt (Labour Law 14/2025)

**Not legal advice.** Get an Egyptian employment lawyer before a dismissal. The
rest of this is the working mechanics.

## The law changed on 1 September 2025

⚠️ **Law No. 12 of 2003 is repealed.** The operative statute is now the
**Labour Law No. 14 of 2025** (قانون العمل), in force **1 September 2025**, which
repealed Law 12/2003 and Law 125/2010 at **Art. 12**.

Anything you read online about Egyptian employment law from before late 2025 is
describing a repealed statute.

**What survives the repeal** — Law 12/2003 still governs, narrowly:
- disputes filed **before 1 September 2025**;
- the pending-case referral under Law 14/2025 **Art. 9**;
- **pre-2025 employment contracts and accrued rights** — **Art. 4** of the new law
  protects previously acquired wages and benefits.

**So: which law governs a contract depends on when the dispute arose, not on
when the contract was signed.** A 2019 contract still being performed today sits
under the new law with its accrued rights preserved. Never answer this question
by the contract's date alone.

## The requirement that catches almost everyone

⚠️ **Art. 89 — the employment contract must be in writing, in Arabic, in FOUR copies:**

1. the **employer**
2. the **worker**
3. the **social insurance office**
4. the **relevant administrative authority**

For a foreign worker who does not speak Arabic, a bilingual contract is allowed —
and **the Arabic version prevails**.

**Required contents:** start date; employer name and workplace; worker's name,
qualifications, trade, insurance number, residence and ID; nature and type of work;
wage, payment method and schedule, and any additional monetary or in-kind benefits.

Where there is **no written contract, both parties may prove the relationship and
all rights by all available means of proof.** The employer must give the worker a
**receipt for deposited documents**.

> **An English-only contract is not compliant**, and the risk is not cosmetic: it
> hands the employee the right to prove the relationship however they like, with
> no document of your own to point at.

## When your contract is actually indefinite

**Art. 88** — a contract is **deemed indefinite** if it is:

- **(a)** not written; or
- **(b)** silent as to duration; or
- **(c)** fixed-term but **performed by both parties after expiry without a written
  agreement**

(c) is the one that catches employers. A fixed-term contract that simply rolls
over — nobody signs anything, everyone keeps working — becomes indefinite. It then
carries notice obligations and severance exposure it was written to avoid.

## Probation

**Art. 90** — probation **may not exceed three months**, and a worker **may not be
placed on probation more than once with the same employer**.

🟡 Law 14/2025 does **not** repeat the old Law 12/2003 Art. 33 formulation about
either party being free to terminate during probation. **UNVERIFIED — check the
primary text** before relying on a right to terminate during probation.

## Dismissal — the closed list

⚠️ **This is the whole point of the skill. Art. 148 restricts dismissal to a
closed list of seven serious-misconduct cases. You cannot dismiss outside it.**

**Art. 148** — only the competent labour court may impose the penalty of
dismissal. All other disciplinary penalties may be imposed by the employer or a
delegated authority. **In all cases, a worker may only be dismissed for serious
misconduct.**

The seven cases:

| # | Ground | Condition attached |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | Proven impersonation or submission of **falsified documents** | — |
| **(b)** | An act causing **serious damage** to the employer | ⚠️ **the employer must notify the authorities within 24 hours of learning of it** |
| **(c)** | Repeated failure to observe **written, visibly posted** safety rules | despite written warnings |
| **(d)** | Disclosing **trade secrets** causing substantial harm | — |
| **(e)** | **Competing** with the employer's business | — |
| **(f)** | Being **intoxicated or under the influence of drugs during working hours** | — |
| **(g)** | Assaulting the employer, the general manager, or a supervisor | at or because of work |

> Note the conditions: (b) has a **24-hour notification deadline** that quietly
> removes the ground if you miss it, and (c) requires the safety rules to be
> **written and visibly posted** and to have been put to the worker in writing.
> Two employers lose a dismissal they thought was sound because the rules were
> never posted.

## The protected reasons — and what dismissal costs

**Art. 165** — where the employer terminates an indefinite contract **without a
legitimate reason**, the worker is entitled to compensation of **not less than two
months' wages for each year of service**, without prejudice to other entitlements.

**The unjustified reasons are listed exhaustively** — this is not a
non-exhaustive list:

- **(a)** trade-union affiliation, or participation in union activities under the law
- **(b)** serving or having served as a union representative, or seeking such a role
- **(c)** **filing a complaint or initiating legal action against the employer**, or
  participating in such actions, regarding violations of law, regulations or the
  employment contract
- **(d)** **attachment (garnishment) of the worker's wages by court order**
- **(e)** **exercising the right to statutory leave**
- **(f)** colour, gender, marital status, family responsibilities, pregnancy, and
  the other protected characteristics

> **(c) is the live one in practice.** Firing someone because they complained or
> sued is not a redundancy — it is the highest-value claim available to them, at
> two months' wages per year of service.

> **(d) deserves a second look.** The statute lists wage garnishment as an
> unjustified reason for dismissal. Read literally, terminating a worker because
> their wages are attached is barred. 🟡 Flag this to a lawyer rather than
> treating it as settled.

## Process — without it, no penalty is lawful at all

**Art. 141** — **no disciplinary sanction may be imposed unless** the worker was:

1. **notified in writing** of the alleged violation,
2. **heard and given the opportunity to defend**, and
3. a record of the investigation is **kept in the personal file**

Investigation must **begin within 7 days** of discovery and be **completed within 3
months**, extendable by up to 3 more. The worker's **labour union may attend**. For a
written warning or a deduction ≤ one day's wage the investigation may be oral.
**The sanction decision must be reasoned and justified.**

**Art. 138** — the violation must be **work-related**, and **no sanction may be
imposed more than 30 days after the investigation is completed.**

⚠️ A dismissal without this process is not a procedural defect. It is a dismissal
that was never lawful, and it carries the Art. 165 compensation.

## Penalties available short of dismissal

**Art. 139** — the closed list: written warning; deduction from basic wage;
**postponement of the annual raise for up to 3 months**; deprivation of up to
**half** the annual raise; **delay in promotion up to 1 year**; reduction of basic
wage by no more than one increment; demotion to the immediately lower grade
without wage reduction; and termination in accordance with the law.

- **Art. 140** — **no more than one sanction for the same violation**; a wage
  deduction may not be combined with another financial penalty if the total exceeds
  **five days' wages per month**.
- **Art. 143** — a deduction for a single violation may not exceed **five days'
  basic wage**; total deductions in one month may not exceed five days' basic wage.
- **Art. 144** — a penalty may be **increased for a repeat** of the same type of
  violation within **one year** of notification of the prior penalty.

**Art. 137** — if you have **10 or more workers** you **must** have internal work
regulations covering promotion, transfer, wages, job-duty violations and
disciplinary sanctions. Prepare within **60 days**, submit to the administrative
authority; the labour union has **15 days** to respond (silence = approval); if the
authority neither approves nor objects within **30 days** the regulations are deemed
effective; **and they must be posted.**

> This is where most employers who "dismissed for serious misconduct" fail — the
> rules were never made, never posted, and Art. 148(c) requires posting.

## Exit mechanics

- **Art. 156** — either party may terminate an **indefinite** contract on **three
  months' written notice**.
- **Art. 158** — notice may **not** be made conditional (suspensive or resolutive
  condition).
- **Art. 159** — notice may **not be given during the employee's leave**; the
  period starts the day after the leave ends; sick leave suspends the period.
- **Art. 161** — the notice period **may not be waived or reduced**; only extended
  by agreement. If the **employee** initiates, the employer may waive it wholly or
  partly.
- **Art. 162** — if the **employer** gives notice, the employee gets **one full
  day per week (8 hours) off with full pay** to look for another job.
- **Art. 92** — the worker file must be retained **at least five years** after the
  relationship ends, and **until a final judgment where a dispute exists**. Do not
  destroy files.
- **Art. 93** — the employer must return the worker to the contract location
  **within three days** of the contract ending.

## Costs and procedural advantage

⚠️ **Art. 7 — worker and trainee lawsuits are exempt from judicial fees and
expenses at every stage**, the court may order **immediate self-execution without
bail**, and they are exempt from **stamp duty**.

That is a structural advantage for employees that most employers do not know
exists. It changes the settlement posture entirely: an employment claim in Egypt is
cheap for the worker to bring and hard for the employer to absorb.

## Working hours, rest and leave — Arts. 117–126

✅ **Verified against the full English translation of Law 14/2025** (Andersen Egypt,
retrieved in full, 180 articles). Book Three, Chapter One, Section Four.

**Art. 117 — the core limits.** Without prejudice to **Law 133 of 1961** on
employment in industrial establishments, an employee may not be required to perform
actual work for **more than 8 hours per day or 48 hours per week**, excluding meal
and rest periods. **The competent Minister may, by decree, reduce** the maximum for
certain categories or industries.

**Art. 118 — breaks.** The working day must include one or more rest and meal
periods totalling **not less than one hour**, and the schedule must ensure an
employee does **not work more than 5 consecutive hours without a break**. The
Minister may, by decree, specify cases where continuous work without rest is
permitted for technical or operational reasons.

**Art. 119 — the span.** The duration **between the start and end** of working hours
must **not exceed 10 hours per day**. Rest periods count as time at the workplace
if the employee remains on-site.

**Art. 120 — weekly rest.** The employer must give each employee a weekly rest
period of **not less than 24 continuous hours after a maximum of 6 consecutive
working days**, and **the weekly rest is paid**. Exception for remote areas or work
whose nature or conditions require continuous operations.

**Art. 122 — post it.** The employer must **post** at the main entrances or in a
visible place: the **weekly rest day**, **working hours**, **scheduled rest
periods**, and any amendments — and must **notify the competent administrative
authority within 7 days** of implementation.

> ⚠️ **Art. 122 is the one to check on a site visit.** A schedule that exists on
> paper and is not posted is a breach on its own terms, separate from any hours
> breach.

**Art. 123 — who is exempt.** Arts. **117, 118, 119 and the second paragraph of
120** do **not** apply to:
- **authorised representatives of the employer**
- employees engaged in **preparatory or supplementary tasks** performed before or
  after regular working hours
- employees assigned to **security or cleaning duties**

⚠️ "Managers are exempt" is a popular simplification and it is wrong as stated —
the category is *authorised representatives*, and the other two limbs are narrower
than employers usually assume. Check the role against the actual wording.

**Art. 124 — annual leave**, paid, excluding official holidays and weekly rest days:

| Service | Leave |
|---|---|
| First year of service | **15 days** |
| From the second year | **21 days** |
| **10 full years** with one or more employers, **or over 50 years of age** | **30 days** |
| 🟡 Persons with disabilities — *translation truncated in the retrieved text* | **45 days** |

**Art. 125 — you cannot bank it.** The employer determines the timing; leave may not
be interrupted except for compelling reasons required by the interest of work.
⚠️ **Employees may not waive their right to annual leave** and are obliged to take
it. If the employee refuses **in writing**, the consequences differ — 🟡 the
retrieved text truncates here; check the primary source before relying on it.

**Art. 126 — exam leave.** An employee scheduled to sit an exam at any stage of
education may **determine the timing of their own annual leave**, on **at least 15
days' notice**, and is entitled to **paid exam leave**.

**Apprentices (Art. 31)** — the provisions on vacations, working hours and rest
periods apply to trainees as well.

**Maternity (Art. 54)** — **4 months**, including before and after delivery, with
**post-delivery leave not less than 45 days**, **paid**, and available **no more than
three times** during the employment.

**Breastfeeding (Art. 56)** — during the **two years** following delivery, in
addition to the regular rest period, **two additional breaks of not less than half
an hour each**, which may be combined. They **count as working hours** and there is
**no reduction in pay**.

**Children (Art. 65)** — not more than **6 hours per day**, with breaks totalling at
least **one hour** scheduled so the child works no more than **4 consecutive
hours**, **no overtime**, and **not on weekly rest days or public holidays**.

## Foreign workers — Arts. 69–74

**Art. 70** — employment of foreigners is governed by this chapter, **with due regard
to the principle of reciprocity**. The Minister sets the maximum percentage of
foreigners, the exceptions, and the professions and trades they may **not** enter,
and may exempt them from the reciprocity condition.

**Art. 71** — a foreigner **may not work without a work permit** from the relevant
ministry and authorisation to enter and reside for employment. Employers may not
employ them without one. ⚠️ **Fees: not less than EGP 5,000 and not more than
EGP 150,000.** Where a foreigner is used under an exemption, the employer must
**notify the administrative authority within 7 days** of the foreigner starting work
and again when the employment ends.

**Art. 72** — the employer must notify the authorities if a foreign worker has been
absent **15 consecutive days** without legal excuse.

**Art. 73** — work permits for foreigners may be granted under **Investment Law
No. 72 of 2017**, in coordination with the investment minister.

**Art. 74** — ⚠️ **the employer must return the foreign worker to their home country
at the employer's own expense** when the employment ends, **unless the employment
contract specifies otherwise**. This is routinely missed in expat hiring, because
employers assume the worker handles their own repatriation.

## Periodic allowance — Art. 12

Workers covered by the law are entitled to an **annual periodic allowance, not less
than 3% of the insurance wage**, payable at the due date. It becomes due **after
one year** from the date of hiring, or after the previous allowance's entitlement.

## Social insurance

**Law No. 148 of 2019** (قانون التأمينات الاجتماعية) — four insured categories,
four branches, employer contribution **12%** and employee **9%**, with an
**employment injury branch at 1%** and long-term pensions capped at **26%**.
Administered by **NOSI** (National Organisation for Social Insurance,
`www.nosi.gov.eg`).

🟡 ⚠️ Transition from Law 79/1975 is **by category of insured person and progressive
inclusion**, not by a single date. Do not tell an employer which law applies to
their existing staff without checking. The social insurance number is **not
confirmed** to equal the national ID — query it via NOSI using the national ID.

## Women, children, and new work models

**Women (Arts. 53–60)** — all employment provisions apply **without
discrimination**, with **equal pay for work of equal value** (Art. 5). The
Minister, after consulting the National Council for Women and the National Council
for Childhood and Motherhood, decides **situations or jobs in which women may not be
employed** (maternity protection, occupational health and safety).

**Children (Arts. 61–68)** — **minimum age 15 to work, 14 to train**; **6 hours per
day**; **no work between 7 pm and 7 am**.

**New work models (Arts. 96–99)** — a genuinely new category in Law 14/2025:
**remote work, part-time, flexible work, job-sharing**, and any other form set by
ministerial decision. Traditional labour provisions apply, and such workers have
**the same rights** — social protection, social security, minimum wages, vocational
training, collective bargaining and freedom of association under the Trade Unions
Law 213/2017. **Art. 99** — the relationship must be in a **written contract, paper
or electronic**.

## Unlawful dismissal and reinstatement risk

**Arts. 145–147** — provisional suspension by written decision for **up to 60 days
with full pay** in three cases: referral for investigation where the
investigation's interests require it; accusation of a **felony or a misdemeanour
involving dishonesty, breach of trust, public morality or any other crime committed
in the workplace**; or the employer asks the labour court to dismiss the worker.
Appeal to the **summary judge within 3 days**; the judge rules **by the following
day**. Extension on application at least 10 days before expiry, at half pay.

⚠️ **If charges are dropped, a dismissal order is issued, or the worker is
acquitted, the worker must be reinstated with all withheld wages — refusal is
unlawful dismissal.**

That is why suspending someone pending investigation is not a free action. The
suspension itself carries the risk.
