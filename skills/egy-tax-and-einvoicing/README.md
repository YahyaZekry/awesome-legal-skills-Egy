Egyptian tax and e-invoicing — income tax and the tax card, VAT registration under VAT Law 67/2016, the ETA e-invoicing and e-receipt platform and its integration SDK, what invoices must carry, revenue stamp, and records that survive an audit.

Deliberately **carries no rates**. Current VAT and income-tax rates, registration thresholds and penalties are marked UNVERIFIED and the skill says so plainly — a rate quoted from memory in a tax document is worse than a gap. What it does carry are the structural rules that are verified:

- **Income tax and VAT are separate registrations.** Registering for one is not registering for the other, and businesses routinely assume otherwise
- ⚠️ **From 1 July 2023, expenses in the tax return are accepted only via the electronic invoice.** This is not a future obligation — it is the current rule, and it is quietly reducing what businesses can deduct. Integration means the ETA **SDK**, an **eSeal X.509 certificate**, and a system that produces the prescribed format
- **Art. 11** — a defective invoice does not invalidate the sale, it lets the customer **prove everything by any means**. You lose the evidence, not the sale
- **Art. 7** — prices must be **VAT-inclusive**, not "from EGP X + VAT"

Also separates three things a naive translation merges into one: **دمغة / ضريبة الدمغة** is a tax paid at the bank, **الختم البريدي** is the postal stamp, and **الختم الإلكتروني** is the electronic seal. And flags that **مأمورية الضرائب** (your local tax district office) and **الشباك** (the counter) have no clean English equivalent — write the Arabic and keep it.
