Navigate Egyptian courts — which court has which case, pecuniary and subject-matter limits, judicial fees, filing and service, deadlines, and execution and enforcement.

Corrects the fee thresholds that material written before 2024 gets wrong: **Law 13/1968 Arts. 42 and 47 were amended twice** (Laws 191/2020, 157/2024), so District Court first instance is now **≤ EGP 40,000** with finality at **≤ EGP 5,000**, and Court of First Instance finality at **≤ EGP 100,000**.

Flags two traps with real consequences. **Court of Appeal tariff changes of 2024–25 are administrative, not legislative**, and are **under challenge in ECESR Case 51843/79** — quoting one as statutory puts a challengeable number in a filing. And **"3/1972", "3/1973" and "17/1973"** in Egyptian fee discussions are **Bahraini and Kuwaiti**, not Egyptian.

Also corrects a persistent online error: **Law 172/2018 is not an electronic-litigation law — it amends the Customs Law 66/1963**. There is no verified e-litigation statute in the corpus, and the skill says so rather than inventing one.

Carries the deadline mechanics that lose cases: **no service before 7 a.m., after 8 p.m., or on an official holiday** (Art. 15); the **Art. 18** holiday extension where "working day" means the court's actual calendar, not the weekday; and **Art. 387** — the court may **not** apply prescription of its own motion, so it must be pleaded by the defendant.

Also notes **there is no "Supreme Labour Court"** — labour cassation runs through specialised circuits inside the Court of Cassation, and a screening-circuit refusal is unappealable.
