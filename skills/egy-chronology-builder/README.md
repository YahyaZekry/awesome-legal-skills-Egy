Build a litigation chronology for an Egyptian matter from the documents themselves — every entry attributed to the source document it came from, with a professional-secrecy screen, case-theory significance tagging, and the deadlines computed rather than narrated.

The rule that governs everything: **every entry cites the document it came from, by name and page. An entry you cannot attribute is an entry you cannot use.** Where two documents give different dates for the same event, both are recorded — the discrepancy is itself a finding, and often the most useful thing in the file.

Three Egyptian date problems break chronologies silently, in that the output looks fine:

- **Arabic-Indic numerals and day/month ordering.** `١٥/٣/٢٠٢٥` is 15 March in Egyptian practice, not May 3rd — but bilingual documents in the same file sometimes disagree. The rule is that a bare `x/y/zzzz` never enters the chronology: every date is written with the **month spelled out**, with the source's original string kept beside it.
- **Hijri dates sit alongside Gregorian on official documents,** often on a different line. A worked example from this project: a Cabinet decision of **30 July 2026** recorded itself as **16 Safar 1448 H**. Both are recorded, and the pair is checked — a mismatch is a transcription error, and the instrument is right.
- **⚠️ Some dates are not provable at all.** The distinctive finding, and the part people skip. Under **Law 25/1968 (Proof Law) Art. 15** an informal instrument is evidence of its *date* against third parties only once it has a **fixed date** — registration, proof in another fixed-date instrument, public-officer endorsement, or a signatory's death or incapacity. And under **Law 114/1946 Art. 12 bis**, for instruments that *must* be registered, **the date cannot be proved at all**. So the chronology carries a **provability mark per entry**, not just a date.

The cheap fix it flags: notarising a document gives it **the force of an executive instrument (قوة السند التنفيذي)** under **Law 4/1996 Art. 2 ¶2** (added by Law 137/2006) *and* fixes its date. It is the single cheapest risk mitigation available and it is routinely skipped.

⚠️ **There is no Egyptian equivalent of the English CPR 31.22 implied undertaking, and this skill deliberately does not import one.** The English rule attaches to a compulsory disclosure regime Egypt does not have. The Egyptian concern is **professional secrecy (سرية المهنة)** under **Advocacy Law 17/1983 as amended by Law 147 of 2019** — which is *not* absolute, since in the AML context a professional-secrecy claim asserted by lawyers or other intermediaries is expressly not accepted as grounds for withholding identification data. The file also warns that the Bar's 2024–25 "new Advocacy Law" was **not enacted**, so citing it is wrong.

Carries the deadline rules, because a chronology is where limitation periods get computed: **Civil Code Arts. 383–384** (interruption by formal notice or acknowledgment), **Art. 387** (the court may **not** apply prescription of its own motion), **Art. 378** (one-year wage limitation), and **Law 13/1968 Art. 18** — official holidays extend the deadline, with service itself barred before 7 a.m., after 8 p.m. or on a holiday under **Art. 15**. Every deadline is computed twice, forward and backward, against the holiday calendar at both endpoints.
