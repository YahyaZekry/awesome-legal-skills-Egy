# Copy checks — advertising and claims under Art. 9

⚠️ **Art. 9 lists eleven elements.** Deceptive conduct is assessed against them.
This is a checklist for claims, not a substitute for the article.

If a claim touches one of these, it must be true and supportable **on the date it
is made**, by whatever evidence you hold.

---

## The eleven elements

No supplier or advertiser may engage in deceptive conduct as regards:

| # | Element | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Nature, essential characteristics, constituent elements or quantity** of the goods | "100% pure", "handmade", "full grain leather", weight and count claims, component materials |
| **2** | **Source, weight, volume, method of manufacture, production or expiry date, conditions of use or precautions** | "Made in Egypt" / "imported", net weight vs gross, manufacturing method, **expiry**, use conditions, warnings |
| **3** | **Product characteristics and expected results of use** | "removes wrinkles", "lasts 10 years", "treats acne" — outcome claims need evidence |
| **4** | **Price or method of payment**, including surcharges and **VAT** | price display, VAT-inclusive or not, delivery and card fees, instalment cost |
| **5** | **Place of production or service provision** | where the good is made, where the service is performed |
| **6** | **Type of service, place of provision, precautions of use, essential characteristics and benefits** | service description, where it happens, how to use it safely |
| **7** | **Contracting conditions and procedures, after-sales service and warranty** | returns policy, who pays shipping, warranty duration and coverage |
| **8** | **Awards, certificates and quality marks obtained** | only if actually held, and not expired |
| **9** | **Trade marks, data or logos** | don't use another's; don't imply affiliation |
| **10** | **Existence of price reductions contrary to reality** | ⚠️ fake "was" prices — see below |
| **11** | **Quantities available** | "only 3 left" when there are 300 |

---

## The three that get sellers in trouble

### Fake price reductions — element 10

⚠️ This is specific and heavily enforced. A crossed-out "was" price must be a
price the product **genuinely sold at**. Setting a permanent inflated "RRP" and
discounting from it is deceptive conduct under element 10.

**Before you run a promotion, check:** did this product actually sell at that price,
for how long, and to whom? Keep the price history. If you cannot evidence it, do
not use the struck-through figure.

### Outcome claims — element 3

"Removes wrinkles in one use", "treats acne", "clinically proven", "cures",
"permanent", "guaranteed results" — each is a claim of **expected results of use**
or a **certificate**. You need substantiation for each, held before publication.

⚠️ Watch the health boundary. Claims about health outcomes on a general consumer
product, combined with **Art. 19** (defect reporting) and **Arts. 70 and 72**
(penalties up to **life imprisonment where death results**), are a different risk
class from ordinary retail copy. Flag anything health-related to a lawyer rather
than editing it yourself.

### Warranty and service descriptions — element 7

If you advertise a warranty, you are describing an **obligation**, and the
statutory minimum applies regardless of what you wrote:

- **Art. 22** — durable goods: **at least 2 years**
- **Art. 24** — same defect **more than twice in the first year** → replace new or
  refund
- **Art. 26** — finishing, maintenance and home-installation services: **1 year**,
  **3 years** if fraud or gross negligence

Advertising a 3-month warranty on a durable good is not a commercial choice. It is
a breach of **Art. 22**, and it is also a deceptive claim under element 7.

## Prohibitions beyond the eleven

- **Art. 13** — importing, producing, trading in or advertising products in a manner
  that **discriminates between citizens, disparages them, or breaches public order
  or public morals**. ⚠️ Note **Art. 76**: proceedings for an Art. 13 offence may be
  initiated **only on the written request of the Prime Minister or delegate**.
- **Art. 14** — a competition may not be advertised until the **CPA is notified at
  least 3 days beforehand**. The CPA may stop a competition and its advertising if
  it is not serious, is deceptive, or violates Art. 13.
- **Art. 15** — advertising or contracting for the sale of **real-estate units or
  land for building** requires a building permit under Building Law **119/2008**.
  And any contract term requiring the buyer or their successor to pay the seller a
  percentage, fee or commission in connection with dealings with the unit is
  **void**.
- **Art. 16** — **prohibited to solicit at residential units** to display or sell
  products without the consumer's prior and express request.

---

## Competitions — the mechanics people miss

- **3 days' notice** to the CPA before advertising (Art. 14)
- The terms must be real and complete — a competition that is not serious, is
  deceptive, or breaches Art. 13 can be stopped
- The CPA may require correction **within 3 days**, then **suspend the advertisement
  for up to 7 days** (Art. 57)
- Referral chain: CPA → Public Prosecution → **Misdemean Appeal Court**, which
  decides within **72 hours**

## What a review should output

For each claim flagged:

1. **The claim**, quoted
2. **Which element** of Art. 9 it engages
3. **What substantiation you currently hold**
4. **Fix** — remove it, qualify it, or evidence it
5. **If it is health-related** — escalate, do not edit

Do not rewrite the copy and present it as compliant. Substantiation is the
obligation; the wording is only how you discharge it.
