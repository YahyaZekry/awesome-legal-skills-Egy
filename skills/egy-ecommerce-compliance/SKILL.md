# E-commerce compliance in Egypt

Audit and fix a shop or site selling to Egyptian consumers.

**Not legal advice.** A structured starting point for a lawyer or for the business
to act on. Say so once.

**Not a substitute for reading the law.** This skill's job is to tell you *which*
obligation bites, not to replace the text. Every citation traces to
`research/egyptian-corpus/02-data-protection-privacy-consumer.md`.

## The statute that runs the whole thing

**Consumer Protection Law No. 181 of 2018** (قانون رقم ١٨١ لسنة ٢٠١٨ لحماية
المستهلك) — promulgated 13 September 2018, Gazette issue **37 bis**, in force
**14 December 2018**. As amended by **Law 20 of 2024**.
**Executive Regulations:** PM Decree **822 of 2019**, Gazette **13 bis (A)**,
1 April 2019, 47 articles.

A **distance contract** (التعاقد عن بعد) is defined in **Art. 1** broadly:
displaying, selling or purchasing products **using the internet or any visual,
audible or readable means of communication, or by telephone or any other means**.
If you take an order by phone, WhatsApp, or DM, you are in scope. This is wider
than most sellers assume.

## Rule 1 — everything must be in Arabic

⚠️ **Art. 5. This is the single most-missed rule by foreign sellers in Egypt, and
it is the first thing an inspector checks.**

All **advertisements, data, information, documents, invoices, receipts and
contracts — including electronic records and electronic documents** — must be:

- **in Arabic**, in **clear, legible script**;
- bearing data identifying the supplier, in particular its **address, contact
  methods, commercial-register registration data and its trade licence** if any.

They **may** be in two or more languages **provided one of them is Arabic**.

> An English-only site with English-only invoices and an English-only checkout is
> non-compliant on every page of the funnel. A bilingual site where the Arabic is
> an afterthought — auto-translated, small, or below the fold — does not satisfy
> "clear, legible". Add `cpa.gov.eg` compliance from the start; retrofitting
> Arabic into a checkout flow is expensive.

## Rule 2 — eleven disclosures before the customer can buy

⚠️ **Art. 37.** Before concluding a distance contract you must give the consumer,
**clearly and explicitly**, the information needed for an informed decision:

1. **Supplier data** — name, address, telephone, email (if any), **commercial-register
   number**, **tax card**, whether the supplier is legally organised, its
   professional capacity, the name of the registering professional body, and any
   information identifying the manufacturer or importer
2. **Product data** — source, specifications and essential characteristics,
   **how to use it**, and **any risks arising from such use**
3. **Price** and every sum added to it — **fees, taxes and shipping costs**
4. **Duration of the offer**
5. **The warranty** offered
6. **After-contract services**, if any
7. For services supplied on a recurring basis — **the duration**
8. **Delivery date and place**, and charges payable on delivery
9. **The withdrawal terms — in particular the period within which the consumer may
   withdraw**
10. **Maintenance-centre data** and how maintenance is arranged, for the goods the
    Law specifies
11. **The contracting data that will be sent to the consumer** if the transaction
    completes

**Art. 38 — the correction window.** Where the consumer accepts, acceptance **must
be confirmed**, and the consumer may **correct or amend their order within 7 working
days** of acceptance (longer if the parties agree). You must send a **written
notification immediately on contracting** containing the Art. 37 data and the other
contract terms — **which may be by email or any storable electronic medium** — and
it **may not contain data inconsistent with your offer**.

> The 7-working-day correction window is an obligation, not a courtesy. A checkout
> that confirms payment instantly with no correction step is short.

## Rule 3 — returns, refunds and withdrawal

**In person (Art. 17)** — the consumer may **exchange or return and recover the cash
value, without giving reasons and without bearing any expenses, within 14 days of
receipt**, without prejudice to a better legal or contractual guarantee. The
Consumer Protection Agency may set a **shorter** period for goods by nature.

**Five exceptions (Art. 17):**
1. the nature, characteristics or packaging make exchange or return impossible
2. perishable or rapidly deteriorating consumables
3. goods not in the condition they were in at sale, for a consumer-attributable reason
4. goods **manufactured to the consumer's own specifications** and conforming to them
5. **books, newspapers, magazines, computer programs and the like**

**Online (Art. 40) — distance contracts.** The consumer may withdraw **within 14 days
of receiving the goods**.

- Refund **by the same payment method** (unless otherwise agreed) **within 7 days**
  of return of the goods, or of the contracting date for services.
- **The consumer bears shipping and return costs unless the contract provides
  otherwise.**
- **If you delay delivery** beyond the agreed date, or fail to deliver **within 30
  days** where no date was agreed, the consumer may withdraw **within 14 days of the
  delay or 30 days of receipt, whichever is longer** — and then you must refund
  **immediately** and bear **all return-shipping and delivery costs**.

> ⚠️ That last paragraph is the expensive one. **Your own late delivery converts a
> consumer cost into your cost**, removes the cap on how long they can wait, and
> forces immediate refund. Build a delivery SLA into the process or absorb the risk.

**When the withdrawal right lapses (Art. 41):**
1. the consumer has **fully enjoyed the service** before the deadline
2. goods **made to the consumer's specifications**
3. **software, information programs or printed matter whose seal the consumer
   removed** ← if you sell digital products, your seal policy is the whole ballgame
4. video tapes, records, CDs or similar where the consumer removed the seal
5. a defect resulted from the consumer's **poor custody**
6. the request conflicts with the nature of the product, contravenes commercial
   custom, or is abusive in exercising the right

**Out of scope (Art. 36):** the distance-contract chapter does **not** apply to
banking and financial services and capital-market-trading services, or to
subscriptions to newspapers and periodicals. Regulations 822/2019 Art. 24 **adds
air/rail transport booking and hotel booking**. So: selling a flight or a hotel
room online is outside these rules; selling anything else usually is not.

**Defective or non-conforming goods (Art. 21):** **30 days from receipt** to exchange
or return with refund, **at no additional cost**.

## Rule 4 — your contract terms are already void if you tried

⚠️ **Art. 28 — the consumer's shield.** **Any condition in a contract, document or
instrument relating to a consumer contract is void if its effect is to reduce any of
the supplier's obligations under the Law or its Executive Regulations, or to
exonerate the supplier from them.**

> Every "by placing an order you waive your statutory rights" clause, every
> "no refunds under any circumstances", every "we are not liable for delivery
> delays" — **void**. You cannot contract out of this statute. Do not waste paper
> on disclaimer clauses; they create a false impression of protection.

## Rule 5 — invoices, prices, advertising

- **Art. 4** — inform the consumer of **all essential data**, in particular
  **source, price, specifications and basic characteristics**.
- **Art. 7** — prices must be displayed clearly and **inclusive of legally imposed
  taxes and other financial charges**. So: VAT-inclusive pricing, not "from EGP X
  + VAT".
- **Art. 10** — give an **invoice** containing in particular the **tax registration
  number**, transaction date, price, specifications, nature, type and quantity.
- **Art. 11** — ⚠️ **if the invoice is not issued as required, the consumer may prove
  the product's specifications and all other contract elements by any means of
  proof.** A missing invoice does not lose you the sale; it loses you the evidence.
  It hands the customer the argument.
- **Art. 9** — **eleven listed elements** of deceptive conduct, covering nature and
  characteristics, source and expiry, expected results, **price or method of payment
  including surcharges and VAT**, place of production or service, type of service
  and precautions, **contracting conditions, after-sales service and warranty**,
  awards and certificates, trade marks and logos, **existence of price reductions
  contrary to reality**, and **quantities available**.
- **Art. 14** — a competition may not be advertised until the **CPA is notified at
  least 3 days before**.
- **Art. 15** — selling real-estate units or land for building requires a building
  permit under Building Law 119/2008; and any term requiring the buyer to pay the
  seller a percentage or commission on dealings with the unit is **void**.
- **Art. 16** — **prohibited to solicit at residential units** to display or sell
  products without the consumer's prior and express request.

**The ad-stop procedure (Art. 57)** — the CPA may require correction **within 3
days**; if refused, or if the advertisement is harmful, it may **suspend the
advertisement for up to 7 days** and refer it to Public Prosecution and on to the
**Misdemean Appeal Court**, which decides within **72 hours**.

## Rule 6 — warranty

- **Art. 22 — durable goods (السلع المعمرة)** carry a warranty against
  manufacturing defects of **at least 2 years** from receipt. Where the product needs
  your installation or operation, the warranty runs from **actual installation, capped
  at 2 months from receipt**, and you must issue a receipt stating the actual
  operation date. The durable-goods list is fixed by a PM decision.
- **Art. 23** — the warranty covers inspection, repair, **original spare parts**,
  technician travel costs, and transport between the customer's premises and the
  service centre and back, **including installation and operating costs**. You must
  tell the customer the periodic-maintenance intervals and issue a receipt of work
  done. If you fail to repair, you must **replace with a new item of the same type
  and specifications, or refund**.
- **Art. 24 — the repeated-defect rule.** If the **same defect occurs more than twice
  within the first year** and materially affects functional quality, you must
  **replace with a new item of the same type and specifications at no cost, or
  refund**.
- **Art. 26** — suppliers of **finishing, maintenance and home-installation
  services** warrant soundness for **at least 1 year**, and **at least 3 years** if
  the defect is due to **fraud or gross negligence**, and must issue a receipt at
  contracting and before starting.
- **Art. 19 — defect reporting.** You must notify the CPA of a product defect and its
  potential harm **within a maximum of 7 days** of discovering it. Where it may harm
  **health or safety**, notify **immediately**, announce suspension of
  production/trading, **warn consumers**, repair/replace/refund at no cost, and
  **may not resume without the CPA's approval**.

## Rule 7 — the customer data you collect

Two layers apply.

**Consumer Law Art. 29 (under-cited, and it bites):** you must **preserve the
confidentiality and data of the consumer** and must not trade in or disclose them
**unless the consumer has expressly consented**. You must take all precautions to
preserve secrecy and privacy. Disclosure is constrained: on request of an official
authority or interested party you may be ordered to disclose consumer data **only
by the Public Prosecutor or their delegate (at least a Deputy/Assistant Public
Prosecutor)**, and **only where uncovering the truth in a crime or misdemeanour is
required and there are serious indications of its occurrence**.

**Data Protection Law No. 151 of 2020** (قانون حماية البيانات الشخصية) — broader,
and governs the processing itself. **Executive Regulations: MCIT Ministerial Decision
No. 816 of 2025**, signed by **Dr. Amr Talaat**, Gazette **issue 244 (Supplement A),
1 November 2025**, in force **2 November 2025**.

⚠️ **The compliance deadline is 1 November 2026.** The regime sat dormant for about
four and a half years; if you are processing customer data now, this is the window.

Things that will bite a shop:
- **Children's data is always sensitive data.** "In all cases, children's data are
  considered Sensitive Personal Data" (Attached Law Art. 1). No age gate
  gymnastics — this is categorical.
- **Electronic marketing needs a Licence or Permit** from the **Personal Data
  Protection Center** — note the Center, not an "Authority".
  ✅ **Checked 10 September 2026: the Center has published no launch announcement
  for its licensing portal and its public site carries no application route.** What
  it *has* published is the Gazette texts of the Law and the Regulations, **ten
  guidelines all dated 26 January 2026**, templates and a glossary, including a
  **Licences and Permits Guideline** that describes the portal and a **Company
  Journey questionnaire** for working out which licence a data user needs.
  → **Do not tell a client the application route is open.** Tell them to finish
  data mapping and licence documentation now, and to check the Center's site for
  the launch before filing.
- **Sending a customer's data abroad** (a foreign host, a CRM, an analytics tool,
  a payment processor outside Egypt, a foreign parent company's system) is a
  **cross-border transfer** requiring a **Centre Licence or Permit**, plus the data
  subject's consent, plus protection no lower than in Egypt.
- **Public-place CCTV footage must not be transferred or made available outside
  Egypt** except for legally prescribed reasons (Regs Art. 31(1)(3)).
- The **CBE regime is largely carved out** of Law 151/2020 — banking and financial
  data follow CBE rules.

⚠️ **On cross-border transfers — the honest answer.** There is **no published list
of countries deemed adequate**, and **no SCC or BCR route**. Legal commentary
describes both as "potential" or "contemplated"; **neither appears in the text of
Decision 816/2025 or Law 151/2020**. Do not tell a client that standard contractual
clauses are an available transfer mechanism in Egypt. The only routes in the enacted
text are a Centre cross-border Licence/Permit, and the statutory exception grounds
— both of which require the data subject's express consent.

**Data subject rights** carry an administrative fee cap of **EGP 20,000**.

## Penalties — read these before you decide this is a formality

| Breach | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Most Arts. 3–7, 12, 14, 16, 18, 21, 35, 38, 40, 56 | **EGP 10,000 – 500,000** or **twice the product value**, whichever is greater |
| Arts. 10, 20, 22, 24, 25, 26, 29, 30, 31, 33 | **EGP 30,000 – 1,000,000** or twice product value |
| **Arts. 9** (deceptive conduct), 13, 15, 32, 34, 56 | **EGP 50,000 – 2,000,000** or twice product value |
| **Art. 19** (defect reporting) | **EGP 50,000 – 2,000,000** or the value of the goods. **If the breach endangers life or exposes to an addictive or incurable disease: imprisonment + EGP 100,000 – 2,000,000** |
| Causing **permanent disability or incurable disease** | **Imprisonment** + **EGP 100,000 – 1,000,000** |
| **Causing death** | ⚠️ **LIFE IMPRISONMENT** + EGP 200,000 – 2,000,000 |
| **Art. 71 — withholding strategic products** (hoarding, concealing, refusing to sell) | ⚠️ **As amended by Law 20/2024: imprisonment ≥ 1 year + fine EGP 150,000 – 3,000,000**, or the value of the goods, whichever is greater. **Recidivism: 2–5 years' imprisonment and the fine doubled**, plus confiscation and publication at the defendant's expense. *Raised from EGP 100,000–2,000,000; the imprisonment minimum was unchanged.* |
| **Recidivism** | **Both minimum and maximum doubled** |
| Manager of a non-compliant company | **Same penalties** if they knew and managerial default contributed; company **jointly liable** |
| Data protection offences (Law 151/2020) | Up to **EGP 5,000,000**; unauthorised sensitive-data processing or cross-border breach → **imprisonment ≥ 3 months + fine** |

**Art. 73** — the court **must** order refund of the product value to the consumer
wherever they are entitled to it and have not got it.
**Art. 75** — conviction must be published in a daily newspaper and online; the CPA
must publish detailed judgments for **three years**.

## The regulator — and it can order the refund

**Consumer Protection Agency** — **جهاز حماية المستهلك**, `cpa.gov.eg`. Public
legal personality, **subordinate to the Prime Minister**, HQ Cairo, branches in all
governorates.

- **Art. 52 — binding decisions.** On a dispute between consumer and supplier about
  applying the Law, or about exchange/refund rights, the matter is referred to the
  CPA for a **binding decision**. The CPA may order exchange or refund **at the
  price announced by the Central Bank of Egypt plus the prescribed return**, or
  refund at market value, in each case from the date the complaint was filed.
- **Art. 43** — the CPA receives and investigates all consumer complaints; the
  Regulations set response periods, **max 30 days by commodity group**.
- **Art. 58** — CPA decisions are **final**, appealable only before the
  **Administrative Court**.
- **Art. 56** — the CPA must require the offender to **rectify within a period set by
  the Board**; where consumer health or safety is at risk it may **suspend the
  service** or **seize the goods**.
- **Art. 59** — reconciliation is possible **unless injuries or deaths resulted**,
  on payment scaled between the minimum and one-third of the maximum fine before
  referral, and three times the minimum to half the maximum after.
- **Art. 51** — CPA staff have **judicial-enforcement-officer status**: they may
  inspect books and documents, **enter any premises used for selling or storing
  products**, and take and analyse samples.

> **An inspector can walk into your warehouse and take samples.** That is not
  theoretical enforcement.

## How to run the audit

Work `references/AUDIT-GRID.md` top to bottom and record status per item:
**Compliant / Not compliant / To correct / To verify**.

Order of work:
1. **Art. 5 Arabic** — every page, including invoices, emails, order confirmations
2. **Art. 37 disclosures** — on the product page and in checkout
3. **Art. 38** — confirmation and the 7-working-day correction window
4. **Art. 40 / 41** — the withdrawal policy, and whether your exceptions hold
5. **Art. 7** — VAT-inclusive pricing
6. **Art. 10 / 11** — invoice contents
7. **Art. 9** — claims in your copy against the eleven elements
8. **Warranty** — Arts. 22–24
9. **Data** — consent for marketing, children's data, cross-border tools
10. **Art. 28** — delete your waiver clauses

**If an item is unclear, mark it "To verify with a lawyer" rather than guessing.**
A wrong "Not compliant" costs a business an unnecessary rebuild; a wrong
"Compliant" costs a fine.
