# Residential leases in Egypt

**Not legal advice.** Eviction is the point at which this matters most, and it is
the point at which self-help destroys a landlord's position. Get an Egyptian lawyer
before you serve anything or change anything. Say so once, then use this.

## ⚠️ Read this first — the old regime is ending, and nobody has written it up

**Law No. 164 of 2025** (قانون الإيجار القديم) and **Law No. 165 of 2025** were
both promulgated **4 August 2025**, Official Gazette **issue 31 bis**, in force
**5 August 2025**.

If a lease, article, blog post, or agent working from anything written before
August 2025 tells you the old regime is permanent, it is out of date.

### What changed

| | |
|---|---|
| **Old-system residential leases** | **end after 7 years** from 5 Aug 2025 — **≈ 5 August 2032** — unless the parties agreed earlier termination |
| **Old-system non-residential, natural persons** | **end after 5 years** — **≈ 5 August 2030** |
| **Old-system non-residential, legal persons** | **≈ April 2027** (under Law 10/2022) |
| **Repeal** | **Laws 49/1977, 136/1981 and 6/1997 are repealed in full** on the day after seven years from entry into force — **≈ 5 August 2032** (Law 164/2025 Art. 9) |

→ **Anyone telling you an إيجار قديم lease is perpetual is wrong.** Every old-system
lease in Egypt now has a clock on it.

### Rent — the first mandatory index in Egypt

⚠️ **Law 164/2025 Art. 6.** The rent fixed under Arts. 4 and 5 **increases every
year, periodically, by 15%**.

> This is a **fixed percentage, not a CPI link**, and it is **not subject to
> contract**. An agreement fixing rent for the whole term does not stop it.

**Art. 4 — residential, from the first instalment falling due after the law took
effect:**

| Area classification | New legal rent |
|---|---|
| **Distinguished** (متميزة) | **20×** the existing legal rent, minimum **EGP 1,000/month** |
| **Medium** (متوسطة) | **10×**, minimum **EGP 400/month** |
| **Economic** (اقتصادية) | **10×**, minimum **EGP 250/month** |

**Interim:** until the Art. 3 inventory committees (لجان حصر) finish, the lessee
must pay **EGP 250 per month**; differences accrue and are then paid in monthly
instalments over the period for which they accrued.

**Art. 5** — non-residential lets to **natural persons**: **5×** the existing legal rent.

**Art. 3** — the classification is made by **inventory committees in each governorate
appointed by the competent governor**, using location and street; building standard,
materials and average unit sizes; connected utilities; road network and services; and
the **annual rental value under Law 196/2008** (real-estate tax). The governor's
decision is published in the Gazette.

## Which regime — the first question, always

| | **إيجار جديد** (new-system) | **إيجار قديم** (old-system) |
|---|---|---|
| Governed by | **Civil Code Arts. 558–634** + **Law 4/1996** | **Laws 49/1977, 136/1981, 6/1997** — until ≈ Aug 2032 |
| Applies to | leases from **31 January 1996** | carried over from the pre-1996 regime |
| Rent | **moves only by contract** (Art. 147(1) — العقد شريعة المتعاقدين) | **20×/10×/5× + 15% annually by statute** |
| Deposit | **no statutory cap** — party autonomy | **capped at 2 months' rent** (Law 49/1977 Art. 25) |
| Key money | lawful under Art. 147(1) | ❌ **absolutely banned** — Law 49/1977 Art. 26 |
| End date | the contract term | fixed by statute: 2030 / 2032 / 2027 |

⚠️ **Do not translate إيجار قديم / إيجار جديد as "old" and "new" in a document.**
They are regime names. Say **"pre-1996 regime lease"** and **"current Civil Code
regime lease"**, and keep the Arabic.

⚠️ **Do not claim Law 4/1996 carved furnished property out of scope.** It did the
**opposite**: Art. 2 applies the Civil Code to places *"خالية **أو مفروشة**"* —
**unfurnished or furnished**. The special *إيجار مفروش* regime (Law 49/1977 Arts.
39–48) applied to **old-system** furnished leases only.

## The provision that catches almost every landlord

⚠️⚠️ **Civil Code Art. 598** — the lease **ends automatically at the end of the
term. No notice is required for it to end.**

⚠️ **Arts. 599(1) and 600** — and here is the trap: **if the lessee stays on and the
lessor serves no notice, the fixed term converts into an indefinite periodic
lease.**

> **Always serve notice at term end.** Serving is what converts "the lease ended" into
> a position the landlord can actually act on. Not serving does not preserve your
> position — it converts your fixed lease into an open-ended one.

⚠️ **Art. 587** — **payment presumes prior instalments have been paid.** If the
lessee pays a later instalment without objecting, that is treated as acceptance that
earlier ones are settled. A landlord who lets arrears run and then accepts a partial
payment has partly waived the argument.

## Money — deposit and key money

**Old-system only.**

**Law 49/1977 Art. 25 — deposit.** Capped at **two months' rent**, and the lessee
may **recover the excess by direct set-off against rent without going to court**.

**Law 49/1977 Art. 26 — key money (مقدّم / بكامان).** ❌ A landlord — owner, head
lessee or agent — **may not demand any consideration, fee or extra payment outside
the deposit and the rent.**

> **The contract is void, and there are criminal sanctions under Art. 77:**
> imprisonment of **not less than 3 months, or a fine equal to double**, with an
> amnesty for the lessee or agent who reports it, and **mandatory restitution**.

This is not a civil formality. It is a criminal exposure with a reporting amnesty
attached, and it is the single most commonly demanded payment in Egyptian
residential letting.

## Rent disputes under an old-system lease

**There is no rent control in the Civil Code.** All 77 articles of the lease chapter
(Arts. 558–634) were read to confirm this — the words for increase and indexation
appear only in Arts. 582–584, and there about the *lessee's* tenant-type repairs.

- **إيجار جديد** — rent moves **only by contract**.
- **إيجار قديم** — controlled, but the control was a **rent-fixing-committee
  formula on 1974 land values** (Law 49/1977 Arts. 14–15), now **replaced** by the
  20×/10×/5× plus 15% regime.

**Law 49/1977 Art. 28** — where the property is unfit, the tenant has a
**rent-reduction remedy**. This is a real answer to "the flat is broken and I am
paying full rent".

## Arrears — and the statutory cure

⚠️ **Law 136/1981 Art. 18** is a **closed list** of eviction grounds. **The expiry of
the agreed term is expressly NOT among them** — because a lease expiring is not an
eviction, it is the lease ending (Art. 598).

**Art. 18(ب)** — a lessee in arrears may **cure** — the corpus renders this as the
tenant being able to remedy arrears **worth more than five years** before the close
of pleadings.

⚠️ **That means a large arrears claim can be cured on the pleadings rather than
litigated.** Do not advise a landlord that a big arrears claim is safe.

**Law 49/1977 Art. 30** — an old-system lease **binds a new owner even where it has
no fixed date**. This is an exception to Civil Code Art. 604 and it defeats the
"but there is no written term" argument.

## Eviction — the routes

⚠️ **Never attempt self-help.** No changing locks, no cutting utilities, no removing
the tenant's property. 🟡 A tenancy-specific criminal provision for this was **not
found** and should not be asserted — but the civil consequences of turning someone
out unlawfully are severe, and the tenant's remedies under Law 49/1977 Art. 28 stand
in the way. Treat it as unlawful and do it anyway and you have lost the position you
were trying to win.

**Old-system, for cause — Law 136/1981 Art. 18.** A **closed list**. Use only the
grounds on it.

**Old-system, at the end of the sunset period — Law 164/2025 Art. 7.** Three
grounds, without prejudice to Art. 18:

1. at the end of the Art. 2 period;
2. the lessee has **left the property shut up for more than one year** without
   justification; or
3. the lessee **owns a residential or non-residential unit usable for the same
   purpose** as the leased one.

> ⚠️ **Ground 3 is new and aggressive.** "Owns another usable unit" became an
> eviction trigger in August 2025 and did not exist before. If a tenant has bought,
> this matters.

On refusal, the owner may apply to the **قاضي الأمور الوقتية** at the court in whose
district the property lies. ⚠️ **A substantive action does not suspend that order.**

**New-system, term expired — Law 165/2025, new Art. 2 bis of Law 4/1996.** The
lessor may apply to the same judge for an order ejecting the person refusing to
vacate, without prejudice to compensation.

⚠️⚠️ **Contested practice — flag this.** A number of first-instance tenancy divisions
read Arts. 7 and 2 bis as making the urgent-judge route the **only** route, and
dismiss substantive eviction actions as inadmissible. A **Suez Court of Appeal**
judgment (appeal No. 811 of year 48, reported June 2026) held the opposite — that
the urgent route is **additional and does not oust the parties' natural court**.

🟡 I did not read that judgment; only press reports. **The prudent sequence is to do
both** — file the substantive action **and** apply for the urgent order — so the
outcome does not depend on which view the court takes. **Get a lawyer on this
specifically.**

## Registration and notarisation — the gap that costs real money

**Law 114/1946 Art. 11** — registration is required for leases **exceeding 9 years**.
A lease over 9 years that is not registered **is not opposable beyond 9 years**.

⚠️⚠️ **Art. 12 bis — the expensive part.** For a registrable instrument that was not
registered, **the date of the instrument cannot be proved at all.**

→ If you sign a long lease and do not register it, you may be unable to prove when
it started. That defeats your own position.

**✅ The cheap fix — Law 137/2006.** Notarised leases were given **قوة السند التنفيذي**
— executive force. → **Get the lease notarised.** It is the highest-value thing you
can do for a long lease.

⚠️ The Code of Evidence is **Law 25 of 1968** (قانون الإثبات), amended by Laws
23/1992 and 18/1999 — **not** "68/1968" as several blogs have it. **Art. 15** fixes
the date for an informal instrument and is the article that matters for leases.

## Limitation — and the demand that does nothing

**Civil Code Art. 375** — rent claims prescribe after **5 years**.

⚠️ **Art. 387 — the court may NOT apply prescription of its own motion.** The
landlord must **plead the 5-year limit expressly**, and the tenant may plead it at
any stage including on appeal.

⚠️⚠️ **An informal demand does NOT interrupt prescription.** Only the **Art. 383**
events do — a judicial claim (**even before an incompetent court**), **formal notice
(التنبيه)**, attachment, a bankruptcy claim, or an enforcement act. **Art. 384** —
also by the debtor's acknowledgment, express or tacit.

> A WhatsApp message asking for rent, repeatedly, over years, **does not stop the
> clock**. Serve a real notice. See `skills/egy-legal-notice/`.

## Where to file

- **Civil Code Art. 50** — possession and in rem actions go to the court in whose
  district **the property lies**, not where the landlord lives.
- **Art. 43(6) and Art. 50** — **delivery of immovables is exclusively the District
  Court's.**
- **Art. 44** — ⚠️ **do not combine a possession claim with the claim to the right**
  in the same action.

## Drafting checklist — a new (إيجار جديد) lease

- [ ] Parties, with **national ID** and, for a company, **commercial-registry data**
- [ ] The **property**, identified precisely — and check the landlord's title at the
      **الشهر العقاري** (Real Estate Registration & Notarisation Authority), since
      **ownership is not complete until registered there**
- [ ] **Term**, stated precisely
- [ ] **Rent in EGP**, and how it moves — remember there is **no statutory cap** for
      an إيجار جديد, and for an إيجار قديم the **+15% annual** applies by statute
- [ ] **Deposit**, and ⚠️ **no key money** if the lease could be characterised as
      old-system — and consider it unlawful-adjacent regardless
- [ ] **Who pays utilities**, and maintenance
- [ ] **Repairs** vs tenant-type alterations (Arts. 582–584)
- [ ] **Permitted use**
- [ ] ⚠️ **Notice on expiry — and a commitment to serve it.** This is the provision
      that protects the landlord under Arts. 598–600
- [ ] **Termination for cause**, matching Law 136/1981 Art. 18's closed list if
      old-system
- [ ] **Registration** if over 9 years
- [ ] ⚠️ **Notarisation** — for executive force under Law 137/2006
- [ ] **Forum** — the court where the property lies
