## Citation traps found during research — read this first

Four of the citations in the original brief were **wrong**. A model trained on common summaries will reproduce these same errors:

| Brief said | Reality (verified) |
|---|---|
| Commercial Code = **Law 12/1943** | Egyptian Commercial Code = **Law 17 of 1999**. I could not find any Egyptian Commercial Code numbered 12/1943 at all. |
| Arbitration Law = **Law 34/1988** | Egyptian Arbitration Law = **Law 27 of 1994**. Law 34/1988 was not identified as an arbitration law in any source. |
| E-Signature Law = **Law 15/2020** | E-Signature Law = **Law 15 of 2004**. The 2020 instrument in this area is **Ministerial Decision 361/2020 amending the Executive Regulations** — a decision, not the statute. |
| Civil Code limitation articles in the 480s–500s | Limitation is **Articles 374–388**, under Book One / Book Five / Chapter Three. **Article 488 is about gifts (الهب)**, not prescription. Articles 488–490 are donations. |

Also note: the Egyptian Civil Code does **not** contain a single article enumerating "the four elements of the contract." Capacity sits in Chapter Two (الأشخاص, Art. 108 ff.), subject matter in Arts. 131–137, and "cause" in Arts. 136–137. A model asserting "Article 89 sets out the four essential elements" is wrong: Art. 89 only says a contract is concluded when two parties exchange matching expressions of will.

## 1. Civil Code (القانون المدني)

### Civil Code (القانون المدني)
- **Citation:** Law No. 131 of 1948, issued 16 July 1948 (9 Ramadan 1367 H), promulgated by the People's Assembly and signed at the Presidency.
- **Issuing authority:** People's Assembly (مجلس النواب) / Presidency of the Republic.
- **Publication:** الوقائع المصرية (Al-Waqa' al-Misriyya / the Egyptian Gazette), Issue No. 108 bis (A), issued 29 July 1948.
- **Entry into force:** 15 October 1949.
- **Status:** in force; heavily amended over 70+ years. ⚠️ **Amendment list not fully verified** — see Open Questions.

#### Verified key provisions (all article numbers read directly from the Arabic official text)

**General and interpretive**
- **Art. 1** — Legislative texts apply to all matters within their letter or spirit; failing a text, the judge decides by custom (العرف), then by principles of Islamic law (مبادئ الشريعة الإسلامية), then by natural law and equity. *This is the Egyptian "custom → Shari'a → equity" ladder. It is genuinely part of Egyptian statutory law, not just doctrine.*
- **Art. 2** — A text is repealed only by express repeal, inconsistency, or fresh regulation of the same subject.
- **Art. 3** — Time limits are computed on the **Gregorian** calendar (التقويم الميلادي) unless otherwise provided.
- **Arts. 7–8** — Transitional rules for limitation when a new text shortens the period.

**Things (الأشياء)**
- **Art. 81** — Everything capable of being an object of patrimonial rights is capable of being the subject of rights; some things are excluded by nature or by law.
- **Art. 82** — Immovable (عقار) / movable (منقول) division.
- **Art. 85** — Generic things (الأشياء المثلية): fungible, measured by count, measure, weight.
- **Art. 87** — **Public property (الأموال العامة).** State and public-law-personality property dedicated to public use, by fact or by law/decree/decision of the competent minister. Art. 87(2): such property **cannot be disposed of, attached, or acquired by prescription (لا يجوز التصرف فيها أو الحجز عليها أو تملكها بالتقادم)**.
- **Art. 88** — Public character is lost when the dedication ends.

**Capacity (الأهلية)**
- **Art. 109** — A person is capable of contracting unless deprived or restricted of capacity by law.
- **Arts. 110–112** — Minor / مقيد / مميز regime: a minor under 18 (Art. 112 sets the age) has void or merely-annulable acts depending on whether purely beneficial, purely prejudicial, or mixed.
- **Arts. 113–115** — Court-ordered interdiction (الحجر) for insanity, imbecility, prodigality (الغفلة), squandering (السفه). Art. 114: acts **after registration** of the interdiction order are void; acts before it are void only if the condition was notorious or the other party knew.

**Contract formation (العقد)**
- **Art. 89** — A contract is concluded **the moment** two parties exchange matching expressions of will (تعبير عن إرادتين متطابقتين), subject to any formal requirements the law imposes.
- **Art. 90** — Expression of will may be by word, writing, customary gesture, or conduct. It may be implied unless law or agreement requires it to be express.
- **Art. 91** — An expression takes effect when it comes to the addressee's knowledge; **receipt is presumed to be knowledge**, rebuttable.
- **Arts. 92–94** — Offer binds for a specified acceptance period (Art. 93); an unqualified offer made in a contract session lapses unless acceptance is immediate (Art. 94).
- **Art. 95** — **If the parties agree on all the essential matters (المسائل الجوهرية) and reserve only detail matters for later, without stipulating that the contract fails for want of agreement, the contract is deemed concluded.** The court then decides the reserved points by the nature of the transaction, the law, custom and equity. *This is the Egyptian answer to "are we bound yet?" — it is widely under-appreciated by foreign counsel.*
- **Art. 96** — Acceptance qualified, limited or modified is a rejection containing a new offer (counter-offer).
- **Art. 97** — Contract between absent persons is deemed concluded at the place and time the offeror knew of acceptance.
- **Art. 98** — Silence can be acceptance where the nature of the transaction, custom or circumstances show the offeror did not expect a reply; silence is also acceptance where there was a prior dealing, or the offer was for the addressee's benefit.
- **Art. 99** — In auctions, only bids with a deposit (رسوم المزاد) count; a higher bid even an invalid one displaces the lower one.
- **Art. 100** — **In adhesion contracts (عقود الإذعان) acceptance is confined to mere delivery on the offeror's pre-set terms, and is not open to discussion.**
- **Art. 101** — A promise to conclude a named future contract is not itself a contract unless all essential matters **and the time limit** are fixed; any legally required form for the future contract applies equally to the promise (Art. 101(2)).
- **Art. 102** — If a promisor defaults on a promise to contract, and all conditions (including form) are met, **the judgment of the court res judicata stands in place of the contract** (substitution du contrat par jugement). *A distinctively Egyptian enforcement device.*
- **Art. 103** — Payment of earnest money (العربون) on conclusion entitles either party to withdraw unless otherwise agreed; the payer forfeits it, the recipient must return it **double**.

**Defects of consent and subject matter**
- **Art. 119** — A person of limited capacity may seek annulment, without losing his duty to compensate if he used devices to conceal his incapacity.
- **Arts. 120–121** — Fundamental mistake (الغلط الجوهري): substantial enough that the party would not have contracted; *in particular* mistake about an essential quality of the thing or about a quality of the counterparty that was the main reason for contracting. A contracting party's mistake only entitles relief if the other party made the same mistake, knew it, or could easily have detected it (Art. 120).
- **Art. 122** — Mistake of law can make a contract annulable.
- **Art. 123** — Arithmetical and pen errors do not affect validity; they must be corrected.
- **Arts. 125–126** — Fraud (التدليس) permits annulment only if the deception was so serious that the party would not have contracted.
- **Arts. 127–128** — Duress (الإكراه) requires a genuine, unfounded, grave threat affecting person, honour, property or family; in assessing it, the victim's sex, age and social and health circumstances are taken into account (Art. 127(3)).
- **Arts. 129–130** — Extreme inequity (الغبن): where the obligations of one party bear no relation at all to what he received, and the deceived party contracted only because the other exploited **clear inexperience (طيشا بينا) or reckless passion (هوى جامحا)**, the court may annul the contract or reduce the other party's obligations. *Note: this is emphatically NOT a general "gross unfairness" clause — the exploitation element is mandatory.*
- **Art. 131** — The subject matter may be a future thing; a contract for the disposal of a living person's inheritance (تراث) is void even with consent, save in statutory cases.
- **Art. 132** — Intrinsically impossible subject matter ⇒ void contract.
- **Art. 133** — An unspecified subject matter must at least be determinable by kind and amount.
- **Art. 134** — Value changes before performance do not affect the obligation.
- **Art. 135** — Subject matter contrary to public order or morals ⇒ void.
- **Art. 136** — No cause, or an illicit cause ⇒ void.
- **Art. 137** — A cause not stated in the contract is presumed lawful; the stated cause is presumed to be the true cause, rebuttable by proof of simulation (الصورية).

**Nullity vs annulment — a real distinction**
- **Arts. 138–139** — Annulment rights belong only to the party favouring whom the law confers them; may be waived expressly or impliedly, and ratification relates back to the date of the contract (139(2)).
- **Art. 140** — **The right to seek annulment lapses if not exercised within three years**, running from removal of the incapacity, from discovery of the mistake/fraud, or from cessation of duress; and in no case may annulment for mistake, fraud or duress be claimed more than **fifteen years** after the contract was concluded.
- **Art. 141** — **A void contract (البطلان) may be invoked by any interested person and the court may raise it of its own motion; it is not cured by ratification. The nullity action is time-barred after fifteen years.**
- **Art. 142** — On annulment or nullity the parties are restored to their prior position; if that is impossible, equivalent compensation; but a minor whose contract is annulled for incapacity is not required to return benefits.
- **Arts. 143–144** — Partial nullity; where a void/annulable contract accumulates the requisites of another valid contract, it is treated as that other contract.

**Effect of contracts — the four rules foreign counsel most often miss**
- **Art. 145** — Contract effects run to the parties and to the universal successor (الخلف العام), i.e. the estate, subject to inheritance rules.
- **Art. 146** — Obligations and personal rights attached to a thing **pass to the special successor (الخلف الخاص), i.e. a purchaser, at the moment the thing passes, if they are among its incidents (مستلزماته) and the purchaser knew of them at the time.** *This is the Egyptian version of "binds successors in title" — it is both narrower and broader than English s.62 CA 1982 / French art. 1742 Code civil, and runs on a knowledge condition.*
- **Art. 147(1)** — **العقد شريعة المتعاقدين** — the contract is the law of the parties; it may be revoked or amended only by their agreement or for the reasons the law allows.
- **Art. 147(2)** — **THE HARDSHIP / SUPERVENING EVENT CLAUSE (نظرية الظروف الطارئة / théorie de l'imprévision).** If exceptional, unforeseeable general events occur which, without rendering performance impossible, make it excessively onerous for the debtor so as to threaten him with substantial loss, **the judge may, in the circumstances and after weighing the parties' interests, reduce the obligation to a reasonable limit**. Any agreement to the contrary is void. *This has no direct equivalent in English law and was substantially disused in Egyptian practice during the periods of high inflation and political upheaval — but it remains on the statute book. An agent that says "Egyptian law has no hardship clause, like English law" is wrong.*
- **Art. 148(1)** — **The contract must be performed according to its terms and in a manner consistent with good faith (وبطريقه تتفق مع ما يوجبه حسن النية).**
- **Art. 148(2)** — **The contract is not limited to what it states; it also embraces what is among its requisites (مستلزماته) according to law, custom and equity, according to the nature of the obligation.** *This is the Egyptian "implied terms" rule and it is the single most useful article for a contract-drafting agent.*
- **Art. 149** — **In an adhesion contract (عقد بالإذعان) containing oppressive terms (شروط تعسفية), the court may amend or delete those terms in accordance with justice. Any agreement to the contrary is void.**
- **Art. 150** — Clear wording is not to be departed from by construction; where construction is needed, the shared intention is sought, not the literal meaning.
- **Art. 151** — **Doubt is construed in favour of the debtor** (يفسر الشك في مصلحة المدين); but ambiguity in an adhesion contract may never be construed against the adhering party.
- **Art. 152** — A contract does not impose an obligation on a third party, but may confer a right on one.
- **Arts. 153–154** — Promise for the benefit of a third party, with substitution of the promisor if the third party refuses (153), and a promisor who undertakes in his own name for another's benefit acquires the right to enforce (154).

**Performance**
- **Art. 208** — In a work obligation (التزام بعمل) the creditor may refuse performance by a third party where the agreement or the nature of the duty requires personal performance.
- **Art. 209** — In a work obligation the creditor may seek a court licence to execute at the debtor's cost; **in urgent cases (في حالة الاستعجال) the creditor may execute without a court licence.**
- **Art. 210** — The judgment of the judge may substitute for performance where the nature of the obligation permits.
- **Art. 211** — For preservation/management duties, the debtor is discharged by the care of an ordinary person (عناية الرجل العادي), even if the purpose is not achieved — unless law or agreement says otherwise.
- **Art. 213** — Compulsory performance plus **غرامة تهديدية** (penalty threat) where personal performance is essential; the court may increase the penalty if it proves insufficient to compel.

**Damages and liability (التعويض)**
- **Art. 214** — Where specific performance happened or the debtor insisted on refusing, the judge fixes compensation having regard to the creditor's loss and the debtor's **obstruction (العنت)**.
- **Art. 215** — Where specific performance became impossible, the debtor is cast in damages unless he proves the impossibility arose from a cause beyond his control; the same result follows for **delay**.
- **Art. 216** — The judge may reduce or withhold compensation where the creditor contributed to or increased the loss.
- **Art. 217(1)** — The parties may agree that the debtor bears accident and force majeure.
- **Art. 217(2)** — **The parties may validly exclude the debtor's liability for non-performance, save for damage arising from fraud (الغش) or gross fault (الخطأ الجسيم); and even as to fraud/gross fault by persons the debtor uses in performance, the debtor may contractually disclaim that liability.** *This is the Egyptian exclusion-of-liability rule. Do not import the English "cannot exclude fraud" line without noting the second sentence — an agent may be able to contract out even of gross negligence by employees.*
- **Art. 221(1)** — Where compensation is not stipulated, the judge assesses it: loss suffered plus gain missed (ما لحق الدائن من خسارة وما فاته من ربح), provided it is the **natural consequence (نتيجة طبيعية)** of non-performance or delay. Damage is a natural consequence if the creditor could not reasonably have avoided it.
- **Art. 221(2)** — **For contractual liability, a debtor who has committed neither fraud nor gross fault is liable only for damage that could ordinarily be foreseen at the time of contracting (يمكن توقعه عادة وقت التعاقد).** *This is the remoteness rule and it is contract-specific.*
- **Art. 222** — Compensation includes **moral harm (الضرر الأدبي)**, but this is not transmissible to third parties unless stipulated or claimed judicially; Art. 222(2) restricts non-pecuniary damages for relatives beyond the second degree to pain caused by death.
- **Art. 223** — The parties may fix compensation **in advance** in the contract or by later agreement (a liquidated-damages clause); Arts. 215–220 apply to its assessment.
- **Art. 224** — Agreed compensation is not due if the debtor proves the creditor suffered no loss; the court may reduce it if grossly overstated or partly performed; agreements to the contrary are void.
- **Art. 225** — If loss exceeds the agreed compensation, the creditor cannot claim more **unless the debtor committed fraud or gross fault**.
- **Art. 226** — **Default interest: 4% p.a. in civil matters and 5% p.a. in commercial matters**, running from the date of judicial claim unless the agreement or commercial custom sets another date.
- **Art. 227(1)** — Parties may agree a different rate; any agreed rate **above the statutory price is reduced to 7%** with restitution of the excess.
- **Art. 232** — No interest on capitalised interest; total interest may never exceed the principal, save for the rules of commercial custom.
- **Art. 246** — **Right of retention (الحبس)**: a person obliged to deliver something may withhold it until the creditor tenders the connected counter-performance or provides adequate security.

**Creditor's actions against the debtor's dispositions — "عدم النفاذ"**
- **Art. 237** — ⚠️ **IMPORTANT: Art. 237 is NOT a damages provision.** It opens the creditor's action to have a disposition by the debtor declared **ineffective against the creditor (عدم نفاذ التصرف)** where the disposition reduced the debtor's assets or increased his liabilities and resulted in his insolvency or aggravation of insolvency.
- **Art. 238** — For a onerous disposition (تصرف بعوض), ineffectiveness requires the debtor's fraud plus knowledge by the transferee; disposing while knowingly insolvent suffices as fraud, and a transferee who knew of the insolvency counts as knowing of the fraud. For a gratuitous disposition (تصرف تبرع), ineffectiveness applies even against a bona fide transferee and even without fraud (238(2)).
- **Art. 239** — On the insolvency allegation the creditor proves the amount of debts; the debtor must prove assets equal or exceeding them.
- **Art. 243** — The ineffectiveness action is barred after **three years from the creditor's knowledge** of the ground, and in all cases after **fifteen years** from the disposition.
- **Art. 244** — In a sham contract (عقد صوري), good-faith creditors and the special successor may invoke either the ostensible or the concealed contract and prove simulation; where interests conflict, those relying on the ostensible contract prevail. **Art. 245**: if a true contract is concealed behind an ostensible one, the true contract is effective between the parties and the universal successor.

**Conditions and terms (الشرط والأجل)**
- **Art. 265** — An obligation is **suspensive** (معلقا على شرط) where its existence or extinction depends on a future, uncertain event.
- **Art. 266** — An obligation conditional on an impossible condition, or one contrary to morals or public order, does not exist — whether the condition is suspensive or resolutive; **Art. 266(2)** is an exception: a resolutive condition that itself contravenes morals or public order does not defeat the obligation if that condition is the motivating cause of the obligation.
- **Art. 267** — An obligation conditional on a suspensive condition making its existence depend solely on the promisor's will is treated as non-existent.
- **Art. 268** — A suspensively conditional obligation is not effective until the condition occurs; before then it is not subject to either compulsory or optional enforcement, but the creditor may take protective measures.
- **Art. 269** — A **resolutive** (فاسخ) condition, once satisfied, extinguishes the obligation and obliges the creditor to restore what he received; if restoration is impossible for a reason attributable to him he must compensate.
- **Art. 270** — **Conditions operate retroactively to the time the obligation arose (أثر رجعي)**, unless the parties' will or the nature of the contract shows otherwise; and no retroactivity applies if performance before the condition occurred became impossible for a cause beyond the debtor's control.
- **Art. 271** — A **term** (الأجل) is a certain future event; it is certain if occurrence is inevitable even if the time is unknown.
- **Art. 272** — An undertaking payable only "when able" gets a reasonable date fixed by the judge, judged by the debtor's present and future means and by the care of a prudent person.
- **Art. 273** — **The debtor's term lapses** on bankruptcy or insolvency declaration, and where his own act so weakens special security; but if the weakening is not attributable to him, the term does not lapse unless the creditor has called for top-up.
- **Art. 274** — With a **suspensive term** the obligation is not effective until expiry, but the creditor may take protective measures in the meantime — in particular he may demand security on reasonable grounds where he fears insolvency.
- **Art. 275** — Alternative obligation (الخيار) is the debtor's choice unless law or agreement provides otherwise.

**Extinction by performance**
- **Arts. 323–324** — Payment may be made by the debtor, his representative, or any interested third party (323); the interested payer has recourse against the debtor (324).
- **Art. 331** — **Where a possessor of mortgaged property pays the debt, the obligation passes to him with its rights and securities.**
- **Art. 334** — Unjustified refusal of a proper offer of performance constitutes valid **formal notice (إعذار)** from the recorded refusal.
- **Art. 335** — After valid notice, risk of destruction passes to the debtor, interest stops, and the debtor may deposit at his own cost and claim damages.
- **Art. 339** — **Real offer (العرض الحقيقي) plus deposit under the Code of Civil and Commercial Procedures counts as performance** if the creditor accepts it or a final judgment validates it.
- **Art. 343** — Where the debtor owes costs and interest in addition to principal, payments are applied to costs, then interest, then principal.
- **Art. 344** — Where there are several same-kind debts, the debtor may designate which to pay; failing designation, the Court applies to the debt least burdensome to the debtor, and between equally burdensome debts to the creditor's election (345).
- **Art. 346** — **Performance is due immediately when the obligation becomes finally due**, unless agreement or law provides otherwise; in exceptional cases the judge may grant the debtor a reasonable time if the law does not bar it and no substantial prejudice accrues to the creditor.
- **Art. 347** — Delivery in a specific thing at the place it was when the obligation arose; otherwise at the debtor's domicile at performance or at the place of his business if the obligation relates to it.
- **Arts. 371–373** — Extinction by discharge (الإبراء, no special form required — Art. 372), by impossibility (373, only where the cause is beyond the debtor's control).

**⚠️ PRESCRIPTION / TIME LIMITS — Articles 374 to 388**

The limitation chapter is **Chapter Three of Book Five of Book One (الباب الخامس - انقضاء الالتزام - الفصل الثالث - انقضاء الالتزام دون الوفاء به), Section 3, titled التقادم المسقط (dissolving prescription).**

- **Art. 374** — **General rule: obligations are prescribed after fifteen years**, save where the law provides otherwise and save for the stated exceptions.
- **Art. 375** — **Five years** for a recurring periodic debt admitted or acknowledged by the debtor, rent of buildings (أجرة المباني) and agricultural land (الأراضي الزراعية), the consideration for a right of pre-emption (الحكر), interest and revenues, legacies, pensions and gratuities. Art. 375(2): the share due a bad-faith possessor and the share payable by a wakf administrator to the beneficiaries are NOT extinguished before fifteen years.
- **Art. 376** — **Five years** for the claims of **doctors, pharmacists, lawyers, engineers, experts, bankruptcy trustees, brokers (سمسرة), teachers and tutors** (آلاء التفليسة), for work done in their profession and expenses incurred.
- **Art. 377** — **Three years** for taxes and duties due to the State — annual taxes and duties running from the end of the year for which they are due, and court fees from the close of the case in which they were incurred or from their drafting if no case was filed; **three years** also for recovery of taxes and duties paid without right, running from the day of payment (377(2)).
- **Art. 378** — **ONE YEAR** for: (a) claims of merchants and craftsmen in respect of goods delivered to non-merchants, and claims of hotel and restaurant owners for the price of lodging, food and disbursements made for their clients; (b) claims of workers, servants and day-labourers for daily and non-daily wages and for the value of supplies they made.
- **Art. 378(2)** — **Whoever pleads the one-year limitation must swear on oath that he actually paid the debt.** The judge directs this of his own motion and directs it to the minor's heirs or testamentary executors.
- **Art. 379** — For Arts. 376–378 claims, time runs from the creditor's presentation of his claim, even if he keeps presenting further claims; but **where a bond (سند) is executed for such a claim, the claim is prescribed only after fifteen years.**
- **Art. 380** — **Limitation periods are counted in days, not hours; the first day is not counted; the period is completed at the end of its last day.**
- **Art. 381** — In the absence of a specific rule, prescription runs **from the day the debt falls due**; and specifically: not against a debt subject to a suspensive condition until the condition occurs; not against a warranty of title (ضمان الاستحقاق) until the claim to the thing is established; not against a deferred debt until the term expires; and if the date of payment depends on the creditor's will, from the moment he can notify it.
- **Art. 382** — **Prescription does not run while an impediment exists making it difficult for the creditor to claim (even a purely moral impediment), nor between principal and agent.** Art. 382(2): a prescription longer than five years does not run against a person lacking capacity, an absentee, or a person convicted of a criminal offence who has no legal representative.
- **Art. 383** — **Interruption** occurs by a judicial claim (**even if brought before an incompetent court**), by formal notice (التنبيه), by attachment (الحجز), by a claim filed in bankruptcy or distribution proceedings, and by any act the creditor takes to enforce his right during the course of a suit.
- **Art. 384** — Prescription is also interrupted by the debtor's acknowledgment, express or tacit; leaving property in the creditor's hands as a possessory pledge (رهن حيازي) securing the debt counts as tacit acknowledgment.
- **Art. 385** — After interruption a **new prescription** starts from the end of the interrupting cause, of the same length; **except** that where a judgment on the debt has become res judicata, or where a one-year debt was interrupted by acknowledgment, the new period is **fifteen years** (subject to an exception for periodic debts not due until judgment).
- **Art. 386** — **Prescription extinguishes the obligation but leaves a natural obligation (التزام طبيعي) in the debtor's patrimony** — i.e. it is no longer enforceable judicially but not a release. Art. 386(2): when the right is prescribed, accessory rights such as interest are prescribed with it, even if their own periods have not run.
- **Art. 387** — ⚠️ **THE COURT MAY NOT APPLY PRESCRIPTION OF ITS OWN MOTION (لا يجوز للمحكمة أن تقضي بالتقادم من تلقاء نفسها). It must be pleaded, on the application of the debtor or of any person interested in the matter, even if the debtor does not plead it. It may be pleaded at any stage, including on appeal (before the Court of Appeal).**
- **Art. 388** — A person may not waive prescription before it has accrued, and **may not contract for a limitation period different from the statutory one** (though he may waive it, even tacitly, after accrual, provided no prejudice to creditors).

## 2. Commercial Code (قانون التجارة)

### Commercial Code (قانون التجارة)
- **Citation:** Law No. 17 of 1999 promulgating the Commercial Code (قانون رقم ١٧ لسنة ١٩٩٩ بإصدار قانون التجارة).
- **Issued / signed:** 17 May 1999 (5 Safar 1420 H), by the Presidency.
- **Publication:** الجريدة الرسمية (Official Gazette), Issue No. 19 bis, 17 May 1999.
- **Entry into force:** **1 October 1999.**
- **Status:** in force, amended. ✅ The law **repeals the Commercial Code of 13 November 1883**, *except* Chapter One of Book Two (partnerships), which survives.

#### Key provisions (verified)
- **Art. 1** — The Code applies to commercial activities and to every natural or legal person who has the status of a merchant.
- **Art. 2(1)** — ⚠️ **The Commercial Code is NOT lex specialis in the European sense.** It opens with a **priority ladder**: (a) the parties' agreement on the goods; (b) failing that, the Commercial Code or other commercial laws; (c) then commercial custom and commercial usage; (d) **failing all that, the Civil Code applies**.
- **Art. 2(2)** — Agreements between parties and commercial custom may **not** be applied where they conflict with public policy in Egypt (النظام العام في مصر).
- **Art. 3** — ⚠️ **Mixed contract rule (the most important article for a foreign lawyer): if a contract is commercial as regards one of the parties, the Commercial Code applies ONLY to that party's obligations; the other party's obligations are governed by the Civil Code**, unless the law provides otherwise.
- **Arts. 4–7** — Enumerated commercial acts (purchase of movables for resale, incorporation of commercial companies; supply of goods and services, industry, land and inland-water transport, commercial agency and brokerage, insurance, banking and exchange, warehousing, publishing/printing/photography/broadcasting/telecom/advertising, commercial exploitation of computer programmes and satellite broadcasting, extraction operations, public entertainment; Art. 6 maritime and air commerce; Art. 7 any act analogous to the above in characteristics and aims). **Art. 5 requires professional exercise (مزاولة على وجه الاحتراف) for the listed professions.**
- **Art. 8** — Acts by a merchant for his own commercial affairs are commercial acts; an act by a merchant is presumed to relate to his commerce unless proved otherwise.
- **Art. 9** — A farmer's sale of the produce of his own land is **not** a commercial act.
- **Art. 10** — A merchant (تاجر) is (1) anyone who professionally carries on a commercial act in his own name and for his own account, or **(2) any company taking one of the forms prescribed by company legislation, whatever its purpose.** *Art. 10(2) is the hook that pulls every Egyptian company into commercial law.*
- **Art. 11** — ⚠️ **Capacity to trade: 21 years as of right; from 18 only with authorisation from the Egyptian court.** Art. 11(2): a person under 18 may not carry on trade in Egypt even if his national law considers him of age or permits him to trade.

#### Commercial register (السجل التجاري)
- **Book Four, Chapter Four (الباب الرابع - الفصل الرابع: السجل التجاري)**
- **Art. 30(1)** — A register of registration is established at the competent administrative authority, recording the names of **merchants, whether individuals or companies**.
- **Art. 30(2)** — ⚠️ **The Commercial Code itself does not specify who must register, the deadlines, the particulars required, de-registration, or penalties.** It expressly refers these out to "the laws and decisions specific thereto" (القوانين والقرارات الخاصة بذلك). *Do not assert register deadlines or penalties from the Commercial Code — cite the commercial-register implementing legislation instead.*
- ✅ Confirmed separately: **Law No. 110 of 1943** laying down rules for the implementation of the law on the sale of commercial premises (بيع المحال التجارية) and security, and Decision No. 110 of 1943 implementing it, cover publication of sales/mortgages of commercial premises in the official commercial-premises gazette (جريدة بيع المحال التجارية) — a genuinely separate and easily-confused instrument.

#### Negotiable instruments (الأوراق التجارية)

  - Chapter One — **الكمبيالة** (bill of exchange)
  - Chapter Two — **السند لأمر** (promissory note)
  - Chapter Three — **الشيك** (cheque)
  - Chapter Four — **أحكام مشتركة** (common provisions)

## 3. Companies Law (قانون الشركات)

### Companies Law (قانون شركات المساهمة وشركات التوصية بالأسهم والشركات ذات المسئولية المحدودة)
- **Citation:** Law No. 159 of 1981.
- **Signed:** 17 September 1981 (19 Dhu al-Qa'da 1401 H).
- **Publication:** الجريدة الرسمية (Official Gazette), Issue No. 40 bis, 1 October 1981.
- **Entry into force:** Article 6 — six months after publication.
- **Status:** in force; amended **eight times**, the most recent verified being Law 4/2018.

#### ⚠️ TITLE CHANGE — a live trap
The law's Arabic title has been amended. As originally titled it was
**«قانون شركات المساهمة وشركات التوصية بالأسهم والشركات ذات المسئولية المحدودة وشركات الشخص الواحد»** (including one-person companies), and
**Law 4 of 2018 substituted the title «قانون شركات المساهمة وشركات التوصية بالأسهم والشركات ذات المسئولية المحدودة»** (omitting one-person companies), **and every reference to the old title in this law, in the accompanying regulations, and in any other law, was replaced accordingly.** A one-person company company (شركة الشخص الواحد) therefore still exists in practice but the statute's own name no longer carries it. Note also that Insurance Law No. 155 of 2024 (see §4) reverts to the longer title when referring to Law 159/1981.

#### Verified key provisions
- **Art. 1(1)** — Applies to **joint stock companies (شركات المساهمة)**, **partnerships limited by shares (شركات التوصية بالأسهم)**, **limited liability companies (الشركات ذات المسئولية المحدودة)** and **one-person companies (شركات الشخص الواحد)** having their head office in Egypt or carrying on their principal activity there. **Added by Law 4/2018.** Art. 1(3), also added by Law 4/2018: **every company incorporated in Egypt must have a head office in Egypt, must state it in its constitutive documents, and must register every change of head office — otherwise the administrative measures, including addressing announcements to the registered head office, apply against it.**
- **Art. 2** — **Joint stock company (شركة المساهمة / ش.م.م)**: capital divided into **shares of equal value**, transferable in the manner prescribed by the law. **Shareholder liability is limited to the value of the shares subscribed for; shareholders are not liable for company debts beyond that.** The company has a trade name deriving from its purpose, which may also include the surname or title of one or more of its founders (last sentence added by Law 4/2018).
- **Art. 3** — **Partnership limited by shares (ش.م.ذ.م — شركة التوصية بالأسهم)**: capital consists of (a) one or more general partner shares held by one or more general partners (شريك متضامن) and (b) equal-value shares subscribed by one or more shareholders. **The general partner(s) are liable without limit for the company's obligations; the shareholder is liable only for the value of the shares subscribed.** The name consists of the names of the general partners only.
- **Art. 4** — **Limited liability company (ش.ذ.م.م — شركة ذات المسئولية المحدودة)**: **may not exceed fifty partners**; each is liable **only to the extent of his share**. **It may not be incorporated or increased in capital by public subscription; it may not issue shares or tradable securities; and transfer of partners' shares is subject to retraction (الشد) by the other partners** on the terms contained in the company contract and in addition to those set by the law. It may have a special name, derived from its purpose, which may include the name of one or more partners.
- **Art. 4 bis** — **One-person company (شركة الشخص الواحد)**: capital wholly owned by one natural or legal person, not contradicting the law. The founder is liable for its obligations **only within the limits of the capital made available to it**, and not personally. It takes a special name derived from its purpose or the founder's name; **the name must state that it is a one-person company with limited liability**; and that designation must appear on its head office, its branches (if any), and **all its offices and its letterhead**.
- **Art. 5** — ⚠️ **Partnerships limited by shares and limited liability companies may NOT carry on insurance business, banking business, savings business, or invest money on behalf of others.**
- **Art. 6** — ⚠️ **Personal liability / capital understatement rule.** All contracts, invoices, papers, trade names, advertisements and other printed matter issued by a company must contain the company's name, its type (before or after the name), in clear legible letters, **together with its head office and the amount of its issued capital according to its value in the last financial statements.** **Whoever uses the company name in any transaction disregarding this is liable in his own assets for all obligations arising from that transaction; and if the capital figure stated is inflated, the counterparty may hold the person who used the name liable for the difference between the real capital value and the stated figure, to the extent necessary to secure the creditor.**
- **Art. 7** — Founders are those who actually participate in incorporation undertaking the resulting liability, and are subject to **Art. 89**. Founders **specially** are those who sign the preliminary agreement, apply for the incorporation licence, or contribute a thing in kind. **One who participates in incorporation on behalf of the founders or the owners of the money in a company is not a founder.**
- **Art. 8** — **Except for one-person companies, a joint stock company may not have fewer than three founding partners, and the other companies under this law may not have fewer than two.** If the number falls below that threshold the company is **dissolved (منحلة) by operation of law**, unless within **at most six months** it either restores the threshold or the remaining partners apply to convert it into a one-person company — in which case those partners remain jointly and severally liable for the company's obligations for that period. (Art. 8(1) substituted by Law 4/2018.)
- **Art. 9** — The preliminary agreement is concluded on the form issued by the competent minister. **It may not contain any clause releasing the founders from the liability of incorporation, or any clause providing for their continuing to apply after incorporation unless incorporated in the constitutive agreement or the articles of association.**
- **Art. 9 bis** — ⚠️ **Shareholders' agreement (اتفاق المساهمين / shareholders' agreement), added by Law 4/2018.** Notwithstanding Art. 9, shareholders or partners may, at incorporation or later, **conclude an agreement regulating the relationship between them.** It does **not** bind the remaining shareholders or partners unless they consent by a **two-thirds majority (by at least three-quarters of the capital) of the ordinary general assembly**, or a higher majority in cases specified by the Executive Regulations.
- **Art. 10** — Founders are **jointly and severally liable (مسئولية تضامنية)** for what they have undertaken. A founder who undertook for another is personally liable unless he includes his principal's name in the incorporation contract or the power of attorney proves invalid.
- **Art. 11** — A founder must act with the diligence of a prudent man in dealings with the company before and after incorporation; founders are **jointly and severally liable for any damage** to the company or a third party from breach. A founder who receives money or information belonging to the company before incorporation must return it, and any profit from using it.
- **Art. 12** — Acts done in the company's name before incorporation bind the company afterwards only if ratified by the board (all its members being unconnected with the transaction) or by the partners' assembly or by the general assembly at a meeting in which the interested founders have limited votes; in all cases the interested founder must put the matter before [the body].

## 4. Capital Markets Law (قانون سوق رأس المال) and the FRA (الهيئة العامة للرقابة المالية)

### Capital Markets Law (قانون سوق رأس المال)
- **Citation:** Law No. 95 of 1992.
- **Signed:** 22 June 1992 (21 Dhu al-Hijja 1412 H).
- **Publication:** dated 25 June 1992 in the FRA's consolidated text header.
- **Entry into force:** Art. 5 — the day following publication.
- **Status:** in force; amended at least eight times, most recently verified as Law 17/2018.

#### Verified key provisions (from the FRA's own consolidated text)
- **Art. 1(1)** — The capital of a joint stock company and the shareholding of non-general partners in a partnership limited by shares is divided into **nominal shares of equal value**. **The share is not divisible.** New shares may be issued on a capital increase at a value different from previous issues, with the same rights and obligations as previous issues. (Bearer shares were dealt with in the pre-2018 wording; **Law 17/2018 amended Art. 1(1) and **abolished the last paragraph of Art. 1** relating to public subscription, while Law 123/2008 had earlier amended Art. 1(2) on the nominal value band. **Art. 1(3)** provides that share certificates, replacement of lost or damaged certificates, and treatment on amendment of the company's articles are set by the Executive Regulations.) ⚠️ Because of these layered amendments I will **not** state the current nominal-value band — verify Art. 1(2) directly in the FRA consolidated PDF before quoting it.
- **Art. 2** — A legal person wishing to issue securities must notify the Authority; **if the Authority does not object within seven working days of notification, the issuance procedure proceeds** (before Law 123/2008 this was three weeks and applied to companies rather than legal persons). The Executive Regulations specify the notification data and documents.
- **Art. 3** — **Repealed by Law No. 13 of 2004** (it dealt with shares issued for a contribution in kind or on a merger and required valuation by the competent valuation committee, with a right of objection to the grievance committee).
- **Art. 4** — **Restraint on offshore public offering.** No Egyptian or non-Egyptian legal person, whatever its nature or the legal system it is subject to, may offer securities or financial instruments offshore — i.e. outside Egypt — by the means of publication determined by the Authority's board. **Amended by Law 17/2018.** Notwithstanding the preceding paragraph, the Authority's board issues the rules to be complied with when any securities or financial instruments are offered in a **public subscription (اكتتاب عام), a public offering (طرح عام) or a private placement (طرح خاص)**, according to the type of security or financial instrument.
- **Art. 17** — ⚠️ **Trading securities listed on an Egyptian exchange outside such an exchange is VOID (باطل).** The Authority's board issues the rules and procedures for dealing in securities **not listed on Egyptian exchange tables**, and for the transfer of title thereto. The exchange must supply the Authority with the periodic data and reports specified by the Executive Regulations. (First paragraph wording as amended by Law 12/2003 per the FRA text.)
- ✅ **Sukuk (الصكوك) framework — verified from the FRA's own English page:** **Law No. 17 of 2018** amended the Capital Markets Law to regulate the legal framework for sukuk; **Prime Ministerial Decree No. 2479 of 2018** amended provisions of the Executive Regulations, setting the rules on public offerings, issuance procedures and the roles of all parties in the securitisation process; and a series of **FRA board resolutions**, including **Decision No. 176 of 2018**, completed the framework.

#### FRA (الهيئة العامة للرقابة المالية)
- **Citation:** **Law No. 10 of 2009** on Regulating the Supervision of Non-Banking Financial Markets and Instruments (قانون رقم 10 لسنة 2009 بتنظيم الرقابة على الأسواق والأدوات المالية غير المصرفية).
- **Constitutional basis:** Art. 221 of the Egyptian Constitution establishes the FRA as an independent regulatory authority.
- **Basic statute:** **Presidential Decree No. 192 of 2009** (النظام الأساسي للهيئة).
- **Status:** in force; **amended by Law No. 71 of 2019** (FRA's own page publishes the consolidated version "نسخة محدثة فى 2019").

#### FRA key provisions (verified)
- **Art. 1** — There is established a **General Authority for the Supervision of Non-Banking Financial Markets and Instruments, called the Financial Regulatory Authority (الهيئة العامة للرقابة المالية)**, with public legal personality, **reporting to the competent minister**. A Presidential Decree issues its basic statute after Cabinet approval; another Presidential Decree designates the competent minister. The Authority may open branches and offices by decision of the competent minister with its board's approval.
- **Art. 2** — The Authority is competent to supervise and oversee **capital markets, derivatives exchanges, insurance activities, real estate finance, financial leasing, factoring and securitisation**.
- **Art. 3** — ✅ **The FRA replaces the Egyptian Insurance Regulatory Authority (الهيئة المصرية للرقابة على التأمين), the Capital Market Authority (الهيئة العامة لسوق المال) and the Real Estate Finance Authority (الهيئة العامة لشئون التمويل العقاري)** in the application of Law 10/1981 (insurance supervision), Law 95/1992 (capital markets), Law 93/2000 (central depository and registration of securities) and Law 148/2001 (real estate finance). The FRA is also the competent administrative authority for Law 95/1995 (financial leasing).
- **Art. 4** — The Authority works to ensure the soundness and stability of non-banking financial markets, their regulation and development, and the balance of the rights of market participants; it provides the means, systems and rules guaranteeing market efficiency and transparency; and among its specific powers is **1 — licensing the exercise of non-banking financial activities.**

## 5. Investment Law (قانون الاستثمار / قانون ضمانات وحوافز الاستثمار) and Executive Regulations

### Investment Law (قانون الاستثمار / قانون ضمانات وحوافز الاستثمار)
- **Citation:** Law No. 72 of 2017.
- **Signed:** 31 May 2017 (5 Ramadan 1438 H).
- **Entry into force:** Art. 10 — the day following publication.
- **Status:** in force; amended, including by **Law No. 160 of 2023**.

#### ⚠️ TITLE CHANGE
**Art. 3 substitutes the expression "Investment Law" (قانون الاستثمار) with "Investment Guarantees and Incentives Law" (قانون ضمانات وحوافز الاستثمار) wherever it appears in other laws and decisions.** The current official GAFI English page still uses "Investment Law"; the current Arabic text uses قانون الاستثمار as the short title. Cite both.

#### Verified key provisions
- **Promulgation Art. 1** — Applies to investment in Egypt under the Domestic Investment system, the Investment Zones system, the Technology Zones system, or the Free Zones system (المناطق الحرة).
- **Promulgation Art. 2** — **Grandfathering:** the tax exemptions and other guarantees, incentives and privileges of companies and establishments **existing at the time this Law came into force continue until the end of their original periods**, in accordance with the legislation and the agreements from which they derive. The Law does **not** affect Law 7/1991 (State Property), Law 83/2002 (Special Economic Zones), Law 14/2012 (Sinai development) or Law 15/2017 (facilitating industrial licensing). The Law does **not** affect the objective conditions for granting approvals, permits and licences in other laws.
- **Promulgation Art. 4** — **Joint stock companies governed by this Law are exempt from Law 113/1958 (appointment to positions in JSCs and public institutions), and Law 73/1973 (election of worker representatives) does not apply to them — the company's by-laws instead provide the method of employee participation in management.**
- **Promulgation Art. 5** — **Disputes arising from the application of this Law and its Executive Regulations are excluded from the scope of Law 7/2000 establishing Settlement Committees in certain disputes where ministries and public legal persons are parties.**
- **Promulgation Art. 6** — Grievances and applications before the **Investment Dispute Settlement Committee** and the **Investment Contracts Dispute Settlement Committee** go to the two committees specified in **Arts. 85 and 88** of the Law immediately on their formation, without any further formality.
- **Promulgation Art. 8** — **Repeals Law No. 8 of 1997 (Investment Guarantees and Incentives Law).**
- **Promulgation Art. 9** — The Executive Regulations are issued by the Prime Minister on the proposal of the competent minister; until they issue, and for 90 days after Cabinet approval, the existing regulations and decisions continue insofar as not inconsistent.

#### Art. 1 (definitions) — as amended
- **Art. 1, paragraph first, as substituted by Law No. 160 of 2023** — the sectors benefiting from the Law's incentives and guarantees: industry; animal, poultry or fish production; commercial; educational; health; transport; tourism; housing; infrastructure; renewable energy; ICT; and such other sectors as the competent minister may add by coordination with the relevant ministry, per the Law's Executive Regulations.
- **Art. 1** — **Special incentives (الحوافز الخاصة): those provided for in Arts. 11 and 11 bis.**
- **Art. 1** — **Capital (الأموال): all types of assets entering the investment project, whether cash, in kind or intangible, including in particular: fixed and movable assets and any original or accessory real rights; shares, incorporation shares and non-government bonds; intellectual property rights and intangible rights used in creating or expanding projects, such as patents, trade marks and registered trade names in a WIPO member state or under international registration rules; and privileges or contracts granted under public-law undertakings and similar laws, and all similar rights conferred by law.**
- **Art. 1** — **Foreign investment (الاستثمار الأجنبي)** = any natural or legal person, Egyptian or foreign, who invests in Egypt under the Law.
- **Art. 1** — **Investment project (المشروع الاستثماري)**: carrying on one of the investment activities in the sectors of industry, agriculture, trade, education, health, transport, tourism, housing and construction, energy, natural resources, water, communications and technology (the first list as amended by Law 160/2023).
- **Art. 1** — Defined institutions: **the General Authority for Investment and Free Zones (الهيئة / GAFI)**; the Supreme Council for Investment (المجلس الأعلى); the competent minister / ministry; **Investor Services Centre (مركز خدمات المستثمرين)** — an administrative unit established by the Authority or one of its branches to apply the system for simplifying and facilitating the investor's obtaining of all approvals, permits and licences for the project within the statutory periods, and providing the necessary data and information; **competent representative (ممثل الجهة المختصة)** — the official delegated from the competent authority or assigned from a public-sector company, to whom **the authority to issue approvals, permits and licences passes** under the Law, within the technical conditions of the relevant laws and the investment procedures guide issued by the Authority, and with all the powers vested in the competent authority in real-estate allocation and granting approvals and licences; **the competent authority (السلطة المختصة)** — the minister, governor, head of the Authority, the head of the service or the board of a public-sector company, as the case may be; **accredited offices (مكاتب الاعتماد)** — offices duly licensed by the Authority to grant approvals, permits and licences and acting to review projects' documents and issue accreditation certificates.

#### Art. 2 (objects and principles of investment)
Lists eight binding principles applying to both the investor and the State: **equality of investment opportunity and equivalence of opportunities regardless of project size or location and without discrimination on grounds of nationality**; support for State backing of start-ups and small, micro and medium enterprises; consideration of social dimensions and protection of the environment and public health; **freedom of competition and prohibition of monopolistic practices and protection of the consumer**; adoption of **governance, transparency, sound management and the absence of conflicts of interest**; **policy and legislative stability**; speed in completing investor transactions and facilitating them in a way that safeguards their legitimate interests; and **the State's right to preserve national security and public interest**.

#### Art. 3 (guarantees)
- All investments established in Egypt enjoy **fair and equitable treatment**; the State guarantees the foreign investor treatment equivalent to that accorded to the national investor, and **may by Council of Ministers decision confer preferential treatment on foreign investors by way of exception to national treatment**. Invested funds may not be subject to **arbitrary procedures or discriminatory measures**.
- Non-Egyptian investors are granted **residence in Egypt for the whole duration of the project**, without prejudice to the laws regulating this and as set out in the Executive Regulations.
- **The State undertakes to honour and perform the contracts it concludes.** The investment project established on the basis of fraud, deceit, corruption of protection or of the guarantees, incentives or privileges under the Law shall **not** enjoy them, and proof of all that is by a final judgment of the competent court or by an **arbitral award**.
- **All decisions relating to the investment project must be reasoned and notified to the interested parties**, as regulated by the Executive Regulations.
- Art. 3 prohibits **attenuation (إ Justin) of investment projects**; prohibits expropriation of the funds of investment projects **except for public benefit and against fair, prompt compensation**, equal to the fair economic value of the expropriated property **payable in advance to its owner on the day preceding the expropriation decision**, and transferable without restriction; prohibits **administrative receivership** over such projects or attachment except by final court judgment.

#### Incentives
- ✅ From the Executive Regulations' consolidated text: **general incentives include an investment incentive of a deduction from tax-compliant profits of a percentage determined according to the investment map** — **50% deduction from investment costs for Sector (A)**, covering the most needy geographic areas under the investment map as determined by CAPMAS data and the general plan for economic and social development, with activity distribution set by the Executive Regulations; and **30% deduction from investment costs for Sector (B)**, covering the rest of the Republic. Special incentives cover **labour-intensive projects** under the Executive Regulations' controls; **micro, small and medium enterprises**; **projects relying on new or renewable energy** — added by **Law No. 160 of 2023**; **electricity and other energy projects**, designated by a decision of the Prime Minister on the proposal of the competent minister and the Minister of Electricity and Energy.

The **article numbers** for each incentive, and the special-incentives article numbers, are **not verified** — cite "Investment Law Arts. 11 and 11 bis (special incentives)" only, which *is* verified, and treat the percentages as "per the Executive Regulations' consolidated text" without an article number.

## 7. Intellectual Property Rights Law (قانون حماية حقوق الملكية الفكرية)

### Intellectual Property Rights Law (قانون حماية حقوق الملكية الفكرية)
- **Citation:** Law No. 82 of 2002.
- **Promulgated:** 2 June 2002. **Published:** 2 June 2002. **Entry into force:** 3 June 2002.
- **Status:** in force; amended. WIPO Lex carries a consolidated version "amended up to 5 September 2020".

#### Commissioning provision
**Art. 4** — The Law is published in the Official Gazette and comes into force the day after publication (2 June 2002), **except the provisions on patents for food-related chemical products, pharmaceutical chemical products, microorganisms, and products not previously subject to protection, which come into force on 1 January 2005** — without prejudice to Art. 44 in Part I and Art. 45 in Part II of Book One.

#### Structure (✅ four Books)
- **Book One:** Patents and Utility Models, Layout Designs for Integrated Circuits, and Undisclosed Information
- **Book Two:** Marks, Trade Names, Geographical Indications and Industrial Designs
- **Book Three:** Copyright and Related Rights
- **Book Four:** Plant Varieties

#### Verified provisions — patents, trade secrets, marks
- **Patent grant** — granted to any industrially applicable invention that is new, involves an inventive step, and is connected with new products, new industrial processes, or a new application of known industrial processes.
- **Foreign eligibility** — without prejudice to international conventions in force in Egypt, **any natural person or legal entity, Egyptian or foreign, belonging to, domiciled or active in a WTO member country, or a country applying reciprocity to Egypt**, may apply for a patent at the Egyptian Patent Office and enjoy the resulting rights.
- **Patent term** — **20 years** from [the filing date — ⚠️ the WIPO excerpt is truncated at "as of the"].
- **Utility model** — a non-renewable period [⚠️ length not verified].
- **Layout design** — **10 years** from the date the application for registration was filed in Egypt, or 10 years from first commercial exploitation in Egypt or abroad, whichever is earlier.
- **Trade secrets (المعلومات غير المكشوف عنها)** — **Arts. 66 and 83** of the Commercial Code 17/1999 also deal with undisclosed information relating to industrial secrets and the secrecy of technology (verified from WIPO Lex's note on Law 17/1999). ⚠️ Article numbers *within* Law 82/2002 for trade secrets: **not verified**.
- **Art. 63** — **Definition of a trademark (العلامة التجارية)**: any sign distinguishing goods, whether products or services, including in particular names represented in a distinctive manner, signatures, words, letters, numerals, designs, symbols, signposts, stamps, seals, drawings, engravings, a combination of distinctly formed colours, and any other combination of these elements, if used or intended to be used to distinguish the products of a particular industry, or agricultural, forest or mining venture, or any goods, or to indicate the origin of products or goods, or their quality, category, guarantee, preparation process, or the provision of any service.
- **Trademark eligibility** — any natural or legal person, Egyptian or foreign, belonging to or having its centre of effective activity in a WTO member, or applying reciprocity to Egypt, may apply to register a trademark with the **Department of Trade Registry** (إدارة العلامات التجارية) in Egypt.

#### Verified provisions — copyright and related rights (Book Three)
- **Art. 139** — **National treatment for copyright and neighbouring rights** extends to Egyptians and foreigners, natural or legal persons, belonging to a WTO member State and nationals thereof. Deemed nationals include, for copyright: (1) authors whose works were **first published in a WTO member State**, or published simultaneously in a non-member and a member State — **simultaneous publication meaning publication in two or more States within thirty days of first publication**. Not treated as publication: performance of a dramatic, dramatico-musical, cinematographic or musical work; public recitation of a literary work; communication by wire or broadcasting; exhibition of a work of art; construction of a work of architecture. (2) Producers and authors of cinematographic works whose maker has headquarters or habitual residence in a WTO member State. (3) Authors of architecture erected in a member State, or of artistic works incorporated in a building located in a member State. For **neighbouring rights**: performers, provided the performance took place in a WTO member State, or was produced in phonograms whose producer is a national of a WTO member State, or whose sound was first fixed in a WTO member State territory.
- **Arts. 143** — ⚠️ **Moral rights (الحقوق الأدبية).** The author and successors enjoy, in respect of the work, **permanent moral rights which are not liable to prescription or assignment (لا تسقط بالتقادم ولا تنتقل)**: (1) the right to first make the work available to the public; (2) the right to claim authorship; (3) the right to object to any amendment the author deems a distortion or mutilation — **amendment of a translation is not infringement unless the translator omitted citation of the places of cancellation or changes, or prejudiced the honour and reputation of the author.**
- **Art. 144** — **The author alone may apply to the Court of First Instance, if serious reasons exist, for a ruling preventing circulation of the work, withdrawing it from circulation, or ordering substantial amendment — notwithstanding disposal of the financial exploitation rights.** In that case the author must **offer fair compensation in advance** to the holder of the financial exploitation rights, to be paid within a period specified by the court; otherwise the ruling is void.
- **Art. 145** — **Any disposal of a moral right under Arts. 143 and 144 is absolutely null and void.**
- **Art. 146** — On the expiry of the period prescribed for protection of the financial rights, **and in the absence of any heir or testamentary beneficiary, the competent Ministry exercises the moral rights under Arts. 143 and 144.**
- **Arts. 156–157** — Referenced as the provisions on producers' exploitation rights for performers (Art. 166) and phonogram producers (Art. 167).
- **Art. 147** — The author and successors enjoy the **exclusive right** to grant licences or prevent any exploitation of the work, in any manner, particularly reproduction [truncated in source].
- **Art. 160** — **Financial rights of the author: the life of the author plus fifty years from death.**
- **Art. 161** — **Joint authorship: life of the authors plus fifty years from the death of the last surviving author.**
- **Art. 162** — **Collective works (except applied art): fifty years from publication or first making the work available to the public, whichever is later**, where the right holder is a legal person; if a natural person, Arts. 160–161 apply. Financial rights on works **first published after the author's death** terminate **fifty years from first publication or first making available to the public, whichever is later**.
- **Art. 163** — **Anonymous and pseudonymous works: fifty years from first publication or first making available, whichever is later**; if the author is identified and well known or has disclosed their identity, Art. 160 applies.
- **Art. 164** — **Applied art works: twenty-five years** from first publication or making available, whichever is later.
- **Art. 165** — The **later** of first publication or first making available is the starting point; repetition does not count unless the author made substantial modifications rendering the work novel. For a work in several separately published parts or volumes, **each part is treated as independent** for computing the term.
- **Art. 166** — **Performers: exclusive financial rights for fifty years from the date of the performance or registration**, as the case requires.
- **Art. 167** — **Producers of phonograms: exclusive financial rights for fifty years** from [date truncated in source].
- **Art. 414** — **Performers and their successors have a permanent moral right** which is not [prescribed or assignable]; in the absence of [any holder], the competent Ministry exercises it.

## 9. Arbitration Law (قانون التحكيم في المواد المدنية والتجارية)

### Arbitration Law (قانون التحكيم في المواد المدنية والتجارية)
- **Citation:** Law No. 27 of 1994.
- **Signed / issued:** **18 April 1994** (7 Dhu al-Qa'da 1414 H), by the Presidency.
- **Publication:** Official Gazette **No. 16 bis, 21 April 1994**.
- **Entry into force:** one month from the day following publication → **22 May 1994** (per WIPO Lex).
- **Status:** in force; amended by **Law 9/1997** and **Law 8/2000**.

#### ❌ CORRECTION: not Law 34/1988
Egypt's arbitration statute is **Law 27 of 1994**. I searched specifically for Law 34/1988 and for an "Egyptian Arbitration Law 34/1988"; nothing supports that citation. The likely sources of the error are the confusion with Law 17/1999 (Commercial Code) and with the Egyptian *Court of Cassation* practice decisions, and with Law No. 34 of a different subject.

#### Promulgation articles (verified, English translation by the Association of Arab and African Arbitrators / CRCICA)
- **Art. 1** — Applies to any arbitration pending at the time of entry into force or commencing thereafter, **even if based on an arbitration agreement concluded before entry into force.**
- **Art. 2** — The Minister of Justice issues the implementing decrees and **establishes the lists of arbitrators from whom selections are made under Art. 17.**
- **Art. 3** — **Repeals Arts. 501–513 of Law No. 13 of 1968** (Code of Civil and Commercial Procedures), and any contrary provision.
- **Art. 4** — Published in the Official Gazette; in force one month from the day following publication.

#### Substantive provisions (verified)
- **Art. 1** — Subject to international conventions in force in Egypt, the Law applies to **all arbitrations between public-law and private-law persons, whatever the nature of the legal relationship**, where the arbitration is **conducted in Egypt**, or where it is an **international commercial arbitration conducted abroad and the parties agree to submit it to the Law.** Art. 1(2) (**added by Law 9/1997, published in the Official Gazette on 15 May 1997, in force one day after publication**): **for disputes concerning administrative contracts, agreement to arbitrate requires the approval of the competent minister or the official assuming his powers in respect of public legal persons, and delegation of that power is not permitted.** ⚠️ This is a very significant carve-out for any public-sector or state-entity dispute.
- **Art. 2** — Arbitration is **commercial** where the dispute arises over a legal relationship of an economic nature, contractual or non-contractal, including: supply of goods or services, commercial agencies, construction, engineering or technical know-how contracts, granting of industrial/touristic and other licences, **transfer of technology**, investment and development contracts, banking, insurance and transport operations, operations relating to exploration and extraction of natural wealth, energy supply, laying of gas or oil pipelines, building of roads and tunnels, reclamation of agricultural land, protection of the environment, and establishment of nuclear reactors.
- **Art. 3** — An arbitration is **international** if: (1) the parties' principal places of business are in different States at the time the arbitration agreement is concluded (with a "closest relationship" test for multiple places of business, and habitual residence as fallback); or (2) the parties have agreed to resort to a **permanent arbitral organisation or arbitration centre** headquartered in Egypt or abroad; or (3) the subject matter is linked to more than one country; or (4) the parties' principal places of business are in the same State but **the place of arbitration, the place of substantial performance, or the place most closely connected with the subject matter** is outside that State.
- **Art. 4** — Arbitration is voluntary, agreed by free will; "arbitral tribunal" is one or more arbitrators; "court" means a court of the Egyptian judicial system; "the two parties" denotes however many there are.
- **Art. 5** — Where the Law lets the parties choose procedure, they may **delegate that choice to a third party**, and any arbitral organisation or centre in Egypt or abroad counts as a third party.
- **Art. 6** — Where the parties subject the legal relationship to a standard contract, international convention or other document, its provisions apply **including its arbitration provisions**.
- **Art. 7** — Default service of any letter or written communication: personally, at the addressee's place of business, habitual residence, or the postal address known to both parties or specified in the arbitration session report or in the document governing the relationship. If, after inquiries, the address cannot be ascertained, delivery is deemed effected by registered letter to the last known place of business, habitual residence or known postal address. Art. 7(3): does not apply to court service.
- **Art. 8** — **Waiver by conduct**: a party who continues in the arbitral proceedings knowing of a breach of the arbitration agreement or of a provision of the Law **agreed to be departed from**, and who does not object within the agreed period or a reasonable time, is deemed to have waived the right to object.
- **Art. 9(1)** — **Supervision competence over matters referred to the Egyptian judiciary by this Law lies with the court having original jurisdiction over the dispute. BUT in the case of an international commercial arbitration, whether seated in Egypt or abroad, competence lies with the Cairo Court of Appeal (محكمة استئناف القاهرة), unless the parties agree that another Court of Appeal in Egypt has jurisdiction.** ⚠️ A frequent and serious drafting trap: parties cannot waive the Cairo Court of Appeal's exclusive supervisory role for international commercial arbitrations by agreeing on another Egyptian forum.
- **Art. 10(3)** — A reference in a contract to a document containing an arbitration clause counts as an agreement to arbitrate **if the reference is clear** and the clause is deemed part of the contract.
- **Art. 13(2)** — **Filing an action on the matters indicated in the preceding paragraph does not prevent the start or continuation of the arbitral proceedings or the issuance of the arbitral award.**
- **Art. 14** — See §10 below.
- **Art. 15(1)** — The tribunal is formed by agreement of the parties from **one or more arbitrators; failing agreement on the number, the number is three.** Art. 15(2): **if there is more than one arbitrator the number must be odd, failing which the arbitration is void (باطل).**
- **Art. 17(2)** — If a party breaches the agreed arbitrator-appointment procedure, or the two appointed arbitrators cannot agree, or a third party fails to perform his appointment function, the court referred to in Art. 9 may, on a party's application, perform the required procedure or act, unless the agreement provides otherwise.
- **Art. 19** — Recusal application in writing to the tribunal stating the grounds, **within fifteen days** of becoming aware of the tribunal's constitution or of the circumstances justifying recusal; a second application by the same person on the same arbitrator in the same arbitration is inadmissible; **a pending recusal application or an appeal against an award refusing it does not suspend the proceedings; and if the arbitrator is challenged, whether by the tribunal or the court, everything done in the proceedings — including the award — is deemed never to have been.** ⚠️ Note that **Art. 19 was amended by Law 8/2000**, published in the Official Gazette on 4 April 2000, enacted to accommodate a decision of the Supreme Constitutional Court.
- **Art. 22(1)–(2)** — The tribunal decides **jurisdiction objections**, including objections that there is **no arbitration agreement, or that it has lapsed or is void, or that it does not cover the subject matter**. **These must be raised no later than the deadline for the respondent's defence under Art. 30(2)**, otherwise they are waived. Appointing or joining in the appointment of an arbitrator does not waive jurisdiction objections — but **the objection that the agreement does not cover matters raised by the other party must be raised immediately or it is waived**, and the tribunal may in any case accept a late objection if it considers the delay justified.
- **Art. 24** — See §10 below.
- **Art. 27** — **Arbitral proceedings begin on the day the respondent receives the request for arbitration, unless the parties agree another date.**
- **Art. 29(1)** — ⚠️ **The language of the arbitration is Arabic unless the parties agree, or the tribunal determines, another language or languages.** The determination governs written submissions and memoranda, oral pleadings, and every decision or message of the tribunal and any award it issues. Art. 29(2): the tribunal may require translation of written documents where more than one language is used.
- **Art. 30(1)–(3)** — The claimant sends a written statement of claim with the matters described in the text (parties, facts, issues, relief) within the agreed or tribunal-set period; the respondent sends a written defence, in which he may include connected counter-claims or invoke a set-off by way of defence **even at a later stage if the tribunal considers the circumstances justify it**, and may attach copies of documents and evidence.
- **Art. 31** — Documents, memoranda and expert reports submitted to the tribunal are copied to the other side.
- **Art. 32** — Each party may amend, add to or complete its claims or defences during the proceedings **unless the tribunal refuses on the ground of preventing delay in the disposal of the dispute.**
- **Art. 33** — Hearings for the parties to present the case; the tribunal may confine itself to written submissions unless otherwise agreed; sessions are notified in sufficient time as the tribunal determines; a minute of each session is drawn up and a copy given to each party; witnesses and experts are heard **without oath (بدون أداء)**, the oath being fixed.
- **Art. 34** — If the claimant fails without acceptable excuse to submit a written statement of claim under Art. 30(1), **the tribunal shall terminate the arbitration** unless the parties agree otherwise; if the respondent fails to submit a defence under Art. 30(2), the arbitration continues **without that alone being deemed an admission** by the respondent unless the parties agree otherwise.
- **Art. 35** — If a party fails to attend a hearing or to produce documents requested, the tribunal may continue and **render an award on the basis of the available evidence.**
- **Art. 36** — The tribunal may appoint one or more experts, fixing the expert's task by a decision served on both parties.

#### Execution of the award (Art. 47 and following)

## 10. Expedited / urgent interim relief before arbitrators (التدابير المؤقتة والتحفظية)

There are **two distinct routes**, and conflating them is the single most common error on this topic.

### Route A — the court, on the application of a party to the arbitration
- **Citation:** **Law No. 27 of 1994, Article 14.**
- **Verified text (English, CRCICA/WIPO):** *"Upon request of either party to the arbitration, the court referred to in Article 9 may order the taking of an interim or conservatory measure, whether before the commencement of the arbitral proceedings or during said proceedings."*
- Arabic: **يجوز للمحكمة المشار إليها في المادة (9) من هذا القانون أن تأمر بناءً على طلب أحد طرفي التحكيم اتخاذ تدابير مؤقتة أو تحفظية سواء قبل البدء في إجراءات التحكيم أو أثناء سيرها.**
- **"The court referred to in Article 9"** = the court with original jurisdiction over the dispute, **except that for an international commercial arbitration (whether seated in Egypt or abroad) it is the Cairo Court of Appeal** unless the parties agree on another Egyptian Court of Appeal.
- ⚠️ The **available types of measures are not limited** by the Arbitration Law; they are the court's ordinary precautionary and conservatory measures under the Code of Civil and Commercial Procedures (Law 13/1968). ⚠️ **The article numbers of the CPC measures (e.g. the preventive attachment / seizure articles) are NOT verified in this file.**

### Route B — the arbitral tribunal, only if the parties agree
- **Citation:** **Law No. 27 of 1994, Article 24.**
- **Verified text (English, CRCICA/WIPO):** *"1. Both parties to the arbitration may agree to confer upon the arbitral tribunal the power to order, upon request of either party, interim or conservatory measures considered necessary in respect of the subject matter of the dispute and to require any party to provide appropriate security to cover the costs of the ordered measure. 2. If the party against whom the order was issued fails to execute it, the arbitral tribunal, upon the request of the other party, may authorize the latter to undertake the procedures necessary for the execution of the order, without prejudice to the right of said party to apply to the president of the court specified in Article 9 of this Law for rendering an execution order."*

#### Practical conditions (verified from Clyde & Co's client alert — **interpretation, not authority for article numbers**)
- To obtain a measure from the tribunal under Art. 24: (1) the parties must **explicitly agree** to confer the power — it can be in the arbitration agreement, at a later stage, **or inferred from the arbitration rules they adopt** (so that agreeing to CRCICA Rules is itself such an agreement); (2) **the arbitration must have commenced** (contrast Route A, which is available **before** commencement); (3) **one of the parties must request it** — the tribunal cannot act *sua sponte*, even where the parties confer the power; (4) the measure must be interim or conservatory; (5) it must be required by the nature of the dispute; and (6) the general conditions must be met, in particular (i) a **probability** that the applicant is entitled to its claim and (ii) an **imminent danger** of damage to the subject matter if the measure is not ordered.
- **Concurrent jurisdiction is recognised**: applying to the court for a measure does **not** waive the arbitration agreement; the parties can still bring the dispute before the tribunal.
- **Two material differences:** (a) a court order has **imperium** effect and is unrestricted as to type, whereas a tribunal order is authorised only by the tribunal's own power and its non-compliance is handled under Art. 24(2); and (b) obtaining a court measure **requires filing a suit heard by the court with the other party heard and making submissions**, whereas enforcement of a tribunal order may proceed by petition.
- ⚠️ Note the **institutional-rules route**: CRCICA Rules authorise tribunals to issue interim and conservatory measures. Verified from CRCICA's own rules publication.

## 11. New York Convention 1958 and Egypt's UNCITRAL position

### New York Convention (اتفاقية الاعتراف بقرارات التحكيم الأجنبية وتنفيذها، نيويورك 1958)
- **Egypt's accession:** **9 March 1959** (marked with (*) = accession, as distinct from signature/ratification).
- **Instrument of accession:** **Presidential Decree No. 171 of 1959, dated 2 February 1959.**
- **Entry into force for Egypt:** **7 June 1959.**
- **Reservations:** ✅ **NONE.** In the UNCITRAL status table Egypt's "Notes" column is **blank** — Egypt made **no reciprocity reservation (Art. I(2)) and no commercial reservation (Art. I(3))**. This matters: a foreign award rendered in a non-contracting state is still covered, and there is no carve-out for non-commercial relationships.
- **Total parties: 172** (per the UNCITRAL page as retrieved).
- **National arbitration act in force** (per the New York Convention Guide): Arbitration Law 27/1994 as amended by Law 9/1997 and Law 8/2000; and, under Art. 47 of that Law, Minister of Justice Decree 8310/2008 (as amended by 6570/2009, 9739/2011 and 1096/2017) governing deposit of arbitral awards for exequatur.

### UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration
- ✅ **Egypt's legislative adoption date: 1994**, with **no reciprocity or commercial reservation recorded.** This is what Law 27/1994 does — Egypt **legislated** the Model Law rather than ratifying a convention.

### ❌ The "Cairo Convention on International Commercial Arbitration (1994)"
**No such instrument was verified as applicable to Egypt.**
- I searched for "اتفاقية القاهرة للتحكيم التجاري الدولي 1994 مصر التصديق" and for the UNCITRAL status pages for the **1994 UNCITRAL Convention on International Commercial Arbitration** (both the `/status` and `/status2` URLs). The NYC status page returned a full table; the two 1994-Convention status pages returned nothing usable on two separate attempts.
- ⚠️ **UNVERIFIED — needs primary-source check.** My working assessment is that Egypt did **not** accede to the 1994 UNCITRAL Convention on International Commercial Arbitration, and that the "Cairo Convention 1994" in circulation is either (a) a confusion with Egypt's adoption of the UNCITRAL **Model Law** in 1994 via Law 27/1994, or (b) a confusion with the **Cairo Convention on the International Sale of Goods (CISG)** — United Nations Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods, concluded in Vienna 11 April 1980, which Egypt is not a party to as far as the evidence here shows, and which must not be confused with it either. **To check: read the UNCITRAL "Status of Conventions" table for the 1994 Convention directly, or the UN Treaty Collection (treaties.un.org) for a bilateral or multilateral agreement between Egypt and the UNCITRAL Secretariat.**

- ✅ **Law No. 7 of 2000 establishing Settlement Committees in certain disputes where ministries and public legal persons are parties** (قانون رقم 7 لسنة 2000 بإنشاء لجان التوفيق في بعض المنازعات التي تكون الوزارات والأشخاص الاعتبارية العامة طرفاً فيها) — verified from Investment Law Promulgation Arts. 5 and 6.
- 🟡 **Mediation law — UNVERIFIED status.** In 2023 the Egyptian Parliament's Constitutional and Legislative Affairs Committee repeatedly considered a **government bill on regulating the procedures of private and judicial mediation for the settlement of civil and commercial disputes** (مشروع قانون بتنظيم إجراءات الوساطة لتسوية المنازعات المدنية والتجارية). I found committee-stage reporting from **May, June and July 2023** but **no gazette reference and no law number**. **Do not tell a user Egypt has a mediation statute.** Verify by searching the Official Gazette for 2023–2026 for a law on الوساطة.

### ❌ A "Unified Arbitration Law 2023" (قانون التحكيم الموحد)
**Not verified to exist.** I searched repeatedly, in Arabic and English, for a unified/new Egyptian arbitration law and for any 2023–2025 instrument on التحكيم, including "قانون التحكيم الموحد", "قرار جمHORIZATION", and a direct search of FRA and WIPO Lex. Everything I retrieved points back to **Law 27/1994** as the operative arbitration statute, amended only by **Law 9/1997** and **Law 8/2000**. The only 2023 legislative activity in the ADR space that I found was the **mediation bill above**, which is a different subject. **An agent asserting that Egypt replaced Law 27/1994 with a "Unified Arbitration Law of 2023" is hallucinating.**

### ❌ A "Commercial Code of 1943" (Law 12/1943)
**Not verified to exist.** I searched specifically for "قانون رقم 12 لسنة 1943" in Arabic, including on legislation aggregators. The results were all unrelated (a 1943 health-fees law, a 1943 inheritance law numbered 77, a 1943 implementing decision numbered 110 on the sale of commercial premises). **The Egyptian Commercial Code is Law 17 of 1999.** The one genuinely 1943 instrument in the commercial-property area is **Law No. 110 of 1943** on the sale and mortgage of commercial premises (بيع المحال التجارية ورهنها), with implementing **Decision No. 110 of 1943** — an easy and plausible source of the mix-up.

## Cross-cutting notes

### What a foreign-trained agent most often gets wrong about Egyptian commercial law

1. **The four citation numbers in the brief were all wrong, and so is the generic "Egyptian Commercial Code 1943" / "Arbitration Law 1988" pattern in most training data.** The real anchors are Commercial Code **17/1999**, Arbitration **27/1994**, E-Signature **15/2004**, Companies **159/1981**. Treat any other combination as suspect until verified.

2. **A codified, Islamic-law-laden civil code, not a codification of European civil law with a Shari'a overlay.** Civil Code Art. 1(2) makes **custom → Shari'a → equity** a statutory decision ladder. Civil Code Art. 87 bars acquisition of **public property** by prescription. Watch for the many register-based rules (a thing is "movable" or "immovable" by reference to registration, Civil Code Arts. 82–83) that have no analogue in a common-law analysis.

3. **Prescription lives at Articles 374–388, and Article 387 forbids the court from applying it of its own motion.** An agent trained on civil-law systems will reflexively apply prescription *ex officio* — in Egypt that is a reversible error of law. Compounding this, **Art. 374's default is fifteen years** (not the ten of the old French Code), the professional claims period is five years (Art. 376), state taxes three years (Art. 377), and merchant/hotel/worker claims **one year (Art. 378)** with a mandatory judicial oath (Art. 378(2)). Do not use the famous "ten years" figure.

4. **Good faith and hardship are in the statute, in specific articles.** Good-faith performance is **Art. 148(1)** — not 146, not 147. Implied terms are **Art. 148(2)**. The duty to perform as the parties' law is **Art. 147(1)**, and **Art. 147(2) contains a full hardship / supervening-event clause allowing judicial reduction of an onerous contract**, voiding any agreement to the contrary. **There is no English-law equivalent.** An agent saying "Egyptian contract law, like English law, does not permit judicial revision for hardship" is wrong on the statute.

5. **Article numbers 146–151 are routinely mis-mapped.** **Art. 146** = obligations passing with the thing to a special successor with **knowledge**; **147** = pacta sunt servanda + hardship; **148** = good faith + implied terms; **149** = court may strike oppressive terms in adhesion contracts; **150** = interpretation; **151** = **doubt construed in the debtor's favour**. **Art. 237 is the creditor's action to have a disposition declared ineffective (عدم نفاذ), not a damages article.** **Art. 488 is about gifts.**

6. **Nullity and annulment are distinct, with different time limits and different curability.** **Art. 141**: a void contract may be invoked by anyone and raised by the court *of its own motion*, cannot be ratified, and the action is barred after fifteen years. **Art. 140**: the right to seek *annulment* lapses after three years and, for mistake/fraud/duress, cannot be exercised more than fifteen years after conclusion. Contrast Art. 387 (prescription is not *ex officio*) with Art. 141 (nullity *is*) — the asymmetry is real and is a favourite exam point and litigation trap.

7. **The Commercial Code is not lex specialis and does not contain its own register regime.** **Art. 2(1)** ends in "and if there is no commercial custom, the Civil Code applies". **Art. 3** splits a mixed contract between the parties, applying the Commercial Code to the merchant's obligations only. **Art. 30(2)** sends the entire commercial register question — who registers, deadlines, particulars, de-registration, penalties — out to other legislation; **do not cite Commercial Code Art. 30 for register deadlines.**

8. **Trader status is a category that swallows every company.** Commercial Code **Art. 10(2)**: *any* company taking a form prescribed by company legislation is a merchant, whatever its purpose. Combined with **Art. 5**'s professional-exercise requirement for listed commercial acts and **Art. 3**'s mixed-contract rule, an agent must analyse each party's obligations separately.

9. **Company form and shareholder-facing minimums.** LLCs are capped at **fifty partners** (Companies Law Art. 4) and cannot use public subscription, issue shares or tradable securities, and transfers are subject to **retraction (الشد)**. Partnerships limited by shares and LLCs are **barred from banking, insurance, savings and investing money on behalf of others** (Art. 5). Art. 6 imposes a **personal-capital-understatement liability** on anyone who uses a company name without stating head office and issued capital. Art. 8's minimum-partner rules now interact with one-person companies via the six-month cure period added by Law 4/2018. **And the statute's own title was changed by Law 4/2018 to drop one-person companies — a citation trap in both directions.**

10. **Shareholders' agreements are now statutorily recognised but need a supermajority to bind minorities.** Companies Law **Art. 9 bis** (added by Law 4/2018): a shareholders'/partners' agreement does not bind the others unless approved by **at least two-thirds of the ordinary general assembly** — i.e. **at least three-quarters of the capital** — or a higher majority in cases set by the Executive Regulations. An agent drafting an SHA must plan for this consent step.

11. **Arbitration: Article 9's split of supervision competence is non-negotiable.** For an **international commercial arbitration, whether seated in Egypt or abroad, supervision lies with the Cairo Court of Appeal** and the parties cannot agree it away to another forum (though they can point it at a different Egyptian Court of Appeal). Domestic arbitration goes to the court with original jurisdiction. Separately, **Art. 15(2): an even number of arbitrators renders the arbitration void (باطل)** — an unglamorous but case-dispositive formality. And **Art. 1(2), added by Law 9/1997: arbitration of administrative-contract disputes requires the competent minister's approval and that power cannot be delegated.**

12. **Egypt arbitrates in Arabic by default.** **Art. 29(1):** the language is Arabic unless the parties agree or the tribunal determines otherwise. Do not assume an English-language arbitration merely because the contract is in English; say the clause should expressly set the language.

13. **Interim relief has two routes and the tribunal route is opt-in.** Under **Art. 14** the *court* may order interim or conservatory measures, **including before the arbitration starts**; under **Art. 24** the *tribunal* may do so **only if the parties agree** (which can be inferred from adopting institutional rules such as CRCICA), **and not *sua sponte***. An agent that tells the user to "apply to the tribunal for urgent relief" without checking for that agreement is giving unusable advice.

14. **Annulment of an Egyptian award is a narrow, 90-day remedy.** Under **Art. 54** of Law 27/1994 the annulment action must be brought within **ninety days of service of the award on the party against whom it was rendered** — and Decision 8310/2008 makes that same ninety-day condition a gate on depositing the award for exequatur in the first place.

15. **Egypt has no reciprocity or commercial reservation to the New York Convention.** It acceded on **9 March 1959** by **Presidential Decree No. 171/1959**, effective **7 June 1959**, and the UNCITRAL status table shows **no notes** for Egypt. An agent who assumes a reciprocity reservation (as with many states) will misadvise on the enforcement of awards from non-contracting states.

16. **IP terms are life + 50, and moral rights are perpetual, unassignable and undisposable.** **IP Law Arts. 160–165**: author life plus fifty years; joint authorship to the death of the last surviving author; collective works fifty years from publication where the holder is a legal person, otherwise life + 50; applied art **25 years** (Art. 164); performers **50 years** (Art. 166). **Art. 143**: moral rights are **permanent and not liable to prescription or assignment**, and **Art. 145 makes any disposal of them absolutely null and void**; **Art. 146** transfers them to the competent Ministry after the financial rights expire and there is no heir. **Art. 144**'s injunction against circulation requires **fair compensation in advance** to the financial-right holder or the ruling is void — a condition that surprises clients.

17. **E-signature validity turns on ITIDA accreditation and the ETSI technical annex, not on a general "electronic signature" concept.** The Executive Regulations key everything to **qualified certificates from accredited certification authorities**, the **RootCA trust chain** with published **one-directional fingerprint verification strings (Regulations Art. 23–25)**, and the **Technical Annex** requiring **ETSI EN 319 411-1 V1.2.2 (2018-04)** or equivalent, with annex amendments published in **الوقائع المصرية** on ITIDA Board approval.

18. **Investment Law grandfathering is written into the promulgating articles.** Promulgation **Art. 2** preserves the tax exemptions, guarantees, incentives and privileges of existing companies and establishments **until the end of their original periods** — a point that materially changes the value of a legacy structure and is often missed.

19. **Read the Official Gazette, not the consolators.** The GAFI publication of the Companies Law says so on its own first page, and the differences are real: the Commercial Code's cheque commencement date is **1 October 2000 in the as-issued text and 1 October 2005 in the later consolidated text**. Agencies and consolidators routinely propagate whichever version they happened to digitise.

**Source-quality notes for whoever picks this up:**
- The **eta.gov.eg** (Egyptian IP authority) host carries official PDFs of Law 131/1948 and Law 159/1981. Its Law 17/1999 PDF has **broken font encoding** — usable only visually, not for text extraction. Its Law 82/2002 path **returns HTML, not a PDF**.
- **WIPO Lex** is the highest-quality English/Arabic source for Law 17/1999, Law 27/1994, Law 3/2005, Law 82/2002 and Law 15/2004, and its "Notes" fields are genuinely informative — but its consolidated versions are often frozen at an early amendment date (Law 27/1994's is consolidated only to April 2000).
- **fra.gov.eg** publishes official consolidated Arabic texts of Law 95/1992 and Law 10/2009 with inline amendment markers, and its sukuk and "definition of the Authority" pages are authoritative English regulator statements.
- **CRCICA** and the New York Convention Guide (PMB/SIGB) are the best sources on the arbitration statute and Egypt's convention status respectively.
- **Clyde & Co's 28 July 2022 alert on Egyptian interim measures** is the single best secondary source for the Art. 14 / Art. 24 split, and correctly labels itself interpretation rather than primary authority.
- **Beware mirror sites.** `alhasswalawfirm.com`, `lawyeregypt.net`, `lawegypt.net`, `egyptlaw.ai`, `mksegypt.org`, `manshurat.org` and `ecica.net` all reproduce gazette text, but none is an official publisher and several carry different amendment states. Where a mirror is the only source of a gazette reference, mark it for re-check.
