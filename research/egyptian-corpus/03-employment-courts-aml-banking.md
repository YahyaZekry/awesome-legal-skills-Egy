# Egypt — Employment, Courts & Procedure, AML, Banking & Financial Regulation

**Scope:** Labour law, social insurance, court structure and procedure, electronic litigation,
judicial fees, legal aid and the Bar, anti-money laundering, banking and financial regulation.

**Research date / "as of":** 12 October 2026 (all "As of" lines below refer to this date unless
otherwise stated).

**Method and honesty rules applied here:**
- Every number, year, article and date below was seen in a retrieved source. Where a fact could
  not be verified, it is explicitly marked `UNVERIFIED — needs primary-source check`.
- **Four premises in the research brief turned out to be WRONG.** They are corrected at the top
  of this file in the "Corrections to the brief" box below. An agent trained on the brief as given
  would hallucinate:
  1. "AML Law No. 40 of 2018 (قانون مكافحة غسل الأموال)" — **no such Egyptian law exists.** Egypt's
     AML statute is **Law No. 80 of 2002**. See §AML.
  2. "Executive Regulations No. 1317 of 2018" for AML — **not found in any Egyptian source.**
     Egypt's AML Executive Regulations are **Prime Minister Decree No. 951 of 2003**, as amended.
  3. "Law No. 172 of 2018 on electronic litigation" — **Egyptian Law No. 172 of 2018 amends the
     Customs Law No. 66 of 1963.** It has nothing to do with electronic litigation. See §Electronic.
  4. "Law No. 12 of 2003 (قانون العمل) is the operative labour law" — **repealed as of 1 September
     2025** by **Labour Law No. 14 of 2025**. Law 12/2003 now governs only transitional/pending
     matters. See §Labour.

## PART 1 — EMPLOYMENT LAW

### Labour Law No. 14 of 2025 (قانون العمل) — the operative Egyptian labour statute

**Citation:** Law No. 14 of 2025 promulgating the Labour Law — *قانون رقم 14 لسنة 2025 بإصدار قانون
العمل*. Promulgated at the Presidency of the Republic on **5 Dhu al-Qi'dah 1446 H / 3 May 2025**;
published in the **Official Gazette (الجريدة الرسمية) issue No. 18 (تابع) of 3 May 2025**.

**Status:** **In force since 1 September 2025.** Art. 13: the law enters into force "on the first day
of the month following the lapse of ninety days from the date of its publication" → 1 Sep 2025.
**With respect to the specialised labour courts, it entered into force on 1 October 2025.**
Art. 12 **repeals** Labour Law No. 12 of 2003 and **Law No. 125 of 2010** on the precedence of
labour rights. The ILO NATLEX record lists Law 12/2003 as "Repealed Text" with entry into force
**2025-09-01**.

**Key provisions (article numbers taken from the Andersen Egypt English translation and from the
Arabic text as published on the Egyptian Tax Authority portal):**

*Scope and general provisions (Book One, Arts. 1–13):*
- **Art. 1** — the law governs employment. It also applies to foreign workers inside Egypt where an
  individual employment contract or collective agreement has no specific provision. Excluded:
  employees of government bodies including local administration units and public authorities, and
  **domestic workers** and similar categories.
- **Art. 3** — "This law is considered the general law that governs labor relations."
- **Art. 4** — bans forced labour and coercion; bans **harassment, bullying and any verbal,
  physical or psychological violence**. Also bars discrimination on religion, belief, gender,
  origin, race, colour, language, disability, social level, political or union affiliation, or
  geographic origin.
- **Art. 5** — explicit equal-pay rule: all workers, male and female, are entitled to **equal pay
  for work of equal value**.
- **Art. 6** — any clause or agreement contradicting the law is **void**, even if made before the
  law, if it reduces the worker's rights. **More favourable individual or collective terms survive
  a change in the company's legal status or an ownership transfer.**
- **Art. 7** — lawsuits by **workers, trainees, apprentices or their beneficiaries are exempt from
  judicial fees and expenses at all stages**; the court may order immediate self-execution without
  bail; also exempt from stamp duty.
- **Arts. 9 and 10** — *transition for pending labour cases.* Courts **of their own motion** refer
  pending labour disputes to the specialised labour courts **in their current procedural posture,
  free of fees**. Cases already decided or reserved for judgment before the entry into force of the
  law stay with the original courts. The **Court of Cassation and the Courts of Appeal continue to
  hear appeals** in those disputes if the appeal was filed **before** the start of the specialised
  labour court system.
- **Art. 11** — the Minister of Labour was to issue executive regulations and decisions within
  90 days; until then, existing regulations continue to the extent not inconsistent. The **Minister
  of Justice** issues the implementing decisions for the **specialised labour courts**.

*Employment contract, forms, probation (Book Three, Section One):*
- **Art. 87** — an individual labour contract may be **indefinite** or **fixed-term** if the nature
  of the work requires; renewals by mutual agreement.
- **Art. 88** — a contract is **deemed indefinite** if it is (a) not written; (b) silent as to
  duration; or (c) fixed-term but **performed by both parties after expiry without a written
  agreement**.
- **Art. 89** — the contract must be **in writing, in Arabic, in four copies**: employer, worker,
  the social insurance office, and the relevant administrative authority. For a foreign worker who
  does not speak Arabic, a bilingual contract is allowed and **the Arabic version prevails**.
  Required contents: start date; employer name and workplace; worker name, qualifications,
  trade, insurance number, residence and ID; nature and type of work; wage, payment method and
  schedule, and any additional monetary or in-kind benefits. Where there is no written contract,
  **both parties may prove the relationship and all rights by all available means of proof**; the
  employer must give the worker a receipt for deposited documents.
- **Art. 90** — **probation may not exceed three months** and a worker **may not be placed on
  probation more than once with the same employer.** (Note: Law 14/2025 does *not* repeat the
  old Law 12/2003 art. 33 formulation about either party being free to terminate during probation —
  treat that as `UNVERIFIED — needs primary-source check` for the new law.)
- **Arts. 91–95** — limits on unilateral variation of duties; mandatory **worker file** (paper or
  electronic) with contents set out and **retention of at least five years** after the end of the
  employment relationship, and **retained until a final judgment where a dispute exists** (Art. 92);
  employer's duty to return the worker to the contract location within three days of the end of the
  contract (Art. 93); joint liability where an employer assigns another employer to perform part of
  its work in the same area (Art. 94); repayment of training expenses if a trained worker leaves
  before the agreed period (Art. 95).

*"New work models" (Art. 96–100) — a genuinely new category:*
- **Art. 96** defines **new work models** as remote work, part-time work, flexible work, job-sharing,
  and any other form identified by ministerial decision.
- **Art. 97** — traditional labour-relations provisions apply to new work models; such workers have
  **the same rights, including social protection, social security, minimum wages, vocational
  training, collective bargaining and freedom of association** under the Trade Unions Law
  (Law No. 213 of 2017).
- **Art. 99** — the relationship must be in a written contract, **paper or electronic**.

*Wages (Arts. 101–107):*
- **Art. 101** — reconstitutes the **National Wages Council** (المجلس القومي للأجور) under the
  Minister of Planning, with 6 employer and 6 worker representatives among its members.
- **Art. 102** — the Council sets the **national minimum wage** and the **minimum annual periodic
  raise (not less than the percentage in Art. 12 of the Law)**, and may grant employers' requests to
  reduce or be exempted from the periodic raise in exceptional economic circumstances.
- **Art. 104** — establishments subject to the law are **obliged to implement the Council's
  decisions**; the Ministry inspects to verify this.
- **Art. 106** — decisions on reduction/exemption may be **challenged before the labour court
  within 30 days** of the scheduled disbursement date or notification.
- **Art. 107** — where the wage is not fixed by contract, collective agreement or internal
  regulations, the worker is entitled to the **prevailing wage for similar work**; failing that, the
  **customary rate in the sector**; failing that, the **summary judge of the competent labour
  court** fixes the wage on the basis of justice.

*Working hours / rest — `PARTIALLY UNVERIFIED`:*
The Law 14/2025 English translation retrieved contains Book structures up to Art. 60 (women/children)
and Arts. 86–190 (contracts, wages, discipline, termination, labour courts) but the retrieved text
did not yield a clean, citable article-by-article statement of the daily/weekly hour limits,
overtime rates or annual-leave scale for the **new** law. **Do not quote a Law 14/2025 working-hour
or leave article number without primary-source check.** For the prior (now repealed) Law 12/2003
schedule, see the repeal note below.

*Women (Book Two, Part Two, Chapter Three, Arts. 53–60) — all verified:*
- **Art. 53** — all employment provisions apply to women without discrimination; **equal pay for
  equal value**; the Minister, after consulting the National Council for Women and the National
  Council for Childhood and Motherhood, decides **situations or jobs in which women may not be
  employed** (maternity protection, occupational health and safety).
- **Art. 54** — **maternity leave of four months**, including before and after delivery, of which
  **not less than 45 days must be after delivery**; paid; medical certificate of probable delivery
  date required; **not more than three times** over the employment. The employer's wage obligation
  is **reduced by the compensation payable under Art. 77 of the Social Insurance Law No. 148 of
  2019**. **Working hours of a pregnant woman are reduced by at least one hour from the sixth month
  of pregnancy**, and **no overtime throughout pregnancy and for six months after delivery.**
- **Art. 55** — right to return to the original or a similar post with no loss of benefits.
  **Dismissal during maternity leave is prohibited.** Dismissal after return from maternity leave is
  **prohibited unless the employer proves a legitimate reason**. If the woman worked for another
  employer during the leave, the employer may deny or recover the compensation, without prejudice to
  disciplinary action.
- **Art. 56** — breastfeeding worker for two years after delivery: **two additional breaks of not
  less than 30 minutes each**, combinable, counted as working hours, **without pay reduction**.
- **Art. 57** — unpaid **child-care leave of up to two years** for a female worker in an
  establishment of **50 or more workers**, **available at most three times**, after at least one year
  of service, with at least a two-year gap between the first and second periods. Subject to Art. 72(2)
  of the Child Law (Law No. 12 of 1996).
- **Art. 58** — a female worker may **terminate her contract by reason of marriage, pregnancy or
  childbirth**, provided she notifies the employer **in writing within three months** of the marriage,
  confirmation of pregnancy, or delivery.
- **Art. 59** — the employer employing one or more women must display a copy of the women's
  employment system at the workplace, including procedures for **flexible or remote working for
  women caring for children with disabilities or dwarfism**.
- **Art. 60** — an employer with **100 or more female workers at a single site** must establish or
  contract a **nursery** (or bear the childcare costs); establishments in the same area each with
  fewer than 100 female workers must collaborate.

*Children (Chapter Four, Arts. 61–68) — minimum age:*
- **Art. 61** — a "child" for the purposes of this Law is **anyone under eighteen**. The Child Law
  applies save where the Labour Law says otherwise.
- **Art. 62** — **it is prohibited to employ children before the age of fifteen.** Training is
  permitted **from the age of fourteen**, provided it does not hinder continued education; the
  employer must issue a training card with the child's photograph, stamped by the competent
  administrative authority.
- **Art. 63** — conditions and procedures for child employment/training set by ministerial decision
  in coordination with the National Council for Childhood and Motherhood.
- **Art. 64** — prohibited: work endangering physical or mental health, safety or morals or
  hindering education; **illegal work**; and the **worst forms of child labour** under international
  instruments ratified by Egypt.
- **Art. 65** — a child **may not work more than six hours per day**; breaks of at least one hour in
  total; **no more than four consecutive hours** working; **no overtime, no work on weekly rest days
  or public holidays**; and **it is prohibited to employ a child between 7:00 PM and 7:00 AM.**
- **Art. 66** — employers must display the chapter's provisions at the workplace, keep an
  authority-approved register of working hours and breaks, notify the authority of the children's
  names, tasks and supervisors, and provide **separate accommodation** from adult workers.
- **Art. 67–68** — disability rehabilitation and parental liability.

*Foreigners (Chapter Five, Arts. 69–74):*
- **Art. 70** — employment of foreigners governed "with due regard for the principle of reciprocity";
  the Minister may exempt foreigners from the reciprocity condition.
- **Art. 71** — a **work permit** is mandatory before working; fees **not less than EGP 5,000 and not
  more than EGP 150,000**; notification within **seven days** for permit-exempt foreign workers.

*Discipline and dismissal (Arts. 137–148) — this is the core of the answer to the brief's
"irrebuttable presumption" question:*
- **Art. 137** — every employer with **10 or more workers** must have internal work regulations
  covering promotion, transfer, wages, job-duty violations and **disciplinary sanctions**; prepared
  within **60 days**; submitted to the administrative authority; the labour union is consulted and
  has **15 days** to respond (silence = approval); if the authority neither approves nor objects
  within **30 days**, the regulations are deemed effective; must be posted.
- **Art. 138** — a disciplinary violation must be **work-related**; **no disciplinary sanction may
  be imposed more than 30 days after the investigation is completed.**
- **Art. 139** — the closed list of permissible disciplinary penalties: written warning; deduction
  from basic wage; **postponement of the annual raise for up to 3 months**; deprivation of up to
  **half** the annual raise; **delay in promotion up to 1 year**; reduction of basic wage by no more
  than one increment; demotion to the immediately lower grade without wage reduction; and
  **termination of service** in accordance with the law.
- **Art. 140** — **no more than one sanction for the same violation**; wage deduction may not be
  combined with another financial penalty if the total exceeds **five days' wages per month**.
- **Art. 141** — **no disciplinary sanction may be imposed unless** the worker was (i) **notified in
  writing** of the alleged violation, (ii) **heard and given the opportunity to defend**, and (iii)
  a record of the investigation is kept in the personal file. Investigation must **begin within 7
  days** of discovery and be **completed within 3 months**, extendable by up to 3 more months.
  The worker's **labour union may attend**. For warning or deduction ≤ one day's wage the
  investigation may be oral. **The sanction decision must be reasoned and justified.**
- **Art. 143** — a wage-deduction penalty for a single violation may not exceed **five days' basic
  wage**; total deductions in one month may not exceed five days' basic wage.
- **Art. 144** — a penalty may be **increased** for a repeat of the same type of violation within
  **one year** of notification of the prior penalty.
- **Art. 145–147** — provisional suspension by written decision **for up to 60 days with full pay**
  in three cases: (1) referral for investigation where the investigation's interests require it;
  (2) accusation of a **felony or a misdemeanor involving dishonesty, breach of trust, public
  morality or any other crime committed in the workplace**; (3) the employer asks the labour court
  to dismiss the worker. Appeal to the **summary judge of the labour court within 3 days**; the
  judge rules **by the following day** (Art. 146). Extension on application at least 10 days before
  expiry, at half pay (Art. 147). If charges are dropped, a dismissal order issued, or the worker
  acquitted, **the worker must be reinstated with all withheld wages; refusal is unlawful dismissal.**
- **Art. 148 — the dismissal bar.** *"(1) Only the competent labour court has jurisdiction to impose
  the penalty of dismissal. (2) All other disciplinary penalties may be imposed by the employer or a
  delegated authority. (3) The establishment's manager may impose written warnings and deductions not
  exceeding three days' wages. (4) In all cases, an employee may only be dismissed for serious
  misconduct."* The seven enumerated serious-misconduct cases are: **(a)** proven impersonation or
  submission of falsified documents; **(b)** an act causing serious damage to the employer, **provided
  the employer notifies the authorities within 24 hours of learning of it**; **(c)** repeated failure
  to observe **written, visibly posted** safety rules despite written warnings; **(d)** disclosing
  trade secrets causing substantial harm; **(e)** competing with the employer's business;
  **(f)** being intoxicated or under the influence of drugs during working hours; **(g)** assaulting
  the employer, the general manager, or a supervisor at or because of work. Closing words:
  *"In all circumstances, dismissal must comply with this law's provisions."*

> **On the "irrebuttable presumption" (القرينة القاطعة) formulation in the brief:**
> `UNVERIFIED — needs primary-source check.` Neither the Law 12/2003 nor the Law 14/2025 texts
> retrieved contains a provision framed as an irrebuttable presumption that no penalty may be
> imposed, and ILO EPLex explicitly states: *"No statutory provisions were found in the examined
> legislation in this respect."* The **functionally equivalent, verified** rules are: (a) the
> **closed list** of serious misconduct in Art. 148 — an employer cannot dismiss outside it; (b) the
> **unconditional prohibition** on dismissal for the reasons in Art. 165; and (c) the **procedural
> preconditions** in Art. 141, without which no disciplinary sanction at all is lawful. An agent
> should describe the legal effect (no penalty/dismissal outside the statutory list or for the
> protected reasons) and **not** use the term "irrebuttable presumption" as a citation.

*Termination and severance (Arts. 154–175):*
- **Art. 156** — either party may terminate an indefinite contract on **three months' written
  notice**.
- **Art. 158** — notice may not be made conditional (suspensive or resolutory condition).
- **Art. 159** — **notice may not be given during the employee's leave**; the notice period starts
  the day after the leave ends; sick leave suspends the notice period.
- **Art. 161** — the notice period **may not be waived or reduced**; only extended by agreement. If
  the **employee** initiates, the employer may waive it wholly or partly.
- **Art. 162** — if the employer gives notice, the employee gets **one full day per week (8 hours)
  off with full pay** to look for another job.
- **Art. 165** — if the employer terminates an indefinite contract **without a legitimate reason**, the
  employee is entitled to compensation **of not less than two months' wages for each year of service**,
  without prejudice to other entitlements. **Unjustified reasons are listed exhaustively**:
  (a) **trade-union affiliation or participation in union activities under this law**;
  (b) serving or having served as a union representative, or seeking such a role;
  (c) **filing a complaint or initiating legal action against the employer**, or participating in
  such actions, regarding violations of law, regulations or employment contracts;
  (d) **attachment (garnishment) of the employee's wages by court order**; (e) **exercising the right
  to statutory leave**; (f) colour, gender, marital status, family responsibilities, pregnancy,
  religion or political opinion.
- **Art. 166** — **constructive resignation by absence**: more than **20 non-consecutive days** in a
  year, or more than **10 consecutive days**, after prior warning by registered letter with
  acknowledgement of receipt (sent after 10 days' absence in the first case and after 5 days in the
  second).
- **Art. 167** — resignation must be **written and approved by the relevant administrative
  authority**; service is deemed terminated only on acceptance; the employer must decide within
  **10 days**, otherwise it is deemed accepted; the worker may **retract within 10 days** of
  notification of acceptance, in writing and approved by the authority, and the resignation is then
  **null and void**.
- **Art. 168** — where the employer breaches an essential obligation or assaults the worker or a
  family member, the worker's termination is **equivalent to termination by the employer without
  just cause**.

*Labour courts, settlement committee, appeal (Arts. 149–150, 185–187):*
- **Art. 149** — either party may request **amicable settlement within 10 days** of the dispute; the
  **settlement committee** comprises the labour directorate director or delegate (Chair), the
  employee or representative, and the employer or representative; must conclude within **21 days**;
  a settlement report is referred to the **summary judge of the competent labour court**, whose
  approval makes it **enforceable**.
- **Art. 150** — if unresolved, the Chair's report goes to the labour court on either party's
  request; the court must **set a hearing within 20 days** of referral. **If the dispute concerns
  dismissal, the court must render an urgent decision within 3 months of the first hearing**; if
  there is merit in the employee's claim, the employer may be ordered to pay **wages from the date of
  dismissal up to a maximum of six months**, and **that ruling shall be final**. If the dismissal was
  due to **union activity, the court shall order reinstatement** on the employee's request.
- **Art. 185** — a specialised appeal circuit ("**دائرة استئنافية متخصصة**") is created in each Court
  of Appeal to hear appeals against specialised labour court judgments.
- **Art. 186** — one or more **circuits in the Court of Cassation** have exclusive competence over
  cassation in labour court judgments, and one or more **circuits for the screening (فحص) of those
  appeals**: if the screening circuit finds the appeal inadmissible or not admissible on the grounds
  in **Art. 263 of Law No. 13 of 1968 (Rules of Procedure)**, it dismisses it with a reasoned
  decision; if admissible, it refers it to the competent circuit. Screening circuits are composed of
  **three judges of at least Vice-President rank**, and the case is laid before them as soon as the
  Public Prosecution deposits its opinion. **No appeal lies from a screening circuit's decision by
  any route.** By way of exception to Art. 269(2) of Law No. 13 of 1968, **where the Court of
  Cassation annuls the judgment appealed against, it adjudicates the merits of the action even if the
  appeal is the first one.**
- **Art. 187** — otherwise the procedural laws (Criminal Procedure, Rules of Procedure, Law No. 13 of
  1968, Evidence Law) apply to labour appeals insofar as the Labour Law does not provide.

*Apprenticeship, unorganised labour, foreign nationals — see the Art. 27–33, 40–52, 75–85 headings
above; notable items:*
- **Art. 28** — a trainee must be **at least 14**.
- **Art. 43** — private recruitment agencies are **prohibited from charging the worker** any fee
  (exception: up to **1% of the worker's wage for the first year**, for administrative expenses).
- **Art. 45** — **establishments are prohibited from employing workers through a contractor or labour
  supply subcontractor.** (A significant departure from a common-law agency-worker model.)
- **Arts. 78–85** — the **Emergency Aid and Social and Health Services Fund for Unorganised Labor**
  (صندوق الإعانات والمساعدات الاجتماعية والصحية لعمال العمل غير المنظم), a public-law fund with
  resources from 1–3% of unorganised workers' wages in construction and mining, registration fees
  of EGP 20–200 per month, 0.5% of state purchases of agricultural products, and similar.

**Amendments:** Law 14/2025 itself repeals Law 12/2003 and Law 125/2010 (art. 12). Its art. 11
contemplates executive regulations within 90 days of entry into force; the labour sector did **not**
receive a single omnibus executive statute within that window — instead the implementing instrument
found is **Minister of Labour Decree No. 162 of 2026** (see next block). Labour Law No. 12 of 2003
had itself been amended by **Law No. 180 of 2008** (amending arts. 70–71 — the committee system in
the disciplinary/dismissal chapter was replaced by the labour court) and **Law No. 125 of 2010** on
the precedence of labour rights, both now repealed.

### Labour Law No. 12 of 2003 (قانون العمل) — REPEALED, retained for transitional matters

**Citation:** Law No. 12 of 2003 promulgating the Labour Law — *قانون رقم 12 لسنة 2003 بإصدار قانون
العمل*. Promulgated at the Presidency of the Republic on **5 Safar 1424 H / 7 April 2003**; published
in the **Official Gazette issue No. 14 (مكرر) in April 2003**; in force 90 days after publication.
It repealed Labour Law No. 137 of 1981.

**Status:** **REPEALED** by Law 14/2025 art. 12, with effect from 1 September 2025. Still relevant for
(a) disputes filed before that date, (b) the pending-case referral under Law 14/2025 art. 9,
(c) pre-2025 employment contracts and accrued rights (Law 14/2025 art. 4 protects previously
acquired wages and benefits).

**Key provisions (from the English text at sadanykhalifa.com and the Arabic text):**
- **Art. 1** — definitions of *worker*, *employer*, *wage* (commission, percentage, allowances,
  in-kind benefits, grants, allowances, profit share, gratuities); *temporary work* (عمل مؤقت),
  *casual/incidental work* (عمل عرضي, **not exceeding six months**), *seasonal work* (عمل موسمي),
  *night* (**between sunset and sunrise**).
- **Art. 3** — the "general law governing work relations", subject to collective agreements and
  art. 5.
- **Art. 4** — the law does **not** apply to (a) employees of state bodies including local
  administration units and public authorities, (b) domestic workers and their like, (c) family
  members whom the employer actually supports, unless otherwise provided.
- **Art. 5** — **any condition or agreement contrary to the law is void**, even if made earlier, if
  it derogates from the worker's rights.
- **Art. 6** — actions brought by workers, trainees, juvenile trainees and industrial apprentices or
  their beneficiaries are **exempt from judicial fees at all stages**; the court may in all cases
  combine its judgment with self-execution without bail.
- **Art. 7** — **amounts due to the worker or their beneficiaries enjoy a lien over all the debtor's
  movables**, collected directly after judicial expenses and State Treasury amounts; **the wage ranks
  ahead of the other rights**.
- **Promulgating law arts. 3–5** — a **periodical annual increment of not less than 7%** of the basic
  wage on which social insurance contributions are reckoned, until the National Council for Wages
  regulates it (carried into Law 14/2025 as the Art. 12 percentage referenced in its Art. 102);
  previously acquired wages and benefits preserved (art. 4); fines go two-thirds to social purposes
  and one-third to the General Federation of Egyptian Trade Unions' cultural/social labour
  institutions (art. 5).
- **Art. 27** — employment of foreigners subject to the **principle of reciprocity (شرط المعاملة
  بالمثل)**; the competent minister may exempt foreigners from that condition.
- **Art. 34** — National Council for Wages; **Art. 35** — **discrimination in wages on grounds of
  sex, origin, language, religion or creed is prohibited**.
- **Discipline (Arts. 58–69):** internal "work and disciplinary sanctions" regulations required for
  establishments with **10 or more workers**; **Art. 59** — a violation must be **work-related**;
  **no disciplinary sanction after the lapse of more than 30 days from completing the
  investigation**; **Art. 60** — the eight-item closed list of sanctions (identical in substance to
  Law 14/2025 Art. 139); **Arts. 61–62** — max five days' wage deduction per offence and per month,
  one sanction per offence; **Art. 63** — penalty may be **aggravated for a repeat within six months**
  (Law 14/2025 raised this to one year); **Art. 64** — written notification, hearing, defence,
  report in file, investigation to begin within at most 7 days; union representative may attend;
  oral investigation allowed for warning or ≤ one day's deduction; **the sanction decision must be
  substantiated**; **Art. 66** — provisional suspension up to 60 days with full pay; **Art. 67** —
  suspension in criminal cases referred within 3 days; if charges are dropped or the worker acquitted,
  return to work with full dues, else **arbitrary discharge**.
- **Art. 68** — **jurisdiction to impose dismissal lies with the committee under Art. 71**; other
  sanctions with the employer or delegate; the establishment director may impose warning and
  deductions of **not more than three days**.
- **Art. 69** — **a worker may not be discharged unless he commits a serious error**, with **nine**
  enumerated cases (false identity/documents; error causing serious damage, employer must notify the
  authorities **within 24 hours**; repeated breach of written, visibly posted safety instructions;
  unexcused absence of more than 20 intermittent days or more than 10 consecutive days after a
  registered-letter warning sent 10 days / 5 days respectively; disclosure of establishment secrets
  causing serious damage; competing with the employer; drunkenness or intoxication during working
  hours; aggression on the employer, general manager or superiors; failure to observe the controls in
  arts. 192–194 of Book Four).
- **Art. 70** — request to the administrative directorate within **7 days** for amicable settlement;
  if not settled within **10 days**, recourse to the Art. 71 committee **within 45 days from the date
  of the dispute, otherwise the right is forfeited.**
- **Art. 71** — the **judicial committees**: two judges (the senior presiding, appointed per the
  Judicial Authority Law), the manpower directorate director or delegate, a member of the General
  Federation of Egyptian Trade Unions, and a member of the employers' organisation. The committee
  decides within **60 days**; a **dismissal request is decided within 15 days from the first
  session and the decision is final**; refusal obliges the employer to reinstate and pay unpaid
  entitlements; **failure to execute a reinstatement decision is an arbitrary discharge compensable
  under Art. 122**; provisional compensation on request is **self-executing forthwith even if
  appealed**; **where the dismissal request is due to union activity the committee shall order
  return to work unless the employer proves otherwise**.
- **Art. 72** — the committee's decision is by majority and reasoned; it is **equivalent to a
  First Instance judgment** after the clerk's office of the concerned First Instance Court sets the
  operative wording; appeal lies to the Court of Appeal under the Rules of Procedure. Where no
  special rule applies, the Civil and Commercial Procedure and Evidence Laws govern.
- **Art. 73** — employee liability for damage to tools/machines/materials/products; employer may
  deduct up to five days' wages per month; worker may challenge the valuation before the Art. 71
  committee; refund within 7 days if the committee rejects the employer's figure.
- **Art. 120** — **reasons that are NOT legitimate and sufficient justification for termination**:
  (A) colour, sex, social status, family obligations, pregnancy, religion, political view; (B)
  union affiliation or union activity within the law's limits; (C) exercising or seeking the quality
  of workers' representative; (D) submitting a complaint, filing an action or joining in it against
  the employer; (E) garnishment of the worker's dues by the employer; (F) the worker exercising
  leave rights.
- **Art. 122** — unjustified termination by the employer: compensation **not less than two months'
  comprehensive wage per year of service**.
- **Child labour (Arts. 98–102, per the ILO National Action Plan 2018–2025):** **Art. 98 sets the
  minimum age for child employment at 14**; **Art. 99** the employer must give the working child a
  card with photograph approved by the labour office; **Art. 100** procedures and permitted
  occupations; **Art. 101** working hours (the law **excludes children working in agriculture** from
  these hours rules); **Art. 102** display of the chapter's provisions, a written schedule of working
  hours and breaks, and notification of the authority with names, tasks and supervisors.
  **Minister of Manpower Decree No. 118 of 2003** prohibits employment of under-18s in **44 listed
  professions/industries** (mining, metals smelting, explosives, glass, alcohol, paints, mercury
  etc.). Note the **conflict** with the **Child Law No. 12 of 1996 as amended by Law No. 126 of 2008,
  Art. 64, which sets the employment floor at 15 calendar years** — the higher figure has generally
  been treated as controlling; `see open questions`.

**Amendments:** Law No. 180 of 2008 (arts. 70 and 71 — replaced the pre-2008 special disciplinary
committee, which consisted of two judges, the manpower directorate head or delegate, a FTU
representative and an employers' representative, with the **Labour Court** replacing it) and Law No.
125 of 2010. **All now repealed by Law 14/2025.**

### Social Insurance and Pensions Law No. 148 of 2019 (قانون التأمينات الاجتماعية والمعاشات)

**Citation:** Law No. 148 of 2019 promulgating the Social Insurance and Pensions Law — *قانون رقم 148
لسنة 2019 بإصدار قانون التأمينات الاجتماعية والمعاشات*. Adopted **19 August 2019**; promulgated at
the Presidency of the Republic; **published in the Official Gazette on 19 August 2019**, per Lexology.
**In force from 1 January 2020**, except Arts. 111–114 which took effect the day after publication
(art. 7 of the promulgating law).

**Status:** **In force and now the single umbrella statute** for social insurance in Egypt. It
**replaced a fragmented regime**: previously workers were under **Law No. 79 of 1975**, employers and
business owners under **Law No. 108 of 1976**, and Egyptians working abroad under **Law No. 50 of
1978** (Amereller & Zaghloul, Lexology, 13 Feb 2020). Egyptians working abroad **may opt not to
subscribe**.

**Executive Regulations:** **Prime Minister Decree No. 2437 of 2021**, issued **28 September 2021**,
enforceable from the following day (i.e. 29 September 2021). Promulgating art. 5 had allowed six
months from August 2019; until the ER issued, **the regulations and decisions implementing the
repealed social insurance legislation continued in force so far as not inconsistent** (art. 4).

**Key provisions:**
- **Art. 1** — definitions, incl. *Contribution Wage* (أجر الاشتراك) which aggregates functional
  wage, basic wage, **complementary wage**, incentives, commissions, tips (on conditions), allowances,
  **overtime**, compensation for extraordinary effort, cost-of-living allowance, social and
  additional bonuses, collective grants and bonuses, **any excess over the maximum basic wage**, and
  special bonuses not added to basic wage; *Inflation Rate* (CAPMAS general consumer price index,
  determined each April); *Actuarial Discount Rate* = inflation rate + 1%; *Retirement Age* = **60**
  for Categories First and Third, **65** for Categories Second and Fourth (subject to Art. 41);
  *Work Injury* = occupational disease in **Schedule 1** or a work/commuting accident.
- **Art. 2 — four categories of insured:**
  - **First — employees working for others:** (1) civilian employees of the State Administrative
    Apparatus, public authorities, public institutions and affiliated economic units; (2) employees
    of public-sector and public-business-sector units and affiliated economic units, **including
    chairpersons and delegated board members of public business sector companies**; also applies to
    **temporary, casual and seasonal workers** of those entities; (3) **private-sector employees
    subject to the Labour Law, where the employment relationship is regular** — the regularity
    condition **does not apply to construction workers, stevedores (loading/unloading), fishermen and
    land-transport workers**; (4) persons engaged in **domestic work** (except in-house as specified
    by the ER); (5) **family members whom the employer actually supports**, on the item-(3)
    conditions. Items (3), (4), (5) require the insured to be **at least 18**. Where a person works
    for more than one employer, **only the period with one employer counts**.
  - **Second — employers and those treated as such (17 types)**: self-employed persons in commercial,
    industrial, agricultural or craft activities or providing services independently (conditional on
    the activity being legislated or licence-requiring); general partners, chairpersons and board
    members of private joint-stock companies and members of partnerships limited by shares, and
    managers of limited liability companies; owners of one-person companies; liberal professionals
    and **members of professional syndicates** (date fixed by decision of the Authority's Chairman);
    productive members of cooperative production societies; **owners/tenants/sharecroppers of
    agricultural land of at least one feddan**; owners of built real estate whose annual income share
    is not less than the minimum contributory wage; owners of motor vehicles for transport; commercial
    agents; owners of fishing boats; marriage registrars and delegated notaries; **village mayors and
    tribal chiefs**; tourist guides, trackers, heritage scouts; **authors and artists**; heirs of
    business owners in sole proprietorships; home-based/environmental/rural/family industries;
    ordained priests and deacons. **Minimum age 21**, and the person must not be in Category First.
  - **Third — Egyptian workers abroad:** those under individual employment contracts; the
    self-employed; emigrants retaining Egyptian nationality; **maritime workers on foreign-flagged
    vessels during the seafarer's passport validity**; and Egyptians employed by international or
    regional organisations or foreign embassies in Egypt who are not subject to the Labour Law
    (treated as working abroad). **Insurance is OPTIONAL for this category** (Art. 4) — the Prime
    Minister may make it mandatory in countries where Egyptian workers are not covered locally.
  - **Fourth — irregular employment:** built-real-estate owners below the minimum contributory wage;
    **itinerant workers**; small self-employed (street vendors, car guides, newspaper distributors,
    shoe shiners); **domestic workers and similar in-house workers**; Quran reciters and teachers;
    church chanters and caretakers; qualifying heirs of sole proprietors; **temporary agricultural
    workers** (defined as employment with a single employer of **less than six consecutive months**
    or work not part of the employer's core activity); holders of agricultural land **below one
    feddan**; **social pioneers**. **Minimum age 18.**
- **Art. 3** — four branches: **old-age, disability and death**; **work injury**; **sickness**;
  **unemployment**.
- **Art. 4** — insurance is **mandatory** except Category Third (optional). **No share of
  insurance expenses may be imposed on the insured unless expressly provided by law**, and no
  insured or pensioner may be deprived of entitlements for any reason.
- **Art. 7** — actuarial review **at least every three years** by a committee of experts, reporting
  to the House of Representatives; funding principles per branch; where cash flow is insufficient,
  **the State Treasury provides the funds** and the Authority reimburses per an arrangement with
  the Ministry of Finance approved by the Cabinet.
- **Art. 8** — the **National Organization for Social Insurance (NOSI)** (الهيئة القومية للتأمين
  الاجتماعي) has legal personality, an independent budget, and technical, financial and
  administrative independence, reporting to the Minister responsible for social insurance.
- **Contribution rates (Art. 19, old-age/disability/death):** Category First — **employer 12%**,
  **employee 9%** of monthly wages. Categories Second and Third — **21%** of the contributory income.
  Category Fourth — **insured 9%** and **State Treasury 12%** of the minimum contributory wage.
  **Rates rise by 1% every seven years**, shared equally, **capped at 26%**. Law-firm alerts
  additionally describe an **end-of-service bonus** of 1% employer / 1% employee layered on top
  (making 18.75% employer / 11% employee in aggregate for private-sector employees, per Amereller &
  Zaghloul) — **treat the end-of-service-bonus allocation and the sickness/unemployment rates as
  medium confidence pending the Arabic text of Art. 19, 46, 70 and 86.**
- **Pension formula:** the minimum pension is **65% of the minimum social insurance salary**,
  increased annually in June (or 30 July per one source — `UNVERIFIED, conflicting dates`) by the
  inflation rate **capped at 15%**; a minimum actual subscription period of **120 months, rising to
  180 months by 2025.**
- **Construction contractors:** the ER imposes social insurance percentages on construction contracts
  whether governmental or private (Shalakany; mnasserlaw).

**Amendments / transitional provisions relevant to the "which law applies to whom" question:**
- **Promulgating art. 3** — does not prejudice the laws on **special employment cadres (وظائف خاصة)**;
  benefits under those laws continue and the State Treasury bears the cost differences.
- **Promulgating art. 4** — prior regulations and decisions continue until the ER issued.
- **Promulgating art. 6** — conflicting provisions repealed in so far as they apply to the new law's subjects.
- The **sickness branch applies progressively** to insured persons identified by decision of the
  **Chairman of the Health Insurance Authority**, **"without prejudice to the rights of insured
  persons who previously benefited from sickness insurance under Law No. 79 of 1975."** The same
  saving formula appears for the chapter on pensioners and eligible dependents.
- **The ILO EPLex and law-firm materials do not state a simple employer-size threshold** (e.g. "below
  X employees Law 79/1975 still applies"). The transition is by **category of insured person and by
  progressive inclusion decisions**, not by employer headcount. **A headline "which system applies to
  which employer size" answer is `UNVERIFIED` — see Open Questions.** A Nexis/Andersen employment
  note does describe Law 148/2019 as covering "permanent and temporary employees in both the
  private and public sectors from their employers," which is consistent with the single-basket design.
- A **2025 amendment is confirmed**: **NOSI Decision No. 4824 of 2025** fixing the date from which
  **seafarers** begin to benefit from Law 148/2019, **issued 27 July 2025, published in the Egyptian
  Official Gazette issue No. 209 on 20 September 2025, effective as of 1 August 2025** (per Lex
  Council's consolidated text).

## PART 2 — COURTS & PROCEDURE

### Civil and Commercial Procedure Law (Rules of Procedure) No. 13 of 1968 (قانون المرافعات المدنية والتجارية)

**Citation:** Law No. 13 of 1968 promulgating the Civil and Commercial Procedure Law — *قانون رقم 13
لسنة 1968 بإصدار قانون المرافعات المدنية والتجارية*. Entered into force **six months after
publication** (promulgating art. 4).

**Status:** **In force.** It repealed the Civil and Commercial Procedure Law promulgated by **Law No.
77 of 1949**, **except** Chapter Seven of Book One (evidentiary procedures), Chapter Two of Part
Twelve of Book One (opposition proceedings), and **Arts. 868–1032 of Book Four** (personal status
procedures); and Part One of the Law on Modes and Procedures of Challenge before the Court of
Cassation. Those savings have been replaced by the accompanying law. All conflicting provisions
repealed.

**Key provisions (all article numbers from the Andersen Egypt English translation and the Arabic
consolidated text):**
- **Promulgating art. 2** — courts **of their own motion, free of fees**, refer any pending case that
  has fallen within another court's jurisdiction to that court **in the condition in which it
  stands**; where a party is absent, the court registry serves the referral order and summons the
  party. This does **not** apply to cases already judged or adjourned for judgment, which remain
  subject to the former provisions (art. 2 ¶2).
- **Promulgating art. 3** — enforcement against immovable property continues under the former law
  where an auction judgment was rendered while that law was in force.
- **Art. 1** — **procedural laws apply to cases not yet adjudicated and procedures not yet completed**
  before their entry into force, except: laws amending **jurisdiction** (only if entry into force is
  after close of pleadings); laws amending **time limits** (only if the time limit began before);
  laws governing **methods of appeal** for judgments rendered before entry into force, where they
  abolish or establish a method of appeal. Any procedural measure validly taken under a former law
  remains valid; a **newly introduced time limit resulting in forfeiture applies only from the date
  the introducing law enters into force**.
- **Art. 3** — **personal, direct and existing interest** is required; a **potential interest**
  suffices to prevent imminent harm or preserve evidence. The court rules of its own motion at any
  stage. Where inadmissibility is for want of interest and the claimant abused the right to litigate,
  a procedural fine **not exceeding EGP 500** may be imposed.
- **Art. 42** — the **District Court (محكمة المواد الجزئية)** has first-instance civil and commercial
  jurisdiction over claims whose **value does not exceed EGP 200,000**; its judgment is **final where
  the value does not exceed EGP 30,000**, and final irrespective of value in an enumerated list of
  subject matters.
- **Art. 47** — the **Court of First Instance (محكمة الابتدائية)** has first-instance jurisdiction over
  all civil and commercial actions not within the District Court's jurisdiction; **its judgment is
  final where the value does not exceed EGP 200,000**; it hears appeals against District Court and
  judge-of-urgent-matters judgments; and it has jurisdiction over provisional/urgent applications,
  incidental applications, and applications connected with the principal claim **irrespective of
  value or nature**.
- **Art. 48** — the **Court of Appeal (محكمة الاستئناف)** hears appeals against Court of First
  Instance judgments and against District Court judgments in the actions listed at **Art. 43(6)**.
- **The judge of summary/urgent matters** at the Court of First Instance is the president of that
  court, the person acting in his place, or a judge designated for that purpose; at a District Court
  of minor civil matters the judge of urgent matters is the judge of that court.
- **Jurisdiction (territorial)** — unless otherwise provided by law, jurisdiction lies in the court
  within whose **territorial district the defendant is domiciled**. Proceedings before the **Court of
  Cassation and the Court of Jurisdictional Conflicts (محكمةxicdfs الاختصاص)** are excluded from the
  general rule.
- **Pleading with prejudice** — a plea challenging territorial jurisdiction, a plea seeking referral
  because the same dispute is pending or proceedings are related, a plea of nullity and all other
  procedural pleas **must be raised together before any claim or defence on the merits or any plea of
  inadmissibility; otherwise the right is forfeited.**
- **Where a court rules it lacks jurisdiction** (including for absence of judicial authority or
  nature or value of the action) it orders **referral of the action, in the condition in which it
  stands, to the competent court**, even where the defect is one of judicial authority.
- **Art. 65 / appearance periods** — **fifteen days** before the Court of First Instance and the
  Court of Appeal, **eight days** before the District Courts; in urgent matters these may be
  reduced to three days and twenty-four hours respectively, and in **actions of summary urgency
  (الدعاوى المستعجلة) to twenty-four hours**, reducible from one hour to one hour in cases of
  necessity with personal service on the opponent.
- **Service of the statement of claim (Iqlam da'wa)** — the **court bailiff (قلم المحضرين)** must
  effect service within **no more than 30 days** of receipt, unless a hearing is fixed within that
  period, in which case service must occur before the hearing.
- **Hearing** — pleadings are heard at the **first session**; documents may still be filed by the
  deadline in Art. 65 if the adjournment does not follow; if it does and the court accepts in the
  interests of justice, a fine of **not less than EGP 100 and not more than EGP 500** is imposed,
  with **adjournment only once and not exceeding two weeks**; **adjournment may not exceed once for
  the same reason and not exceed three weeks in total** (Art. 98); non-compliance with a court-ordered
  deadline incurs a fine of **not less than EGP 20 and not more than EGP 200**, unappealable, with the
  option of a stay of the action for up to one month (Art. 99).
- **The general appeal period is forty days unless the law provides otherwise** (per the Consortio
  translation of Art. 745 area). **Verify the current text — `UNVERIFIED` for the exact article
  number and for the currently operative short periods for summary-court appeals and cassation
  (commonly cited as 15 days and 60 days respectively, but not verified here).**
- **Hearing is public (علنية)** unless the court, of its own motion or on a party's application,
  holds it **in camera (سرا)** to preserve public order, morals or family honour (Art. 101).
- **Art. 103** — parties may have agreed terms recorded in the minutes, signed by them or their
  representatives; the minutes have the force of an enforceable instrument. **Note:** agreements
  establishing rights in rem over immovable property must be registered to be recorded in the
  minutes.
- **Art. 104** — the presiding judge controls the sitting and may, subject to the Advocacy Law,
  order removal of anyone disrupting it, with the court's immediate order of up to **24 hours'
  imprisonment** or a fine of **EGP 5**, which is **final**; disciplinary responsibility for court
  staff.
- **Art. 107** — courts trying **contempt committed in their own sittings** (misdemeanours committed
  against the court or its members, and **perjury**, per Law No. 109 of 1951) — the judgment is
  effective **even if appealed**.
- **Distance allowance** — for a person whose domicile is abroad, **sixty days**.

### Court structure of Egypt

**Hierarchy (verified from multiple sources):**

| Level | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| Supreme | محكمة النقض | Court of Cassation |
| Appellate | محاكم الاستئناف | Courts of Appeal |
| First instance | محاكم الابتدائية | Courts of First Instance |
| Summary / minor | محاكم المواد الجزئية (محاكم الجزئية) | District / Summary Courts |
| Enforcement | محاكم التنفيذ | Execution Courts (judge of execution) |
| Specialist | محاكم economics economics (المحاكم الاقتصادية); المحاكم العمالية المتخصصة; مجلس الدولة | Economic Courts; Specialised Labour Courts; State Council |

**Specialised and parallel courts:**
- **State Council (مجلس الدولة)** — administrative adjudication; a court of Cassation within the
  administrative jurisdiction. Not part of the ordinary judicial hierarchy; final for administrative
  annulment actions.
- **Economic Courts (المحاكم الاقتصادية)** — created by **Law No. 120 of 2008**, substantially amended
  by **Law No. 146 of 2019** (issued 7 August 2019), which extended jurisdiction to Commercial
  Maritime Law and Civil Aviation disputes, made provision for electronic litigation, and expanded
  the methods of executing judgments.
- **Specialised Labour Courts (المحاكم العمالية المتخصصة)** — created by **Labour Law No. 14 of
  2025**, one in the territorial district of each Court of First Instance, **in force from
  1 October 2025**. Implemented by **Minister of Justice Decision No. 4621 of 2025** designating
  **38 specialised labour courts**, published in Al-Waqa'i Al-Misriyya, **effective 1 October 2025**.
  Each has its own **registry (قلم كتاب)**, an **enforcement department** for its own judgments, and
  a **labour legal aid office (مكتب المساعدة القانونية العمالية)**.
- **Specialised appeal circuits** within each Court of Appeal (Law 14/2025 art. 185) and **specialised
  Cassation circuits** within the Court of Cassation (art. 186) — see §Labour.
- **Family courts, child courts, criminal courts (جنح / جنح مستأنفة / جنايات), economic courts** —
  specialist first-instance courts.

**Amendment note:** **Minister of Justice Decision No. 5614 of 2025** (published in Al-Waqa'i
Al-Misriyya **issue No. 244 bis, 1 November 2025**) designated **additional venues for labour actions
inside the District Courts of certain Courts of First Instance** (e.g. the Central Safi District Court
building for South Giza). It expressly recites Decisions **4621/2025** and **4693/2025**.

**Court of Cassation:** hears appeals on **points of law, not facts** (per the Lexology Egypt
employment chapter). Cassation is by reference on a restricted set of grounds (Art. 263 of Law 13/1968
as cross-referenced by Law 14/2025 art. 186).

### Court of Cassation Technical Office and the publication of legal principles (المبادئ القانونية)

**Citation:** The **Office of Legal Principles (المكتب الفني للمبادئ القانونية)** at the Court of
Cassation. Created by a decision of the President of the Court of Cassation dated **12 October 1950**
per the Court's own publication, and vested by successive Judicial Authority laws; its mandate is
stated in **Art. 5 of the current Judicial Authority Law** (قانون السلطة القضائية): to **extract,
classify and publish the legal principles** established by the Court's judgments, and to issue
collections of judgments and legislative bulletins.

**Status:** Active. Outputs are published electronically on the Court's site **https://www.cc.gov.eg/**
and on its judgments-and-principles search platform **https://www.cc.gov.eg/judgments_search**, which
separates **civil** and **criminal** competence and returns "latest principles" by topic.

**Key facts on how principles are published and cited:**
- Judgments are collected into **numbered annual volumes ("مجموعة أحكام محكمة النقض عن السنة …")** by
  the Technical Office and indexed in both civil and criminal series. Verified examples: **Year 73
  (2022)**, published electronically, praised by the President of the Court of Cassation — who is
  also President of the **Supreme Judicial Council** — for its record production time; and the
  **relaunch of the Year 72 (2021) civil volume**. The Office has also issued a **"practical guide to
  preparing the annual civil volumes" (الدليل العملي لإعداد مجموعات أحكام محكمة النقض المدنية)**.
- A legislative bulletin (**النشرة التشريعية**) is also issued.
- The Court highlights the distinction between publishing a **judgment** and publishing a
  **principle**: an analysis in *al-M<Argument* law journal states that the Court publishes under the
  title "**مجموعة المبادئ القانونية التي أقرتها محكمة النقض**" whether from the **General Assembly
  (الهيئة العامة)** or the civil/criminal circuits, and that the Technical Office **exercises
  discretion not to publish** a judgment it does not consider fit to become a principle — precisely
  so that publishing does not convert case-specific justice into a binding rule. This is a critical
  point for an agent: **Egyptian Cassation "principles" are official-but-editorially-selected, are
  not formally binding precedent, and the annual volumes are the authoritative citation form.**
  They are used as persuasive authority ( persuasive / مبادئ قانونية ) and are what practitioners
  cite when advising.

### Execution and enforcement (محاكم التنفيذ / قاضي التنفيذ) and bailiffs (محضرين)

**Citation:** The **Judge of Execution (قاضي التنفيذ)** and the **Execution Courts (محاكم التنفيذ)**
operate under the **Civil and Commercial Procedure Law No. 13 of 1968**, and **Administrative Seizure
Law No. 308 of 1955** (قانون الحجز الإداري). `Specific article numbers within Law 13/1968 for the
execution judge's powers are `UNVERIFIED — needs primary-source check` — the retrieved English
translation excerpts did not surface them.`

**Status:** In force and now also integrated into the specialised labour courts, which each have
their **own enforcement department** for their own judgments (per Decision 4621/2025).

**Key features (from a Mandumah-indexed legal study, *الولاية القضائية لقاضي التنفيذ في ضوء القانون رقم
191 لسنة 2020 م. مقارنا بالفقه الإسلامي* — note this study title cites Law 191/2020 and needs
careful reading as to which jurisdiction's law it discusses; `the article's own content describes
Egyptian doctrine`):**
- The execution judge is a **stand-alone court with its own statutory jurisdiction**; its position
  is **analogous to the judge of urgent matters** at the Court of First Instance — i.e. there is
  **one execution judge per court district**, not one per District Court, although **execution
  courts are as numerous as District Courts**.
- **Functional (exclusive) jurisdiction:** execution matters relating to judgments of the **ordinary
  judiciary** and instruments given executive force under the Rules of Procedure. But it **also
  hears disputes relating to judgments of bodies other than the ordinary judiciary** (whether
  execution is against money or otherwise), and **all execution disputes of any kind** — civil,
  commercial, **labour**, or **personal status**, temporary or substantive — whether or not the
  legislator expressly assigned them to it. It issues all decisions and orders relating to execution.
- **Local jurisdiction:** for seizure of movables and immovables, the criterion is the **location of
  the property subject to execution** — the court nearest the place of execution; for seizure of what
  the garnishee owes or holds, the criterion is the **garnishee's domicile**.
- Judicial work done by the execution judge in exercise of **territorial** (jurisdictional) competence
  differs from the **functional/administrative** work he does as head of the enforcement department
  (وظيفة إدارية لإدارة التنفيذ).
- Work done by the bailiff in execution is **judicial work**, based on territorial competence, whereas
  the execution judge's work in execution disputes is based on **functional competence**.

**Bailiffs (محضرين):** the **court bailiff's office (قلم المحضرين)** serves the statement of claim
within no more than 30 days of receipt (Law 13/1968). Under the **Administrative Seizure Law No.
308 of 1955**, the **bailiff's deputy (مندوب الحاجز)** has been given by the legislator the powers
that the Rules of Procedure gave the bailiff for judicial seizure — **Art. 4** (to carry out the
seizure notice and warning, describing the amounts due and their kinds in the minutes); **Art. 5**
(breaking doors and locks to effect the seizure **only in the presence of a judicial police officer**);
**Art. 6** (inventory and setting the sale day, not earlier than **eight days** from seizure unless
the seized goods are perishable); **Art. 7** (notice to the debtor); **Art. 8** (seizure of attached
produce/crops standing is **void** if not less than 55 days have passed since ripening); **Art. 9**
(gold, silver, precious metals, jewellery, precious stones, and other small/valuable items valued by
an expert); **Art. 10** (the deputy may **take delivery of seized cash** and issue a receipt);
**Art. 11** (appointment of a custodian); **Art. 13** (custodian's powers); **Art. 12** (the custodian
may not use, exploit or rent out the seized items). **Art. 25** governs the interaction between
multiple judicial and administrative seizures.

### Electronic litigation — the actual Egyptian instruments

**Correction to the brief:** Egyptian **Law No. 172 of 2018 does NOT concern electronic
litigation** — it **amends certain provisions of the Customs Law promulgated by Law No. 66 of 1963**
(concerning temporary exemption from customs duty and other taxes for raw materials, intermediate
goods, components imported for manufacture, and inputs for repair or completion then re-exported).
Confirmed by two independent Egyptian sources (El-Watan News on the 2018 laws; the Arabic Wikipedia
list of Egyptian tax/customs/fee legislation, which places Law 172/2018 in the Customs Law column).
Egypt's electronic-litigation instruments are as follows.

**A. Economic Courts Law No. 120 of 2008, as amended by Law No. 146 of 2019 (issued 7 August 2019)**
- Introduced an **electronic litigation system** before the Economic Courts: filing, registration and
  service of cases by electronic means; service of the petition on the party's **chosen electronic
  domicile** (email, phone, fax or other technological means); **electronic service is deemed made
  from the date it is sent**, so procedural time limits begin immediately; the secretary must still
  prepare a **paper file** with copies of all documents — the system **runs alongside** the paper
  system; the case is announced on the Economic Court website **at least 5 working days before the
  hearing**; a judgment is treated as an **adversary judgment** if the defendant attended any
  hearing **or uploaded any memorandum or documents electronically**; if a proceeding done
  electronically cannot proceed on that track, the secretary circulates a certified copy to the
  **court bailiff** within at most a day.
- Regulation of the Court Electronic Registrar: certain categories **must** register (entities
  governed by the Economic Courts Law, public bodies and authorities, local and foreign companies,
  law firms); any individual may register of their own choice.
- Implemented by **Minister of Justice Decree No. 8548 of 2020**, creating a **unified electronic
  litigation registry**.
- Live platform: **https://elec.eecourts.gov.eg/user** — the entry point for lodging a new action,
  following a pending action, depositing statements of claim and attachments and defence
  memoranda, and **paying all court fees and costs by credit card or other electronic payment
  method**.
- Officially launched by the Ministry of Justice with MCIT (Microsoft Egypt / Link Development),
  covering **eight Economic Courts** at launch, with work cycles automated to the appeal stage and a
  secure document complex.

**B. Minister of Justice system No. 95 of 2018 — *نظام استعمال الوسائل الإلكترونية في الإجراءات
القضائية المدنية* (System for the Use of Electronic Means in Civil Judicial Procedures, 2018)**
- Issued under the second paragraph of Art. 5, the third paragraph of Art. 58 and the seventh
  paragraph of Art. 81 of the **Civil/Criminal Procedure Law No. 24 of 1988 and its amendments** —
  `note: the law number cited in the retrieved text is 24 of 1988, and this raises a flag that the
  instrument may be of Jordanian or comparative origin; VERIFY before citing for Egypt.`
- **11 articles**, in force from its publication, dated **2 September 2018**.
- Content: registration of actions and applications and payment of their fees electronically;
  exchange of statements and memoranda; **the parties must produce the originals of what was filed
  electronically at the first session following the electronic step, on pain of nullity of that
  step**; executive instruments may be filed electronically; **online payment of claim and
  application fees**; each lawyer is given an **electronic account on the Ministry's portal**; the
  approved means of electronic service are **email, SMS via mobile, the lawyer's electronic account,
  and any other means the Minister approves**; every stakeholder has the right to **challenge the
  validity** of an electronic service; an electronic service has **the same legal effects** as service
  under the law; the Ministry must archive electronic services for reference; courts verify addresses
  and service using the Ministry's electronic systems and other bodies' electronic systems linked to
  the Ministry's databases; the court may hear witnesses by **audio-visual electronic means** where
  the witness is outside the court's district or cannot attend.

**C. Minister of Justice electronic litigation procedural guide — Decision No. 8056**
- **الدليل الإجرائي لخدمة التقاضي الإلكتروني** — issued under paragraph (1) of **Art. 71 of the

### Judicial fees (الرسوم القضائية)

**Citation (statute):** Egypt's civil and criminal judicial fees statute is **Law No. 90 of 1944 on
Judicial Fees and Notarisation Fees in Civil Matters** — *القانون رقم 90 لسنة 1944 بالرسوم القضائية
ورسوم التوثيق في المواد المدنية* — **as amended, most substantially by Law No. 126 of 2009**
(*قانون رقم 126 لسنة 2009 بتعديل بعض أحكام قوانين الرسوم القضائية في المواد المدنية والجنائية
وأمام مجلس الدولة*).

> **Do not confuse Egypt with Kuwait.** "Law No. 3 of 1973", "Law No. 17 of 1973" and "Legislative
> Decree No. 3 of 1972" on judicial fees are **Bahrain / Kuwait** instruments, frequently surfaced by
> English-language searches. They are **not Egyptian law**.

**Status:** In force. Rates below are as amended by **Law No. 126 of 2009**, which is the version
retrieved; `confirm against the current consolidated text before quoting a figure`.

**Key provisions (as amended by Law 126 of 2009):**
- **Art. 1** — **proportional fee on claims of known value**, as a percentage banded: **2% up to EGP
  250**; **3% on the excess above EGP 250 up to EGP 2,000**; **4% above EGP 2,000 up to EGP 4,000**;
  **5% above EGP 4,000**. Fixed fees for claims of unknown value: **EGP 10** in actions before the
  judge of urgent matters; **EGP 5** in District Court actions; **EGP 15** in first-instance plenary
  actions; **EGP 50** in bankruptcy or pre-insolvency settlement actions (covering the whole
  proceeding including publication costs). Criminal fixed fees: **EGP 1.5** for a *mukhallafa*
  (petty offence), **EGP 3** on appeal; **EGP 5** for a *jina* (misdemeanour), **EGP 10** on appeal;
  **EGP 30** for a *jinaya* (felony); **EGP 20** on cassation (no new fee if the case is referred
  back to the court of first instance or Cassation decides the merits); **EGP 20** for
  rehabilitation. A case is classified as *mukhallafa*, *jina* or *jinaya* by the Public Prosecution's
  description unless the judgment provides otherwise, and **a jinaya for which the law prescribes
  jina punishment is treated as jina**.
- **Art. 3** — appeals carry the **same proportional bands** as art. 1, valued on the **amount in
  dispute on appeal**; fixed fees for appeals of unknown value: **EGP 10** on appeals to Courts of
  First Instance; **EGP 15** on appeals to Courts of First Instance from judgments of the summary
  courts; **EGP 30** on appeals to the **Courts of Appeal**.
- **Art. 4** — **a flat fee of EGP 75 on cassation**, and **EGP 40 on applications to stay execution
  before the Court of Cassation.**
- **Art. 18** — the civil fee rules apply to civil claims brought before the **criminal courts**,
  with the proviso that the advance fee may never be **less** than the criminal fee for that case;
  the civil claimant must pay the applicable fee **as soon as he claims** in the criminal case;
  a **25 piastre** per original-and-copy fee for notices other than service of the statement of
  claim and judgment, **rising by court level** (50 piastres for first-instance criminal matters;
  75 piastres for felony; EGP 1 for cassation and rehabilitation).
- **Art. 30** — **EGP 0.50 per sheet** for copies sought from registers and certificates, capped at
  **EGP 100 per action**; for **judicial papers**: **25 piastres per sheet at District Courts, 75
  piastres at Courts of First Instance, and EGP 1.50 at Courts of Appeal and the Court of Cassation.**
- **Art. 31** — search fees of **15 piastres per name per year** for extracting a copy, abstract or
  certificate from registers; search is charged **per person searched for**, and multiply if they are
  partners or heirs; visual inspection **EGP 0.50**.
- **Art. 34** — flat fees of **EGP 0.25 at District Courts, EGP 0.75 at First Instance and

### Legal aid (المساعدة القانونية)

**Position:** There is **no single stand-alone Egyptian statute on access to justice and legal aid**;
the Library of Congress report *Egypt: Access to the Justice System and to Legal Aid* expressly
records that gap. Access to justice and legal aid are constitutional rights, and they are scattered
across a range of instruments.

**Constitutional and statutory anchors:**
- **Constitution of 2014, Art. 98** — "The right of defence, whether in person or by proxy, is
  guaranteed… **The law shall provide all means by which those who are financially unable can resort
  to justice and defend their rights.**" The Administrative Court held under Art. 98 that a claimant is
  **not** entitled to be compensated for counsel fees and litigation expenses unless he **requested a
  fee exemption or a court-appointed lawyer from the trial court** — a practical trap for
  unrepresented foreign litigants.
- **Criminal Procedure Law No. 150 of 1950, Art. 124** — prohibits interrogation or cross-examination
  in the **absence of a lawyer**, except in flagrante delicto or where evidence may be lost; if the
  accused has no lawyer or cannot afford one, the **prosecutor must appoint one**.
- **Advocacy Law No. 17 of 1983, Arts. 93–94** — require Bar Association branches to form **legal aid
  committees** to give pro bono legal assistance and representation to those who cannot afford it at
  the investigation, pretrial and trial stages; the Board must delegate lawyers to defend citizens
  **free of charge**. **Art. 64** requires lawyers providing legal aid to those unable to bear the
  costs to serve with the same care and diligence as for a paying client, and provides that the
  lawyer **may not decline** to represent the indigent client unless permitted by the court and not
  before handing the case to another lawyer.
- **Law No. 1 of 2000 (Child Law's parent-support provisions)** — all claims for **alimony and
  maintenance are exempt from litigation fees and charges.**
- **Labour Law No. 14 of 2025, Art. 7** — worker, trainee and apprentice actions are exempt from
  judicial fees and expenses at all stages, with the court able to order self-execution without bail.
- **Institutional provision:** the Ministry of Justice established **Legal Aid Offices (LAOs)** in
  **family courts in 2008**, providing free legal assistance and dispute-settlement services;
  equivalent offices exist in **courts of first instance and labour courts** (for women victims of
  violence and labour litigants), and **child courts** use a specially composed panel and special
  trial proceedings. **Law clinics** are a more recent addition.
- **New under Labour Law 14/2025:** **Minister of Justice Decision No. 4693 of 2025** established
  **labour legal aid offices (مكاتب المساعدة القانونية العمالية)** at First Instance and labour courts,
  to assist litigants in **bringing their labour claims correctly**, with **all these office services
  rendered free of charge**. Each of the 38 labour courts created by Decision 4621/2025 hosts one.

### Advocacy Law No. 17 of 1983 and the Egyptian Bar Association (نقابة المحامين)

**Citation:** Law No. 17 of 1983 promulgating the Advocacy Law — *قانون رقم 17 لسنة 1983 بشأن إصدار
قانون المحاماة*. It **repealed** the Advocacy Law promulgated by **Law No. 61 of 1968** and Law No.
125 of 1981 (in part), and any provision in another law contrary to it.

**Status:** In force, **as last amended on 8 July 2020** per the Egyptian Bar Association's own
publication, with the substantive amendment package being **Law No. 147 of 2019**.

**Amendments (verified):** **Law No. 147 of 2019** amending certain provisions of the Advocacy Law
No. 17 of 1983 replaced Arts. 13 (paras. 1 and 3), 14 (para. 1), 24, 51 (¶1), 99 (¶¶1–2), 103, 128,
129, 131, 135 bis ¶3, 143, 144, 152, 156 bis, 187 and 196 (item 2), and added Arts. 13 bis and
50 bis and ¶2 of Arts. 51 and 62. It also **repealed Arts. 30, 136 ¶2 and 153**. Its key substantive
provisions:
- **Art. 13 as amended** — registration in the Bar is only permitted after obtaining the certificate
  under Art. 230 and **passing a national written examination** set by a committee formed by the
  **General Bar Council (مجلس النقابة العامة)**, comprising sufficiently experienced members and
  **involving senior lawyers, judges and law professors**, to be held **outside the Bar's
  headquarters twice a year** to ensure fairness and equal opportunity, per rules made by the General
  Council. **Application of the examination from January 2021.**
- **A Code of Professional Conduct (مدونة السلوك المهني للمحاماة)** is drawn up by the General Council,
  **approved by the Bar's General Assembly**, and **published in Al-Waqa'i Al-Misriyya**; it is
  **binding on members**, and breach gives rise to **professional responsibility**.
- **A new Book Seven: "The Academy of Advocacy and Legal Studies" (أكاديمية المحاماة والدراسات
  القانونية)** — established under the Bar, with legal personality and technical, administrative and
  financial independence, responsible for developing legal studies relevant to the profession,
  training lawyers, raising their skills, and granting **professional certificates**; administered by
  a **board of directors for four years**, chaired by the **Bar President** with twelve members
  elected by the Council (up to four Council members may be appointed). **Without prejudice to Art.
  13, first-time registration in the General Register requires completing the Academy's studies and
  obtaining the completion certificate, from January 2021.**
- A transitional article empowers the **incumbent General Bar Council** to implement all legislative
  assignments under the law and to review and clean the Bar's registers and election schedules within
  **six months** of the end of its term.

**Syndicate structure and professional duties (verified from the Arabic text):**
- **Arts. 68–69** — a lawyer may not accept a mandate **against a colleague**; and outside actions of
  summary urgency and civil claims, a lawyer may not accept a mandate in an action or complaint
  **against a colleague** without the **prior permission of the president of the Bar branch to which
  he belongs**; if permission is not issued within fifteen days in either case, the lawyer may take
  what steps he sees fit. **Art. 69** — a lawyer must refrain from mentioning the personal matters
  of the opposing party's client or accusing him of things touching his honour unless required for the
  defence of the client's interests.
- **Art. 70** — a lawyer may not make statements about a pending case he is defending, or publish
  anything affecting its course favourably or unfavourably to a client or opponent.
- **Art. 71** — a lawyer may **not use advertising, inducements, intermediaries or suggestion of any
  real or alleged influence or connection**, nor may he place on his office stationery any title other
  than his academic title and the statement of **the degree of the court before which he has the right
  of audience (المرافعة أمامها)**, nor any reference to an office previously held.
- **Art. 72** — subject to the rights of a lawyer's heirs, **no share of a lawyer's fees may be
  allocated to a non-lawyer**, even one employed in his office.
- **Art. 73** — a lawyer must appear before all courts **in the lawyer's robe (الرداء)** and must
  maintain a respectable appearance.
- **Art. 74** — subject to the rules for lawyers who are members of legal departments, a lawyer must
  have a proper office **within the district of the branch in which he is registered**, and **may not
  have more than one office anywhere in the Arab Republic of Egypt.**
- **Art. 75** — the lawyer supervises his office staff; and **may issue a power of attorney (توكيل) to
  one or more of his office employees** to inspect papers on his behalf, deliver and receive documents
  before any body, receive judgments, take execution steps, settle fees and deposits, and recover them.
  Such a power of attorney may be **attested by the competent branch**.
- **Art. 76** — a lawyer **may not sign statements of claim, appeals, other bailiff papers, or contracts
  submitted to the real property registration**, or appear and plead, in breach of the rules on the
  practice of advocacy; doing so renders the act **inadmissible or void**, without prejudice to the
  lawyer's liability and the liability of the person harmed.
- **Arts. 77–83** — duties to the client: represent within the limits of the mandate and per the
  client's instructions, **while retaining the freedom of defence** as to characterisation of the claim
  and presentation of legal arguments according to the sound principles of legal reasoning (Art. 77);
  inform the client of the stages of the action and of judgments, advise on appeals and deadlines
  (Art. 78); **preserve client confidences unless the client consents to disclosure** (Art. 79);
  **may not assist the opposing party, even for shared gain, in the same dispute or a connected
  dispute** if he has advised or represented them and then withdraws; and **generally may not act for
  conflicting interests — a prohibition extending to the lawyer's firm and to everyone working in the
  same office in any capacity** (Art. 80); may not purchase all or part of a contested right he is
  defending (Art. 81); fees are due per the retainer, and **fees may never be taken as an *in rem* share
  (حصة عينية) of the contested rights** — the Constitutional Court declared the third paragraph of
  Art. 82 unconstitutional on **6 December 1993**; where the dispute ends in settlement or arbitration,
  the agreed fees are due (Art. 83). **Arts. 84 and 85 were struck down by the Constitutional Court on
  5 June 1999** (Art. 85 entirely repealed, Art. 84 paragraphs 1, 2 and 3 repealed). **Art. 86** — the
  lawyer's right to claim fees from client or heirs is **extinguished if there is no written agreement
  on fees after five years from the client's death**, interrupted by a claim made by registered post.

### Beneficial ownership (المستفيدين الحقيقيين)

**There is no single Egyptian "beneficial ownership" law.** The regime is assembled from at least
three instruments:

**(a) AML Law No. 80 of 2002, Art. 6 of the Executive Regulations (2003, as amended)** —
Financial institutions must establish systems adequate for obtaining information on the **identity
and legal status of customers and their beneficial owners, whether natural or legal persons**, through
official or acceptable customary verification means, and **must register that information**. In the
2003 regulations the definition of **"beneficial owner"** is: *"Every natural or legal person having a
real interest in any of the activities mentioned in the previous item, even if the transaction is
conducted via another natural or legal person acting as a trustee, a proxy or under any other
capacity."* The 2023 amendments reinforced the digital/virtual-asset dimension of "الأموال أو الأصول"
but `did not, on the retrieved text, replace the beneficial-owner definition.`

**(b) Commercial Register — Ministerial Decree No. 41 of 2020** (Minister of Supply and Internal
Trade). A **2020 decree of the Minister of Supply and Internal Trade inserted provisions into the
executive regulations of the Commercial Register Law** requiring every registrant to **keep its own
register of beneficial owners (سجل المستفيدين الحقيقيين)**, recording those who actually own or
control the establishment, whether natural or legal persons; to **update it and notify the Commercial
Register immediately**; and to **retain it throughout the activity and for five years after cessation
or deletion**. The beneficial owner's **name, nationality and identity or passport number** are
recorded as a substantive item in the commercial register file. **Access is granted to judicial
law-enforcement officers on request; there is no public access channel.**
`Confidence: MEDIUM — sourced from InfoProff's business-register dataset citing the decree by number
and date. Verify the decree text and its placement in the Commercial Register executive regulations.`

**(c) Financial Regulatory Authority** — the FRA is the supervisory authority with the technical
instruments to compel identification of customers and beneficial owners by the non-banking financial
institutions it supervises, under AML Law Art. 9 and the Unit's measures.

**Status note:** Egypt does **not** have a public beneficial-ownership register; the register is a
**filer-maintained** one lodged with the Commercial Register. **An agent must not tell a client that
Egyptian beneficial ownership can be verified from a public database.**

- Applies to **all banks operating in Egypt, including their branches abroad and the branches of
  foreign banks operating in Egypt.**
- **Record keeping:** banks must retain **at least five years** records, documents and due-diligence
  records after **closure of accounts** or after **execution of transactions for customers without
  accounts**, unless the Unit or investigative authorities require longer — covering customer
  due-diligence records, transaction details, **unusual transaction reports**, **suspicious transaction
  records with associated notifications**, **decisions on examined transactions**, **analysis results**,

  decision was taken, and files on UN Security Council lists, terrorist entity lists and local
  terrorists (including disclosure records and freezing/unfreezing outcomes), must be retained.

  beneficiary, or any entity other than the competent authorities under the AML Law, any operation
  suspected to be proceeds of, or to involve, money laundering or terrorist financing.**
- **Prompt reporting:** the bank must **promptly notify the Egyptian Financial Intelligence Unit,
  through the Unit's electronic system or other systems, of all operations suspected of involving
  money laundering or terrorism financing**, as soon as the suspicion arises, and must have
  procedures and systems for it compatible with the Unit's systems, with periodic effectiveness
  assessment and updating. Internal notification to compliance staff must run through **clear and
  secure channels**.
- **Group / extraterritorial:** policies, procedures and risk assessments must be **approved by the
  board**, **documented, periodically reviewed and updated**, and **accessible to relevant staff**;
  the bank must ensure the existence of AML/CFT policies and procedures at foreign branches and
  subsidiaries. **Where the host country does not permit proper implementation of AML/CFT measures
  required by Egypt, the bank — including its branches abroad and subsidiaries — must apply
  additional measures suited to managing the AML/TF risks and must inform the CBE of those measures.**
- **Due diligence:** procedures for identifying the customer, and where the risk requires it, the
  **beneficial owner**, the purpose of the relationship and the nature of the customer's business
  activities, and **ongoing monitoring**.

### Central Bank and Banking Sector Law No. 194 of 2020 (قانون البنك المركزي والجهاز المصرفي)

> **VERIFIED: Law No. 194 of 2020 is in force and Law No. 88 of 2003 is repealed.** This is one of the
> few points in this file with no ambiguity.

**Citation:** Law No. 194 of 2020 promulgating the Central Bank and Banking Sector Law — *قانون رقم
194 لسنة 2020 بإصدار قانون البنك المركزي والجهاز المصرفي*. Promulgated at the Presidency of the
Republic on **15 September 2020**; **published in the Official Gazette, issue No. 37 bis (و), of
15 September 2020**; **in force 16 September 2020** (the day after publication). The electronic
courts portal hosts a text whose header reads "الجريدة الرسمية … بتاريخ 26 سبتمبر 2020", which
**conflicts** with the gazette record — treat the gazette record (issue 37 bis (و), 15 September 2020,
in force 16 September 2020) as the better-evidenced one, as it is corroborated by Shalakany, eastlaws
and the Technical Publication No. 33 of the Real Estate Registration and Notarisation Authority dated
7 December 2020, which all give the same dates.

**Status:** In force. It **repeals Law No. 88 of 2003** on the Central Bank, the banking sector and
money. It also repeals **Art. 10 of Law No. 11 of 1940** (sale and pledge of commercial
establishments); **Art. 1 of Law No. 19 of 1975** (rights and privileges of the Industrial Bank);
**Art. 94 of the Companies Law No. 159 of 1981**; and **Art. 21 of Law No. 95 of 1983** establishing
the Export Development Bank of Egypt. Subject to international agreements on the establishment of
certain banks in Egypt, **all conflicting provisions are repealed, including in the special laws
establishing those banks**, in respect of **minimum capital requirements, supervisory and regulatory
powers of the CBE, early intervention, resolution of troubled banks, account confidentiality, and
foreign exchange regulations.** A further promulgating article repeals **Art. 10 of Law No. 194 of
2020 itself** `UNVERIFIED — check.`
**Grace period:** compliance grace periods of **one to three years** (as applicable) from 16 September
2020 were granted (Matouk Bassiouny).

**Scale:** **241 articles**, compared to **135 articles** in the repealed Law No. 88 of 2003.

**Scope (promulgating article 1, verified):** the annexed law applies to the **Central Bank, the
banking sector, exchange companies (شركات الصرافة), money transfer companies (شركات تحويل الأموال),
credit information and credit rating companies, credit guarantee companies, payment system operators,
and payment service providers.**

**Key provisions (article numbers from the Andersen Egypt English translation and the Arabic text; the
Translation's internal numbering is used):**
- **Definitions** include **Banking Activities** (receiving deposits, granting credit, issuing and
  managing payment instruments, and other activities determined by the CBE); **Payment Systems**
  (arrangements comprising rules, procedures and technical infrastructure to facilitate the transfer,
  clearing or settlement of funds or financial instruments); **Payment System Operator** (a legal
  person **authorised by the Central Bank** to operate payment systems); **Payment Service Provider**
  (a legal person **authorised by the Central Bank** to provide one or more payment services);
  **Early Intervention** (CBE actions to address a supervised entity's deteriorating financial
  condition **before it becomes critically undercapitalised or insolvent**); **Bank Resolution** (CBE
  measures to restructure or resolve a bank in financial distress to maintain financial stability and
  **protect depositors**).
- **CBE powers and independence:** the CBE is an **independent regulatory body with public legal
  personality and technical, financial and administrative independence, reporting directly to the
  President**. Its powers include ensuring the **safety and efficiency of payment systems and
  services**; execution of **clearing, settlement, depository, registration and custody operations for
  government securities and financial instruments** under the **Central Securities Depository Law No.
  93 of 2000**; promotion of **financial inclusion** and expansion of access to banking services,
  including frameworks to reduce cash use; and the **establishment and management of payment systems
  and services**. It has **exclusive authority** over banks and entities conducting banking activities
  and payment services in Egypt, and issues **decisions, directives and circulars**.
- **Capital and supervision:** the CBE instructs banks on applicable **capital adequacy ratios and
  leverage calculation ratios**, supervises **all banking products** (as against the narrower "accounts
  opening guidelines, and provision of banking services" under the old law), and ensures the presence
  of a **code of ethics** and the sufficiency of **internal audit mechanisms**.
- **Financing to banks:** the Board of Directors may, in exceptional circumstances, approve financing
  to banks with low solvency or at risk of default, acting as the **agent of the government**
  (promulgating arts. 10–11 context). **Arts. 149–171** regulate the circumstances in which a bank may
  be deemed distressed, insolvent, or lacking liquidity, and the actions the CBE may take.
- **Resolution of distressed banks:** the CBE is the **competent authority for resolving the situations
  of distressed banks**, exercising its powers directly or through an **appointed resolution
  official**. The CBE prepares a **resolution plan** for distressed banks following a **viability
  assessment** that **excludes government support or emergency funding**; banks must remove obstacles
  to execution and respond to CBE data requests concerning **holding companies, affiliates or principal
  shareholders**. The resolution follows stated principles.
- **Early intervention (Eleventh):** the CBE may take any of the actions or penalties in **Art. 147**
  upon the occurrence of the listed circumstances; and may require the bank to implement one or more
  corrective measures.
- **Cancellation of bank licences** is a stated CBE power (Arabic article-list header:
  "إلغاء تراخيص البنوك").
- **Deposit Insurance Fund (صندوق تأمين الودائع) — see the dedicated block below.**
- **Customer complaints and dispute resolution:** the CBE has a **department receiving complaints from
  banks' clients**; a **Dispute Resolution Committee** inside the CBE can deliberate any dispute
  between a bank and any of its clients; and the law establishes an **arbitration and settlement centre**
  for disputes connected with the application of the Law.
- **Data privacy and security** are a new feature of the Law relative to the old one.
- **Repo transactions** are expressly permitted.
- **Bankruptcy:** Matouk Bassiouny records that the new Law **expressly excludes the application of the
  Bankruptcy Law No. 11 of 2018 to banks registered with the CBE.** `Confirm against the Arabic
  promulgating articles.`
- **Foreign exchange:** the CBE Board issues the rules governing the foreign-exchange market and the
  EGP rate is market-determined within the framework the CBE's Monetary Policy Committee sets
  (convertCCY, citing **Arts. 211–212 of Law 194/2020** at Official Gazette / NIB PDF pp. 106–107);
  **Art. 213** governs carriage of foreign currency and its declaration; **CBE Decision No. 2200 of
  2020** sets the maximum Egyptian banknotes entering or leaving Egypt (reported as **EGP 5,000**,
  Official Gazette / Al-Waqa'i Al-Misriyya issue 220, supplement of 30 September 2020, art. 1).
  `These article numbers and the EGP 5,000 figure are MEDIUM confidence — sourced from a secondary
  currency-rules compilation. Verify against the Arabic text and the CBE's own FX pages.`
- **National Payments Council (المجلس القومي للمدفوعات):** established by **Presidential Decree
  No. 89 of February 2017**, headed by the President of the Republic, with the objective of reducing
  the use of physical banknotes and promoting electronic payment channels to enhance financial
  inclusion and integrate more citizens into the formal financial system. Under Law 194/2020 the
  Council is established by Presidential decree; under the Payment Law it is **not** bound by
  government, public-sector or public-business-sector rules.

## Cross-cutting notes

**What an agent trained on a common-law or non-Egyptian civil-law system gets wrong**

1. **Egypt is not a precedent jurisdiction, and "stare decisis" language is a trap.** The Court of
   Cassation hears **points of law only**, and it publishes **"legal principles" (المبادئ القانونية)**
   through a Technical Office that **exercises editorial discretion not to publish a judgment it does
   not consider fit to be elevated into a principle.** The correct citation form is the **numbered
   annual volume** ("مجموعة أحكام محكمة النقض عن السنة …") plus the technical-office reference, and
   the correct characterisation is **persuasive**, not binding. An agent that says "the principle of
   the Court of Cassation is that…" without the volume reference will be cited back.

2. **"Labour Law 12 of 2003" is a dead citation.** An agent trained before September 2025 will produce
   it reflexively. The operative statute is **Law No. 14 of 2025**, and the transition is messy:
   pending cases were **transferred by the courts themselves, sua sponte and free of fees**, to
   specialised labour courts; **appeals already filed before 1 October 2025 stay with the Court of
   Cassation and the Courts of Appeal**; and cases already judged or reserved for judgment stay where
   they are. There is no single "date of application" test an agent can apply mechanically.

3. **Dismissal in Egypt is not an employer decision — it is a court decision.** Under **Art. 148 of
   Law 14/2025** (and its predecessor Art. 68–69 of Law 12/2003), **only the competent labour court has
   jurisdiction to impose dismissal.** A dismissal that does not run through that court is void. This
   is the structural opposite of at-will employment, and it is the single most important thing an
   English-trained agent will get wrong. Equally: an employer **cannot** unilaterally "make redundant" —
   severance for a legitimate-reason termination and the **two months' wages per year of service** for
   an illegitimate one are different statutory numbers.

4. **"No penalty may be imposed" in Egypt is a *closed-list* problem, not a presumption problem.** The
   brief's framing ("irrebuttable presumption cases") does not map onto the statute. The real rules are:
   a **closed list of serious misconduct** (Art. 148) for dismissal; an **exhaustive list of protected
   reasons** that cannot justify termination (Art. 165: union activity, having complained of the
   employer, wage garnishment, exercising leave rights, pregnancy, sex, colour, religion, political
   opinion); and **mandatory procedural preconditions** without which no disciplinary sanction at all is
   lawful (Art. 141: written notification, hearing and defence, written record, investigation within 7
   days and completed within 3 months, reasoned decision). An agent that reaches for a doctrinal label
   instead of citing the article will hallucinate.

5. **"Irrebuttable presumption" is not an Egyptian statutory idiom here.** Where the brief asks for it,
   the closest verified Egyptian concepts are: (a) the **rebuttable presumption that a contract is
   indefinite** if it is unwritten, silent as to duration, or continued after a fixed term without a
   written agreement (Law 14/2025 Art. 88); and (b) the **rebuttable presumption of resignation by
   absence** after 20 non-consecutive or 10 consecutive days with prior registered warning
   (Art. 166). Both are rebuttable. **Do not manufacture an irrebuttable presumption.**

6. **Labour-court procedure is heavily front-loaded by mandatory conciliation, and the outcome is
   atypical.** Under Law 14/2025: a settlement request within **10 days**, a committee that must finish
   within **21 days**, a settlement that must be **approved by the summary judge** to be enforceable
   (Art. 149); a hearing set **within 20 days** of referral (Art. 150); and for **dismissal disputes, an
   urgent decision within 3 months of the first hearing, capping the employer's wage payment at six
   months' wages and making that ruling FINAL** — i.e. not appealable in the ordinary way. An agent
   used to "file, await judgment, appeal" will mis-plan entirely.

7. **The Egyptian appeal time limits in the brief's terms were not verified.** One retrieved
   translation says the general appeal period is **forty days** unless the law provides otherwise. The
   commonly cited **15-day and 60-day** periods for summary appeals and cassation were **not confirmed
   from the current Arabic text** and must not be stated without primary-source verification. Egyptian
   law also has **unusual finality thresholds by value** — EGP 200,000 and EGP 30,000 for District Court
   judgments — which have **nothing** to do with common-law small-claims rules and which determine
   whether an appeal exists at all.

8. **"Judicial fees" in Egypt is two different things.** The **statutory tariff** is **Law No. 90 of
   1944** (as amended by Law No. 126 of 2009). Separately, since **February–March 2025** the **Presidents
   of the Courts of Appeal** have imposed **per-court "consideration" (مقابل) for mechanised services
   and IT upgrades**, which the Bar Association is contesting as an unconstitutional charge on access
   to justice and which was before the **Administrative Court** in **Case No. 51843 of Judicial Year 79**
   (urgent motion rejected 31 May 2025; merits set for 9 September 2025). **An agent that quotes a Cairo
   Court of Appeal figure as a statutory fee is wrong; an agent that omits the existence of the
   charges is also wrong.** Note the jurisdictional trap: the court fees numbers "3 of 1972", "3 of
   1973" and "17 of 1973" that dominate English-language and Arabic search results are **Bahraini and
   Kuwaiti**, not Egyptian.

9. **Egypt's labour courts are new and only just operating.** **38 specialised labour courts** were
   sited by **Minister of Justice Decision No. 4621 of 2025**, effective **1 October 2025**, each with
   its own registry, its own **enforcement department**, and a **labour legal aid office** created by
   **Decision No. 4693 of 2025**. Decision **5614 of 2025** later added venues inside District Courts
   in some districts. **Specialised Cassation screening circuits can refuse a labour cassation on
   Cassation-admissibility grounds (Art. 263 of Law 13/1968) and that refusal is unappealable by any
   route** — a hard procedural gate with no equivalent in common law.

10. **Child labour has two floors and they do not match.** The Labour Law now sets **15** as the
    employment floor and **14** as the training floor (Law 14/2025 Arts. 61–62). The **Child Law
    Art. 64, as amended by Law 126 of 2008**, also says **15** for work and **13** for training. But the
    **repealed** Labour Law 12/2003 **Art. 98** said **14**, and **ILO source documents and commentary
    still routinely quote 14**. An agent quoting 14 is citing a repealed article.

11. **Women's protections in Egypt are far broader than maternity leave alone**, and the numbers are
    not negotiable: four months' maternity leave (≥45 days post-delivery, max 3 times, paid, with the
    employer's cost **reduced by Art. 77 of Law 148/2019**), a **one-hour daily reduction from the sixth
    month of pregnancy plus a complete overtime ban until six months post-partum**, **two paid
    30-minute breastfeeding breaks for two years**, **unpaid child-care leave of up to two years in
    establishments of 50+ workers**, a **right to resign on marriage/pregnancy/childbirth on three
    months' written notice**, a **mandatory nursery at 100+ female workers at one site**, and a
    **prohibition on dismissal during maternity leave and after return absent proof of a legitimate
    reason**. An agent trained on a European maternity model will understate this badly.

12. **The Egyptian social insurance system is not "the old law for big employers".** Law 148/2019 is a
    **single-basket** statute replacing the old **79/1975 (workers)**, **108/1976 (employers)**
    **50/1978 (Egyptians abroad)** fragmentation, with **four categories of insured person** (employees;
    employers/self-employed; **Egyptians abroad — insurance is OPTIONAL**; and **irregular/unorganised
    labour**), four branches (old-age/disability/death; work injury; sickness; unemployment) and
    **NOSI** as the single administrator. Transition is by **category and progressive inclusion
    decisions**, not by employer headcount. **A claim that Law 79/1975 still applies "below X employees"
    is `UNVERIFIED` and probably false.** Contributions for private-sector employees are commonly
    described as **18.75% employer / 11% employee** in aggregate (12%/9% base plus a 1%/1% end-of-service
    bonus), **rising 1% every seven years, split equally, capped at 26%** — but the layer-by-layer
    breakdown must be re-read from the Arabic Art. 19 before being asserted.

13. **A social insurance subtlety an agent will miss: continuity across categories is presumed.** The
    Regulations provide that it is **presumed the insured person's employment or activity has not
    ceased** when they move between the Article 2 categories, and entitlements are computed on **total
    contribution periods as a single unit**. Also, an employer who fails to implement the committee's
    alternative-employment decision under **Art. 21(2)** **must keep paying wages** until the insured
    person obtains alternative employment, and **may not terminate the employment** during the review
    period (the contract is deemed extended). This is a distinctive, easily-missed protection.

14. **AML: the predicate-offence list and the "gatekeeper" offence matter more than the headline
    penalty.** Egypt's **Art. 18** criminalises **providing any service, opening an account for, or
    supplying any means of disposing of or transferring funds to an unlicensed financial institution or
    person** — the classic gatekeeper offence. **Art. 14** carries **up to seven years' imprisonment
    and a fine of twice the value**, with **mandatory confiscation** and an **express exclusion from
    ¶2 of Art. 32 of the Penal Code**. An agent trained on the EU's four-tier predicate model will
    mis-map Egypt's list, which is set out in **Art. 2** and includes **narcotics trafficking,
    hijacking, terrorism under Art. 86 of the Penal Code, unlicensed import/export and counterfeiting**
    among others.

15. **Egypt's FIU governance changed structurally in 2022 and that is where the reporting lines moved.**
    **Law No. 154 of 2022** replaced Art. 3 and gave the FIU a **Board of Guardians chaired by a
    judicial expert with 15+ years in the Court of Cassation or a Court of Appeal**, sitting alongside
    the Public Prosecution, the CBE, the FRA, the Cabinet Advisors Bureau, the **Egyptian Banks
    Union**, an economics expert, and the Unit's **Executive Director** (a new statutory office under
    Art. 3 bis). It also created **targeted financial sanctions under new Art. 9 bis/1** (freezing and
    refusal of services, **including on any natural or legal person who may hold related funds or
    assets**), enforced by a **new Art. 15 bis** penalty of up to one year or EGP 100,000–300,000, and
    imposed **parallel financial investigations (Art. 17 bis/1)** on law-enforcement and investigative
    authorities. **The old Presidential Decree 164 of 2002 on the Unit was repealed.** Note the
    cross-sector reach of Art. 9 bis/1 — it reaches **owners of non-financial businesses**, not only
    financial institutions.

16. **Egypt has no public beneficial-ownership register.** The register is **filer-maintained** and
    lodged with the **Commercial Register** (Ministerial Decree No. 41 of 2020), retained five years
    after deletion, with **access confined to judicial law-enforcement officers on request**. An agent
    trained on the Corporate Transparency Act or the UAE register will invent a public search that
    does not exist. Note also that the AML Regulations **expressly refuse to accept professional
    secrecy (سرية المهنة) asserted by lawyers, accountants and financial intermediaries as a ground
    for withholding identification data** — a direct blow to the confidentiality expectation that a
    common-law-trained agent would assume.

17. **Beneficial-ownership CDD thresholds are instrument-type-dependent, not universal.** Identification
    is mandatory **on account opening, on the first banking transaction, or on commencing any dealing**,
    and must be **refreshed whenever suspicion arises at any stage**; for **one-off transactions** it
    triggers only **above a limit set by the supervisory authorities in coordination with the FIU for
    each type of financial institution**. In the AML Executive Regulations, the look-through
    threshold for shareholders is stated as **partners or shareholders owning more than 10% of the
    company's capital** (from the 2003 text as amended) — **verify the current threshold against the
    2023 amendments.**

18. **FATF status is the opposite of the assumption in the brief.** Egypt is **not grey-listed and has
    never been listed by FATF**; it is in **MENAFATF enhanced follow-up** from a **June 2021** MER,
    with 1st–4th EFURs adopted at the **34th, 36th, 38th and 40th Plenaries (May 2022, 2023, 2024,
    2025)**. As at the 4th EFUR (May 2025) Egypt stood at **11 Compliant, 26 Largely Compliant, 3
    Partially Compliant**, having just moved **R.3 from Partially Compliant to Largely Compliant**,
    following the May 2024 upgrades of **R.14 and R.23 to Compliant** and **R.15 and R.22 to Largely
    Compliant**. An agent asked "is Egypt on the grey list" should answer **no**, and should explain
    that the binding instrument here is **MENAFATF**, not FATF proper.

19. **"Banking Law 194/2020 vs 88/2003" — this one is clean.** **Law No. 194 of 2020 is in force from
    16 September 2020** (gazette issue 37 bis (و) of 15 September 2020) and **repealed Law No. 88 of
    2003 entirely**. The trap is **staleness in the sources**: GAFI, FRA and many English-language
    summaries still serve Law 88/2003 as the operative banking statute, and AML Law Art. 12 still
    cross-refers to **Art. 116 of Law 88/2003**. Also note **Law No. 194 of 2020 expressly excludes
    CBE-registered banks from the Bankruptcy Law No. 11 of 2018** — an insolvency point with real
    consequences that no common-law-trained agent would anticipate.

20. **Regulator routing in Egypt is a two-agency split, and agents routinely get it wrong.** **Banks,
    exchange companies, money transfer companies, credit information and credit rating companies,
    credit guarantee companies, payment system operators and payment service providers → CBE** under
    Law 194/2020. **Capital markets, future exchanges, insurance, mortgage finance, financial leasing,
    factoring, securitisation, microfinance, movable guarantees, and non-banking fintech → FRA** under
    Law 10 of 2009, Law 95 of 1992, Law 10 of 1981, Law 148 of 2001, Law 176 of 2018, Law 141 of 2014,
    Law 115 of 2015 and **Law 5 of 2022**. **Fintech straddles both**: Law 5/2022 applies to
    non-banking financial fintech "without prejudice to" Law 194/2020, and makes the FRA the **sole**
    competent authority for that statute.

21. **Egyptian lawyers' professional conduct rules are statutory and detailed, and the Bar has real
    institutional teeth.** Law 17/1983 as amended by **Law 147 of 2019** introduced a **national
    qualifying examination** (effective January 2021) and a **Code of Professional Conduct** approved by
    the Bar's General Assembly and **published in Al-Waqa'i Al-Misriyya, binding on members with
    professional liability for breach** — neither of which exists in most common-law jurisdictions. It
    also created the **Academy of Advocacy and Legal Studies**, whose completion certificate is now a
    precondition to **first-time** registration. Note the strong conflict rules: **one office only in
    the whole of Egypt (Art. 74)**; **no share of fees may go to a non-lawyer, even a firm employee
    (Art. 72)**; **no representation of a colleague without branch-president permission (Arts. 68)**;
    and **fees may never be an *in rem* share of the contested rights** — Art. 82 ¶3, struck down by
    the **Constitutional Court on 6 December 1993**. Articles 84 and 85 were struck down on **5 June
    1999**. An agent that says "Egyptian lawyers are subject to a professional conduct code" without
    the statutory cite, or that assumes the Bar's 2024–2025 "new Advocacy Law" is enacted, will be
    wrong on the second count.

22. **Two-minute check before citing anything in this file:** Egypt re-publishes its laws under
    colliding numbers. Law **80 of 2002** is BOTH the AML law AND a companies law. Law **17 of 1983**
    is the Egyptian Advocacy Law AND (in Kuwait) the judicial fees law. Law **90 of 1944** is the
    Egyptian judicial fees law. Law **3 of 1972** is Bahraini judicial fees. **Always confirm the
    jurisdiction alongside the number**, and always confirm that the instrument has not been repealed —
    Egypt repealed both of its labour law and its banking law inside the last three years.
